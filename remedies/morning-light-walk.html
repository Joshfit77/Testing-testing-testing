Morning Light Walk

Bright morning light tells your internal clock that the day has started, and gentle movement gets blood flowing. Together they're a simple energy habit.

What it may help with

  • Morning grogginess
  • Low energy during the day
  • A body clock thrown off by late nights

Ingredients

  • 10–20 minutes outdoors, within 1–2 hours of waking
  • Comfortable shoes

How to make it

  1. Within an hour or two of waking, step outside — even on a cloudy day.
  2. Walk at an easy, comfortable pace for 10–20 minutes.
  3. Don't wear sunglasses unless you need them, and never look directly at the sun.
  4. Drink a glass of water when you get back.

Suggested amount

10–20 minutes.

How often

Daily, especially on workdays.

Evidence level: Some research

Morning light exposure has been studied for supporting the body clock and sleep timing, and regular walking has been studied for reducing fatigue.

Safety warnings

  • Use sunscreen and protect your eyes in strong sun.
  • In very hot or cold weather, keep it short and dress for the conditions.
  • If you have heart or lung disease, ask your doctor what activity level is right for you.

When to seek medical care instead

  • Fatigue that doesn't improve after a few weeks of good habits
  • Chest pain, dizziness or unusual shortness of breath when walking — stop and seek care
