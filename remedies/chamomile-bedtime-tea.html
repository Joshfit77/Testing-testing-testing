Chamomile Bedtime Tea

A warm cup of chamomile is a gentle signal to your body that the day is done. Chamomile's apigenin acts mildly on the brain's calming GABA receptors.

What it may help with

  • Winding down before bed
  • Mild trouble falling asleep
  • A restless, busy mind at night

Ingredients

  • 1–2 tsp (2–3 g) dried chamomile flowers, or 1–2 tea bags
  • 1 cup (240 ml) just-boiled water
  • 1 tsp (optional) honey

How to make it

  1. Put the chamomile in a mug or infuser.
  2. Pour the just-boiled water over it and cover.
  3. Steep for 5–10 minutes (longer makes it stronger).
  4. Strain, add honey if you like, and sip 30–60 minutes before bed.

Suggested amount

1 cup.

How often

Once nightly. It's fine to drink every night.

Evidence level: Some research

Small clinical trials found chamomile improved sleep quality in older adults and eased mild anxiety. Results for insomnia are mixed.

Safety warnings

  • Avoid if you're allergic to ragweed, daisies or marigolds.
  • May add to drowsiness from sleep medicines, sedatives or alcohol.
  • Large amounts may affect warfarin — tell your doctor if you drink it daily.
  • If pregnant, ask your provider about regular use.

When to seek medical care instead

  • Trouble sleeping most nights for more than a month
  • Loud snoring, gasping or stopping breathing at night (possible sleep apnea)
  • Sleep problems with low mood, anxiety or thoughts of self-harm — call or text 988 in the U.S.
