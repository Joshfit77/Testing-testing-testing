Warm Salt Water Gargle

Gargling warm salt water is one of the simplest, most widely recommended sore-throat remedies. Salt draws fluid from swollen tissue and may help loosen mucus.

What it may help with

  • Scratchy or sore throat
  • Throat irritation from a cold or postnasal drip

Ingredients

  • ½ tsp (about 3 g) table salt
  • 1 cup (240 ml) warm water (comfortably warm, not hot)

How to make it

  1. Stir the salt into the warm water until dissolved.
  2. Take a sip, tilt your head back and gargle for about 30 seconds.
  3. Spit it out — don't swallow.
  4. Repeat until the cup is finished.

Suggested amount

1 cup, gargled in sips.

How often

Every 3–4 hours as needed.

Evidence level: Some research

Widely recommended by doctors for comfort. A Japanese study found regular gargling was linked with fewer upper respiratory infections.

Safety warnings

  • Don't swallow the salt water.
  • Not for young children who can't gargle safely (usually under about 6).
  • If you're on a strict low-sodium diet, ask your doctor.

When to seek medical care instead

  • Trouble breathing or swallowing, or drooling — seek care right away
  • Fever over 101°F (38.3°C), or white patches on the tonsils with fever (possible strep)
  • A sore throat lasting more than a week
  • A rash, or a very swollen neck or jaw
