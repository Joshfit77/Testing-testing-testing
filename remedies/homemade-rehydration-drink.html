Homemade Rehydration Drink

The right balance of water, sugar and salt helps the gut absorb water faster than plain water alone. This is the World Health Organization's simple home recipe.

What it may help with

  • Replacing fluids and salts after diarrhea or vomiting
  • Mild dehydration in adults

Ingredients

  • 1 liter (about 4¼ cups) clean drinking water
  • 6 level teaspoons sugar
  • ½ level teaspoon salt

How to make it

  1. Measure carefully with level measuring spoons — the amounts matter.
  2. Stir the sugar and salt into the water until completely dissolved.
  3. Sip small amounts often, every few minutes, rather than drinking a lot at once.
  4. Make a fresh batch each day and throw away anything left after 24 hours.

Suggested amount

Small sips often; adults can drink up to about 2–3 liters a day while unwell.

How often

Throughout the day until urine is pale yellow again.

Evidence level: Well researched

Oral rehydration solution is a WHO-recommended treatment for dehydration from diarrhea and has saved millions of lives.

Safety warnings

  • Measure exactly — too much salt can be dangerous.
  • For babies and young children, use a store-bought oral rehydration solution and call your pediatrician.
  • If you have kidney disease, heart failure or high blood pressure, ask your doctor first.

When to seek medical care instead

  • No urine for 8 hours, or very dark urine
  • Dizziness, fainting, confusion or a racing heart
  • Blood or black color in the stool, or vomiting blood
  • Diarrhea lasting more than 2 days in adults (sooner for children)
  • A baby with fewer wet diapers, no tears, or a sunken soft spot — call your pediatrician right away
