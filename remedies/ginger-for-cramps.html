Ginger for Period Cramps

Ginger calms the body's production of prostaglandins — the same messengers that cause period cramps.

What it may help with

  • Period cramps
  • Nausea that comes with your period

Ingredients

  • 1-inch (2.5 cm) piece fresh ginger, sliced — or capsules of powdered ginger (250 mg each)
  • 1½ cups (360 ml) water (for tea)
  • 1 tsp (optional) honey

How to make it

  1. Simmer the sliced ginger in water for 10 minutes, then strain.
  2. Sip a cup up to 3 times a day during the first days of your period.
  3. Or, as in studies, take 250 mg of ginger powder 4 times a day for the first 3 days of your period.

Suggested amount

1 cup of tea, or 250 mg capsules as described (about 1 g a day).

How often

Up to 3–4 times a day, for the first 2–3 days of your period.

Evidence level: Some research

Several small trials found ginger eased period pain, and some found effects similar to ibuprofen. The studies are small.

Safety warnings

  • May increase bleeding with blood thinners; ask your doctor if your periods are very heavy.
  • Ask your doctor first if you have gallstones.
  • Stop if you might be pregnant and check with your provider.

When to seek medical care instead

  • Pain that keeps you from daily activities or is getting worse each month
  • Very heavy bleeding (soaking a pad or tampon every hour), or bleeding between periods
  • Pain with fever or unusual discharge
  • Severe pain and you might be pregnant — seek care right away
