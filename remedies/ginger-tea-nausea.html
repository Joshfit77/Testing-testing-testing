Fresh Ginger Tea for Nausea

Ginger is one of the best-studied natural options for nausea. Its gingerols act on the stomach and on nausea signals in the body.

What it may help with

  • Mild nausea and queasiness
  • Motion sickness
  • An unsettled stomach

Ingredients

  • 1-inch (2.5 cm) piece fresh ginger, thinly sliced (about 5–10 g)
  • 1½ cups (360 ml) water
  • 1 tsp (optional) honey
  • 1 squeeze (optional) lemon juice

How to make it

  1. Add the sliced ginger and water to a small pot.
  2. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer for 10 minutes.
  3. Strain into a mug.
  4. Stir in honey and lemon if you like, and sip slowly.

Suggested amount

1 cup at a time. Keep total ginger to about 4 g of dried ginger (or roughly 1 oz / 30 g fresh) a day or less; in pregnancy, about 1 g dried a day is the amount usually studied.

How often

Up to 3 cups a day.

Evidence level: Well researched

Many clinical trials have studied ginger for nausea, including pregnancy nausea and motion sickness, with modest benefits for many people.

Safety warnings

  • Larger amounts may increase bleeding risk with blood thinners.
  • Ask your doctor first if you have gallstones.
  • In pregnancy, ginger is commonly used for morning sickness in modest amounts — check with your provider first.
  • Never give honey to babies under 1 year.

When to seek medical care instead

  • You can't keep fluids down for more than 24 hours (12 hours for a child)
  • Signs of dehydration: very little or dark urine, dizziness, dry mouth
  • Vomit that is bloody or looks like coffee grounds
  • Severe belly pain, a stiff neck, or vomiting after a head injury
  • Severe vomiting in pregnancy
