Saline Nasal Rinse

Rinsing the nose with salt water washes out mucus and irritants. Doctors commonly recommend it for colds, sinus symptoms and allergies — but the water must be safe.

What it may help with

  • A blocked or stuffy nose
  • Sinus pressure
  • Allergy symptoms and postnasal drip

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (240 ml) distilled or sterile water, or tap water boiled for 1 minute and cooled to lukewarm
  • ¼–½ tsp non-iodized salt (canning or pickling salt)
  • 1 pinch baking soda
  • A clean neti pot or squeeze bottle

How to make it

  1. Mix the salt and baking soda into the lukewarm safe water until dissolved.
  2. Lean over a sink and tilt your head to one side.
  3. Gently pour or squeeze half the solution into your upper nostril so it drains out the lower one. Breathe through your mouth.
  4. Gently blow your nose, then repeat on the other side.
  5. Wash the device with safe water and let it air-dry.

Suggested amount

About 1 cup, split between both nostrils.

How often

1–2 times a day.

Evidence level: Well researched

Saline rinses have been studied for sinus and nasal symptoms and are widely recommended by doctors.

Safety warnings

  • NEVER use untreated tap water — rare but deadly infections have occurred. Use distilled, sterile, or boiled-and-cooled water.
  • Don't use if your ears are blocked or you have an ear infection.
  • Ask your pediatrician before using with children.

When to seek medical care instead

  • Symptoms lasting more than 10 days
  • Fever over 102°F (38.9°C), or severe headache or facial pain
  • Swelling or redness around the eyes
  • A stiff neck or confusion
