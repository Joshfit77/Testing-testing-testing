Tofu

Beans & lentils

What it is

Tofu is made by curdling soy milk and pressing it into blocks. It's a complete plant protein, and tofu made with calcium sulfate is also a good source of calcium.

Key nutrients

  • About 10–20 g protein per ½ cup (126 g), depending on firmness
  • Calcium (if calcium-set)
  • Iron
  • Isoflavones
  • Magnesium

Potential benefits

Plant protein (Well researched)

Soy is one of the few plant foods with complete protein, making tofu a helpful swap for some meat.

Heart health (Some research)

Replacing some animal protein with soy has been studied for modestly lowering LDL cholesterol.

Menopause comfort (Some research)

Soy isoflavones are plant compounds that have been studied for easing hot flashes in some women.

Best ways to eat it

  • Press, cube and bake or air-fry until crisp.
  • Scramble with turmeric and vegetables.
  • Add to stir-fries and soups.
  • Blend silken tofu into smoothies or creamy dressings.

Typical serving

½ cup (about 126 g) (about 126 g).

When to eat it

Lunch or dinner, or scrambled at breakfast.

Who may benefit

  • Vegetarians and vegans
  • People cutting back on red meat
  • Women in menopause
  • Anyone supporting heart health

Possible downsides

  • Some people find soy causes bloating.
  • Flavored or fried tofu products can be high in sodium and oil.

Allergies & interactions

Soy is a major food allergen. Soy can reduce absorption of thyroid medicine (levothyroxine) — take your medicine about 4 hours apart from soy foods.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Tofu is a good protein during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Food amounts of soy are considered fine; concentrated isoflavone supplements are not recommended.
