Chia Seeds

Nuts & seeds

What it is

Chia seeds are tiny seeds that soak up liquid and form a gel. They're one of the richest plant sources of fiber and omega-3 ALA.

Key nutrients

  • About 10 g fiber per 1 oz (2 tbsp)
  • Omega-3 ALA
  • Calcium
  • Magnesium
  • 4–5 g protein

Potential benefits

Regularity (Some research)

Chia's soluble fiber forms a gel that supports regular, comfortable bowel movements.

Fullness and blood sugar (Some research)

The gel slows digestion and has been studied for reducing blood-sugar spikes after meals.

Heart health (Some research)

Provides plant omega-3s and fiber that support heart health.

Best ways to eat it

  • Make chia pudding: 2 tbsp chia + ½ cup milk, chill overnight.
  • Stir into oatmeal or yogurt.
  • Blend into smoothies.
  • Use "chia eggs" (1 tbsp chia + 3 tbsp water) in baking.

Typical serving

1–2 tablespoons (about 15 g).

When to eat it

Breakfast or a snack. Drink plenty of water with chia.

Who may benefit

  • People wanting more fiber
  • People managing blood sugar
  • Vegetarians wanting plant omega-3s

Possible downsides

  • Always soak or mix with liquid — dry chia seeds can swell in the throat.
  • Increase slowly to avoid bloating.

Allergies & interactions

Allergy is rare. May slightly lower blood pressure and blood sugar — use normal food amounts if you take medicines for these.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Generally fine in food amounts during pregnancy and helpful for constipation.
