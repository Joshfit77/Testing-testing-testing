Chickpeas

Beans & lentils

What it is

Chickpeas (garbanzo beans) are nutty, versatile legumes and the base of hummus. They provide plant protein, fiber and minerals.

Key nutrients

  • 7 g protein per ½ cup cooked (82 g)
  • 6 g fiber
  • Folate
  • Iron
  • Manganese

Potential benefits

Fullness and blood sugar (Well researched)

Chickpeas' fiber and protein slow digestion and have been studied for helping people feel full and keep blood sugar steadier.

Heart health (Some research)

As part of a legume-rich diet, chickpeas may help lower LDL cholesterol.

Digestive health (Some research)

Their fiber supports regularity and feeds beneficial gut bacteria.

Best ways to eat it

  • Blend into hummus with tahini, lemon and garlic.
  • Roast with olive oil and spices for a crunchy snack.
  • Add to salads, soups and curries.
  • Mash with Greek yogurt for a sandwich filling.

Typical serving

½ cup cooked (about 82 g).

When to eat it

Lunch, dinner or as a snack with vegetables.

Who may benefit

  • People managing blood sugar or weight
  • Vegetarians
  • Anyone wanting more fiber

Possible downsides

  • May cause gas at first — rinse canned chickpeas and increase slowly.
  • Store-bought hummus can be salty.

Allergies & interactions

Chickpea allergy is uncommon; some people allergic to peas or lentils also react to chickpeas.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

A great source of folate, fiber and protein during pregnancy.
