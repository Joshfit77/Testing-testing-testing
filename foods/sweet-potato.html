Sweet Potato

Vegetables

What it is

Sweet potatoes are orange-fleshed root vegetables loaded with beta-carotene, fiber and potassium.

Key nutrients

  • Beta-carotene (more than a day's vitamin A per medium potato)
  • Fiber
  • Potassium
  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin B6

Potential benefits

Skin and eye health (Some research)

Beta-carotene becomes vitamin A, which supports healthy skin, vision and immunity.

Steady energy (Some research)

Eaten with the skin, sweet potatoes provide fiber-rich carbohydrates for longer-lasting energy.

Blood pressure (Some research)

A good source of potassium, which helps balance sodium.

Best ways to eat it

  • Bake whole and top with Greek yogurt and cinnamon.
  • Roast cubes with olive oil and rosemary.
  • Mash as a side.
  • Add to chili or curry.

Typical serving

1 medium (about 5 inches) (about 114 g).

When to eat it

Lunch or dinner, or before exercise for energy.

Who may benefit

  • Active people
  • Anyone wanting more vitamin A and fiber

Possible downsides

  • Candied or fried sweet potatoes add sugar and fat.

Allergies & interactions

Allergy is rare. High in potassium — people on a kidney diet may need to limit it.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

A great source of vitamin A from food during pregnancy (food beta-carotene is safe; high-dose vitamin A supplements are not).
