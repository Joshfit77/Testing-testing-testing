Almonds

Nuts & seeds

What it is

Almonds are nutrient-dense tree nuts rich in vitamin E, magnesium, fiber and heart-healthy fats.

Key nutrients

  • 6 g protein per 1 oz (about 23 almonds)
  • Vitamin E (about half a day's needs)
  • Magnesium
  • Fiber
  • Monounsaturated fat

Potential benefits

Heart health (Well researched)

Eating nuts regularly is linked with lower heart disease risk, and almond studies show modest drops in LDL cholesterol.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Eating almonds with a carbohydrate food has been studied for blunting the blood-sugar rise.

Skin protection (Some research)

Vitamin E is an antioxidant that helps protect skin cells.

Best ways to eat it

  • Snack on a small handful.
  • Add slivered almonds to oatmeal, salads and yogurt.
  • Spread almond butter on apple slices.
  • Use almond flour in baking.

Typical serving

1 oz (a small handful) (about 28 g).

When to eat it

As a snack between meals, or a small handful in the evening for magnesium.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone supporting heart health
  • People managing blood sugar
  • Anyone wanting a satisfying snack

Possible downsides

  • Calorie-dense — a small handful is a serving.
  • Choose unsalted or lightly salted.

Allergies & interactions

Tree nuts are a major allergen and can cause severe reactions. Whole nuts are a choking hazard for children under 4.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

A healthy snack during pregnancy unless you are allergic.
