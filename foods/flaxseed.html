Ground Flaxseed

Nuts & seeds

What it is

Flaxseeds are small seeds rich in fiber, omega-3 ALA and lignans. Grind them first — whole seeds often pass through undigested.

Key nutrients

  • Omega-3 ALA (about 1.6 g per tbsp)
  • Fiber (about 2 g per tbsp)
  • Lignans
  • Magnesium

Potential benefits

Regularity (Some research)

Ground flaxseed has been studied for relieving constipation thanks to its fiber.

Heart and blood pressure (Some research)

Trials found daily ground flaxseed modestly lowered blood pressure and LDL cholesterol in some people.

Menopause (Some research)

Flax lignans are plant compounds studied for hot flashes, with mixed results.

Best ways to eat it

  • Stir 1–2 tablespoons into oatmeal, yogurt or smoothies.
  • Mix into muffins and pancakes.
  • Use as a "flax egg" (1 tbsp + 3 tbsp water).

Typical serving

1–2 tablespoons ground (about 10 g).

When to eat it

Breakfast or with meals. Take medicines 1–2 hours apart from flaxseed, as fiber can slow their absorption.

Who may benefit

  • People with constipation
  • Anyone supporting heart health
  • Women in menopause

Possible downsides

  • Drink extra water — too much fiber without fluid can worsen constipation.
  • Store ground flax in the fridge.

Allergies & interactions

Allergy is uncommon. Large amounts may add to bleeding risk with blood thinners and may lower blood sugar. People with hormone-sensitive conditions should ask their doctor.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Small food amounts are likely fine; avoid large amounts and flaxseed supplements during pregnancy and breastfeeding, as safety hasn't been well studied.
