Sardines

Fish & seafood

What it is

Sardines are small, oily fish usually sold canned. Because they're eaten bones and all, they're one of the best non-dairy sources of calcium, and they're low in mercury.

Key nutrients

  • About 20 g protein per can
  • Omega-3s EPA + DHA
  • Calcium (from soft bones)
  • Vitamin B12
  • Vitamin D
  • Selenium

Potential benefits

Heart health (Some research)

Like other oily fish, sardines supply omega-3s that may help lower triglycerides and support heart health.

Bone health (Some research)

A can of sardines with bones can provide about a third of the daily calcium most adults need, along with vitamin D.

Energy (Some research)

Sardines are rich in vitamin B12, which is needed for healthy red blood cells and energy.

Best ways to eat it

  • Mash onto whole-grain toast with lemon and pepper.
  • Toss with pasta, olive oil, garlic and parsley.
  • Add to a salad for an easy protein.
  • Make sardine "tuna salad" with Greek yogurt and celery.

Typical serving

1 can (about 3.75 oz / 92 g), 2 times a week (about 92 g).

When to eat it

Lunch or dinner. Keep a few cans in the pantry for quick, protein-rich meals.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone wanting affordable omega-3s
  • People who don't eat dairy (for calcium)
  • Older adults supporting bones
  • Pregnant women looking for low-mercury fish

Possible downsides

  • Canned sardines can be high in sodium — rinse or choose low-sodium.
  • High in purines, which may trigger flares in people with gout.

Allergies & interactions

Fish is a major food allergen.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Sardines are on the FDA's "best choices" list for low-mercury fish — 2–3 servings a week is encouraged during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
