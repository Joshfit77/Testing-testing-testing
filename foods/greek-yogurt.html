Greek Yogurt

Dairy & fermented

What it is

Greek yogurt is yogurt that has been strained to remove much of the liquid whey, making it thicker and higher in protein. Choose plain yogurt with "live and active cultures" and add your own fruit and honey.

Key nutrients

  • 15–20 g protein per 6 oz (170 g)
  • Calcium
  • Live cultures (probiotics)
  • Vitamin B12
  • Iodine

Potential benefits

High-protein snack (Well researched)

One serving gives roughly as much protein as three eggs, which may support fullness and muscle maintenance.

Gut health (Some research)

Yogurts with live cultures supply beneficial bacteria. Fermented dairy has been studied for supporting digestion and a healthy gut microbiome.

Bone health (Some research)

Its calcium and protein support bones — especially important for women as they age.

Best ways to eat it

  • Top with berries, nuts and a drizzle of honey.
  • Use instead of sour cream on tacos and potatoes.
  • Blend into smoothies for creaminess and protein.
  • Stir with garlic, cucumber and dill for tzatziki.

Typical serving

¾ cup (6 oz) (about 170 g).

When to eat it

Breakfast or an afternoon snack. A small bowl in the evening can be a satisfying, protein-rich treat.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone looking for an easy high-protein snack
  • People supporting gut health
  • Women needing more calcium
  • People with mild lactose intolerance (Greek yogurt is lower in lactose)

Possible downsides

  • Flavored yogurts can contain a lot of added sugar — choose plain.
  • Dairy doesn't agree with everyone.

Allergies & interactions

Contains milk, a common allergen. Usually tolerated better than milk by people with lactose intolerance, but not safe for anyone with a milk allergy.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

A great choice during pregnancy and breastfeeding for protein and calcium. Choose yogurt made from pasteurized milk.
