Salmon

Fish & seafood

What it is

Salmon is an oily fish rich in the long-chain omega-3 fats EPA and DHA, plus high-quality protein and vitamin D. It is one of the FDA's "best choices" for low-mercury fish.

Key nutrients

  • About 22 g protein per 3 oz (85 g)
  • Omega-3s EPA + DHA (about 1–1.8 g)
  • Vitamin D
  • Vitamin B12
  • Selenium

Potential benefits

Heart health (Well researched)

Eating fatty fish about twice a week is linked with a lower risk of dying from heart disease in large studies, and omega-3s help lower triglycerides.

Brain and mood (Some research)

DHA is a building block of the brain. Diets with regular fish have been studied for supporting memory with age and mood.

Calmer inflammation (Some research)

EPA and DHA are turned into compounds that help the body resolve inflammation, which has been studied for joint comfort.

Best ways to eat it

  • Bake with lemon, garlic and dill at 400°F (200°C) for 12–15 minutes.
  • Flake into salads or grain bowls.
  • Make salmon patties with canned salmon (bones add calcium).
  • Grill on a cedar plank in summer.

Typical serving

3–4 oz cooked (85–113 g), 2 times a week (about 113 g).

When to eat it

Lunch or dinner, about twice a week. Pair with vegetables and a whole grain.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone supporting heart health
  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women (DHA for baby's brain — cooked)
  • People who rarely eat fish
  • Older adults supporting memory

Possible downsides

  • Smoked and cured salmon can be high in salt.
  • Large amounts of omega-3 supplements (not food) may increase bleeding risk with blood thinners.

Allergies & interactions

Fish is a major food allergen. People allergic to one fish are often allergic to others — ask your allergist.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

The FDA advises 2–3 servings a week of low-mercury fish like salmon during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Cook to 145°F (63°C); avoid raw sushi and refrigerated smoked salmon unless cooked into a hot dish.
