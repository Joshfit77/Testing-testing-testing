Kefir

Dairy & fermented

What it is

Kefir is a tangy, drinkable fermented milk made with kefir "grains" — a mix of bacteria and yeasts. It usually contains more types of microbes than yogurt.

Key nutrients

  • About 9 g protein per cup (240 ml)
  • Calcium
  • Many probiotic strains
  • Vitamin B12
  • Riboflavin

Potential benefits

Gut health (Some research)

Kefir supplies a wide range of live microbes, and small studies suggest it may support digestion and a diverse gut microbiome.

Easier on lactose (Some research)

The fermentation breaks down much of the lactose, and kefir has been studied for improving lactose digestion.

Immune support (Traditional use)

Fermented milks have a long tradition of use for resilience; early studies suggest probiotics may modestly support immune health.

Best ways to eat it

  • Drink plain or blend with berries.
  • Use in overnight oats instead of milk.
  • Make a tangy salad dressing with herbs.
  • Use in pancakes and baking like buttermilk.

Typical serving

1 cup (240 ml) (about 240 g).

When to eat it

Morning or as a snack. Start with ½ cup if you're new to fermented foods.

Who may benefit

  • People supporting gut health
  • People with mild lactose intolerance
  • Anyone looking for a probiotic food

Possible downsides

  • Flavored kefir often has added sugar.
  • May cause temporary bloating when you first start.

Allergies & interactions

Contains milk, a major allergen. People with a weakened immune system should ask their doctor before using probiotic foods.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Choose kefir made from pasteurized milk during pregnancy.
