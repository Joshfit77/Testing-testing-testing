Sauerkraut

Vegetables

What it is

Sauerkraut is finely shredded cabbage fermented with salt. Raw, refrigerated sauerkraut contains live microbes; shelf-stable jars are usually pasteurized and don't.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin K
  • Live microbes (if raw)
  • Sodium

Potential benefits

Gut health (Some research)

Fermented vegetables supply beneficial bacteria and have been studied for supporting a more diverse gut microbiome.

Digestion (Some research)

A small forkful with meals is a traditional digestive aid.

Immune support (Traditional use)

Fermented cabbage has a long tradition as a winter food for health.

Best ways to eat it

  • Add a forkful to sandwiches, salads and grain bowls.
  • Serve beside eggs or roasted meats.
  • Make your own with cabbage and salt in a jar.

Typical serving

2 tablespoons to ¼ cup (about 35 g).

When to eat it

With meals. Start with a small forkful if you're new to fermented foods.

Who may benefit

  • People supporting gut health
  • Anyone wanting more fermented foods

Possible downsides

  • High in sodium — use small portions if you watch your salt.
  • May cause gas or bloating at first.

Allergies & interactions

Fermented foods contain tyramine — avoid with MAOI antidepressants. People with histamine intolerance may react. Ask your doctor before eating raw fermented foods if your immune system is weakened.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Generally considered fine as a food during pregnancy; mind the sodium.
