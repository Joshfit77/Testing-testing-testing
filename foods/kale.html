Kale

Vegetables

What it is

Kale is a hearty leafy green in the cabbage family, rich in vitamins K, A and C and in protective plant compounds.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin K (very high)
  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A (beta-carotene)
  • Calcium
  • Glucosinolates

Potential benefits

Immune support (Some research)

Kale is rich in vitamin C and vitamin A, both used by the immune system.

Antioxidant protection (Some research)

Like other cabbage-family vegetables, kale contains glucosinolates that have been studied for supporting the body's natural detox enzymes.

Bone health (Some research)

Provides vitamin K and calcium for bones.

Best ways to eat it

  • Massage raw kale with lemon and olive oil for salads.
  • Bake into kale chips.
  • Add to soups and stews.
  • Blend into green smoothies.

Typical serving

1 cup raw or ½ cup cooked (about 65 g).

When to eat it

Lunch or dinner.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone supporting immunity
  • People eating more greens

Possible downsides

  • Very large amounts of raw kale (in smoothies) contain goitrogens that may affect the thyroid in people with low iodine — cooking reduces them.

Allergies & interactions

Very high in vitamin K: keep intake consistent if you take warfarin.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

A healthy green during pregnancy; wash well.
