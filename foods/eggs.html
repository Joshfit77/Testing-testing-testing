Eggs

Meat & eggs

What it is

Eggs are one of the most affordable sources of complete protein, with all nine essential amino acids. The yolk carries most of the vitamins, choline and healthy fats, so the whole egg is the most nourishing choice.

Key nutrients

  • 6 g protein per large egg
  • Choline (about 150 mg)
  • Vitamin B12
  • Vitamin D (small amount)
  • Lutein & zeaxanthin
  • Selenium

Potential benefits

Protein for muscle and fullness (Well researched)

Eggs provide high-quality protein that supports muscle repair. Studies suggest a protein-rich breakfast such as eggs may help people feel full longer.

Brain and baby development (Some research)

Eggs are one of the richest food sources of choline, a nutrient used for memory and mood signaling and important for a baby's brain development during pregnancy.

Eye health (Some research)

The lutein and zeaxanthin in yolks collect in the retina and have been studied for supporting eye health with age.

Best ways to eat it

  • Scramble with spinach and peppers for a veggie-packed breakfast.
  • Hard-boil a batch for quick snacks and salads.
  • Top a grain bowl or toast with a soft-cooked egg.
  • Bake into an egg muffin cup with vegetables.

Typical serving

1–2 large eggs (about 100 g).

When to eat it

Breakfast is a great time — a protein-rich start may help with steady energy and fullness until lunch. Also handy as a post-workout snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone wanting more protein at breakfast
  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women (for choline — fully cooked)
  • Active people and older adults maintaining muscle
  • People who don't eat meat but do eat eggs

Possible downsides

  • Fry in a little olive oil rather than lots of butter, and pair with vegetables rather than processed meats.
  • People with diabetes or high LDL cholesterol may be advised to keep yolks moderate — follow your doctor's guidance.
  • Raw or runny eggs can carry Salmonella.

Allergies & interactions

Egg is one of the most common food allergies, especially in children. Egg allergy can be serious — avoid eggs and foods containing them if you are allergic.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Eggs are a great pregnancy food for protein and choline. Cook until the yolk and white are firm, and avoid raw-egg foods such as homemade mayonnaise, raw cookie dough or eggnog.
