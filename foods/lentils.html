Lentils

Beans & lentils

What it is

Lentils are small, quick-cooking legumes that don't need soaking. They pack protein, fiber, folate and iron into an inexpensive pantry staple.

Key nutrients

  • 9 g protein per ½ cup cooked (100 g)
  • 8 g fiber
  • Folate (about 45% of daily needs)
  • Iron
  • Potassium

Potential benefits

Heart health (Well researched)

Eating beans and lentils regularly has been linked with lower LDL cholesterol in clinical trials.

Steadier blood sugar (Well researched)

Their fiber and protein slow digestion, so lentils raise blood sugar gently — helpful for people managing blood sugar.

Gut health and regularity (Some research)

Lentil fiber feeds healthy gut bacteria and supports regular bowel movements.

Best ways to eat it

  • Simmer into lentil soup with carrots, celery and cumin.
  • Toss cooked lentils into salads and grain bowls.
  • Make a lentil Bolognese or shepherd's pie.
  • Blend red lentils into dal with turmeric and ginger.

Typical serving

½ cup cooked (about 100 g).

When to eat it

Lunch or dinner. Start with small portions if you're not used to fiber, and drink extra water.

Who may benefit

  • People managing blood sugar
  • Anyone supporting heart health
  • Women who need folate and iron
  • Vegetarians and families on a budget

Possible downsides

  • Can cause gas and bloating at first — increase gradually and rinse canned lentils.
  • Plant (non-heme) iron is absorbed better with vitamin C foods.

Allergies & interactions

Lentil allergy is uncommon but can occur, sometimes alongside pea or chickpea allergy.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

An excellent pregnancy food for folate, iron, protein and fiber (which also helps with pregnancy constipation).
