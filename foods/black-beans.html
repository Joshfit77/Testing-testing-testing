Black Beans

Beans & lentils

What it is

Black beans are a staple of Latin American cooking, rich in fiber, plant protein and deeply colored anthocyanin antioxidants.

Key nutrients

  • 7.5 g protein per ½ cup cooked (86 g)
  • 7.5 g fiber
  • Folate
  • Magnesium
  • Anthocyanins

Potential benefits

Heart health (Well researched)

Regular bean eaters tend to have lower LDL cholesterol, and bean fiber helps carry cholesterol out of the body.

Blood sugar balance (Well researched)

Beans digest slowly and have been studied for blunting the blood-sugar rise of a meal, especially when paired with rice.

Gut health (Some research)

Their fiber and resistant starch feed beneficial gut bacteria.

Best ways to eat it

  • Add to tacos, burritos and rice bowls.
  • Make black bean soup with cumin and lime.
  • Mash into bean burgers.
  • Toss with corn, tomato and cilantro for a salsa salad.

Typical serving

½ cup cooked (about 86 g).

When to eat it

Lunch or dinner. Pairing beans with rice is a classic, balanced combination.

Who may benefit

  • People managing blood sugar
  • Anyone supporting heart health
  • Budget-conscious families

Possible downsides

  • Can cause gas — soak dried beans, rinse canned ones and increase slowly.

Allergies & interactions

Bean allergies are uncommon.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

A great source of folate and fiber during pregnancy.
