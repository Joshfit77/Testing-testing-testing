Carrots

Vegetables

What it is

Carrots are crunchy root vegetables famous for beta-carotene, which your body turns into vitamin A.

Key nutrients

  • Beta-carotene (vitamin A)
  • Fiber
  • Vitamin K
  • Potassium

Potential benefits

Eye health (Some research)

Vitamin A is essential for vision, especially in dim light.

Skin and immunity (Some research)

Vitamin A supports healthy skin and the linings that help defend against germs.

Digestion (Some research)

Raw carrots provide fiber that supports regularity.

Best ways to eat it

  • Snack on raw carrot sticks with hummus.
  • Roast with honey and thyme.
  • Grate into salads or muffins.
  • Simmer into soups and broths.

Typical serving

½ cup chopped or 1 medium carrot (about 64 g).

When to eat it

Any time — an easy snack between meals.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone wanting an easy, crunchy vegetable snack
  • Children (cut safely for age)

Possible downsides

  • Eating very large amounts can harmlessly turn the skin slightly orange.

Allergies & interactions

People with birch pollen allergy may feel mouth itching from raw carrots (oral allergy syndrome); cooking usually helps.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

A healthy source of food-based vitamin A during pregnancy.
