Extra Virgin Olive Oil

Oils & healthy fats

What it is

Extra virgin olive oil is cold-pressed from olives without chemicals or heat. It's the main fat of the Mediterranean diet and rich in monounsaturated fat and polyphenols.

Key nutrients

  • Monounsaturated fat (oleic acid)
  • Polyphenols (oleocanthal, hydroxytyrosol)
  • Vitamin E
  • Vitamin K

Potential benefits

Heart health (Well researched)

In the large PREDIMED trial, a Mediterranean diet with extra virgin olive oil was linked with fewer heart attacks and strokes than a low-fat diet.

Calmer inflammation (Some research)

Oleocanthal has an anti-inflammatory action in the lab similar in kind to ibuprofen, though much milder.

Better nutrient absorption (Some research)

Cooking vegetables in olive oil helps your body absorb their fat-soluble vitamins and carotenoids.

Best ways to eat it

  • Drizzle over salads, vegetables and soups.
  • Sauté vegetables over medium heat.
  • Mix with lemon and herbs for dressings.
  • Use instead of butter on bread.

Typical serving

1–2 tablespoons (about 14 g).

When to eat it

With meals throughout the day, in place of butter and other fats.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone supporting heart health
  • People following a Mediterranean-style diet

Possible downsides

  • Calorie-dense (about 120 calories per tablespoon).
  • Store away from light and heat to keep it fresh.

Allergies & interactions

Olive oil allergy is very rare.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

A healthy fat during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
