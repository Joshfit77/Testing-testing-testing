Raw Honey

Pantry & treats

What it is

Honey is the natural sweetener bees make from flower nectar. Raw honey is minimally processed. It's still a sugar, so a little goes a long way.

Key nutrients

  • About 17 g natural sugars per tablespoon
  • Small amounts of antioxidants
  • Enzymes (in raw honey)

Potential benefits

Cough relief (Well researched)

Reviews of studies found honey may ease cough symptoms in children over 1 year and adults about as well as some over-the-counter cough medicines.

Soothing a sore throat (Some research)

Honey coats the throat, and warm water with honey and lemon is a classic comfort when unwell.

Wound care (medical honey) (Some research)

Medical-grade honey is used in some wound dressings — kitchen honey should not be used on wounds.

Best ways to eat it

  • Stir into warm water or tea with lemon.
  • Drizzle on yogurt or oatmeal.
  • Take 1–2 teaspoons at bedtime for a cough (ages 1 and up).
  • Use in salad dressings.

Typical serving

1–2 teaspoons (about 10 g).

When to eat it

As needed; a spoonful at bedtime has been studied for nighttime coughs.

Who may benefit

  • People with a cough or sore throat (ages 1 and up)
  • Anyone replacing refined sugar in small amounts

Possible downsides

  • Still a sugar — count it if you manage blood sugar or weight.
  • Can contribute to tooth decay.

Allergies & interactions

Never give honey to babies under 1 year — it can cause infant botulism. People with pollen or bee allergies may rarely react.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Honey is considered safe for pregnant and breastfeeding adults.
