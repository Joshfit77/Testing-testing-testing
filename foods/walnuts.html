Walnuts

Nuts & seeds

What it is

Walnuts are the tree nut richest in ALA, the plant form of omega-3 fat, along with polyphenol antioxidants.

Key nutrients

  • Omega-3 ALA (about 2.5 g per oz)
  • 4 g protein
  • Polyphenols
  • Magnesium
  • Melatonin (small amounts)

Potential benefits

Heart health (Well researched)

Walnut-rich diets have been shown to lower LDL cholesterol in clinical trials.

Brain health (Some research)

Walnuts have been studied for supporting memory and thinking as part of a Mediterranean-style diet.

Calmer inflammation (Some research)

Their ALA and polyphenols may help lower inflammation markers.

Best ways to eat it

  • Sprinkle on oatmeal, salads and yogurt.
  • Toast and add to roasted vegetables.
  • Blend into pesto.
  • Snack with dried fruit.

Typical serving

1 oz (about 14 halves) (about 28 g).

When to eat it

Any time; a small handful in the evening is a gentle, sleep-friendly snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone supporting heart and brain health
  • Vegetarians wanting plant omega-3s

Possible downsides

  • Calorie-dense — keep to a small handful.
  • Store in the fridge so the oils don't go rancid.

Allergies & interactions

Tree nuts are a major allergen. Whole nuts are a choking hazard for young children.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

A healthy snack during pregnancy unless allergic.
