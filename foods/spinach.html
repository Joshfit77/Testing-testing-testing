Spinach

Vegetables

What it is

Spinach is a tender leafy green that cooks down quickly. It's rich in folate, vitamin K, magnesium and plant compounds such as lutein and nitrates.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin K (very high)
  • Folate
  • Magnesium
  • Iron (plant form)
  • Vitamin A (beta-carotene)
  • Lutein

Potential benefits

Folate and iron (Some research)

Spinach provides folate and plant iron, which support healthy red blood cells — eat with vitamin C foods to help absorb the iron.

Magnesium for relaxation (Some research)

Magnesium supports muscle and nerve relaxation and has been studied for sleep quality.

Blood pressure and eyes (Some research)

Its natural nitrates may help relax blood vessels, and its lutein supports eye health.

Best ways to eat it

  • Wilt into eggs, soups and pasta.
  • Blend a handful into smoothies.
  • Use raw in salads with strawberries and walnuts.
  • Sauté with garlic and olive oil.

Typical serving

1 cup raw or ½ cup cooked (about 90 g).

When to eat it

Any meal. Add to breakfast eggs, lunch salads or dinner sides.

Who may benefit

  • Women needing folate and iron
  • Anyone eating more greens
  • People supporting heart health

Possible downsides

  • High in oxalates, which may contribute to kidney stones in people prone to them.
  • Wash well — leafy greens can carry germs.

Allergies & interactions

Very high in vitamin K: if you take warfarin, keep the amount of leafy greens you eat consistent from week to week and tell your doctor about big changes.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

An excellent folate source during pregnancy. Wash thoroughly.
