Raspberry

Rubus idaeus

What it is

Raspberry is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Rubus idaeus. In season: summer.

Raspberries are delicate berries with one of the highest fiber contents of any fruit. They are rich in vitamin C, manganese and ellagic acid antioxidants. Their low sugar and high fiber help keep blood sugar steady. Raspberry polyphenols support heart and blood vessel health. Raspberry leaf is also a traditional women's herb.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber (8 g per cup)
  • Vitamin C
  • Manganese
  • Ellagitannins
  • Anthocyanins

Potential benefits

Blood sugar (Some research)

Eating raspberries with a meal lowered blood sugar and insulin in a small trial.

Digestion (Some research)

One cup provides about 8 g of fiber.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Ellagitannins and anthocyanins protect cells.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh by the handful.
  • Top oatmeal, yogurt or pancakes.
  • Blend into smoothies.
  • Mash into a quick chia jam.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 123 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting digestion and regularity
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Women at any life stage

Possible downsides

  • Generally very safe.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
