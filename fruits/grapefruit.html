Grapefruit

Citrus × paradisi

What it is

Grapefruit is a fruit from the Rutaceae family (citrus family), known botanically as Citrus × paradisi. In season: winter.

Grapefruit is a tangy citrus fruit rich in vitamin C. Pink and red grapefruit also contain lycopene and beta-carotene. It is low in calories and high in water, which helps with fullness. Some studies link grapefruit to modest improvements in weight and cholesterol. However, it interacts with many medicines.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A
  • Lycopene (pink/red)
  • Fiber
  • Potassium

Potential benefits

Immunity (Some research)

Half a grapefruit supplies most of a day's vitamin C.

Weight and fullness (Some research)

Eating grapefruit before meals helped modest weight loss in a small study.

Cholesterol (Some research)

Red grapefruit lowered cholesterol in a small trial.

Best ways to eat it

  • Halve and eat with a spoon.
  • Segment into salads with avocado.
  • Broil with a drizzle of honey.
  • Juice fresh.

Typical serving

½ grapefruit (about 123 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure

Possible downsides

  • Grapefruit blocks the enzyme CYP3A4 and interacts with many medicines, including some statins, blood pressure drugs and anti-anxiety medicines. Ask your pharmacist before eating it if you take any medication.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
