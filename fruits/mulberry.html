Mulberry

Morus alba / Morus nigra

What it is

Mulberry is a fruit from the Moraceae family (mulberry family), known botanically as Morus alba / Morus nigra. In season: summer.

Mulberries are sweet, juicy berries that grow on trees. They are rich in vitamin C, iron and anthocyanins. White mulberry leaf is studied for helping control blood sugar after meals. Mulberries are delicious fresh or dried. They have been grown for silkworms and food for thousands of years.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Iron
  • Vitamin K
  • Anthocyanins
  • Fiber

Potential benefits

Blood sugar (Some research)

Mulberry leaf extract (DNJ) reduced the rise in blood sugar after meals in several studies.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Black mulberries are rich in anthocyanins.

Iron (Traditional use)

Mulberries are a good fruit source of iron.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh off the tree.
  • Add dried mulberries to granola.
  • Bake into pies.
  • Blend into smoothies.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 140 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Mulberry leaf supplements can lower blood sugar further with diabetes medicine. Mulberry juice stains.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
