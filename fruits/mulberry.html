Mulberry

Morus alba / Morus nigra · Moraceae (mulberry family) · In season: Summer

What it does for you

Mulberries are sweet, juicy berries that grow on trees. They are rich in vitamin C, iron and anthocyanins. White mulberry leaf is studied for helping control blood sugar after meals. Mulberries are delicious fresh or dried. They have been grown for silkworms and food for thousands of years.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Iron
  • Vitamin K
  • Anthocyanins
  • Fiber

Benefits explained

Blood sugar (Some research)

Mulberry leaf extract (DNJ) reduced the rise in blood sugar after meals in several studies.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Black mulberries are rich in anthocyanins.

Iron (Traditional use)

Mulberries are a good fruit source of iron.

How to use it

  • Eat fresh off the tree.
  • Add dried mulberries to granola.
  • Bake into pies.
  • Blend into smoothies.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 cup (about 140 g).

Choosing & storing

Very delicate — eat within a day or two, or freeze. Dried mulberries keep for months.

Safety & cautions

Mulberry leaf supplements can lower blood sugar further with diabetes medicine. Mulberry juice stains.
