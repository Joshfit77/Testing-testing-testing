Passion Fruit

Passiflora edulis

What it is

Passion Fruit is a fruit from the Passifloraceae family (plant family), known botanically as Passiflora edulis. In season: summer to autumn.

Passion fruit is a tangy, fragrant tropical fruit filled with juicy edible seeds. It is one of the highest-fiber fruits by weight. It provides vitamin C, vitamin A and antioxidants. Passion fruit peel extract has been studied for easing asthma and blood pressure. Its intense flavor brightens desserts and drinks.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A
  • Iron
  • Polyphenols

Potential benefits

Digestion (Some research)

Passion fruit pulp and seeds provide a lot of fiber for its size.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Contains piceatannol, a polyphenol studied for blood vessel health.

Relaxation (Traditional use)

Like its passionflower relative, it is traditionally associated with calm.

Best ways to eat it

  • Halve and scoop out the pulp.
  • Spoon over yogurt or pavlova.
  • Blend into juices and smoothies.
  • Make passion fruit curd.

Typical serving

2 passion fruits (about 36 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting digestion and regularity
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Latex allergy may cross-react. Generally safe.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
