Lime

Citrus × aurantiifolia

What it is

Lime is a fruit from the Rutaceae family (citrus family), known botanically as Citrus × aurantiifolia. In season: year-round.

Limes are small green citrus fruits with a sharp, refreshing flavor. Like lemons, they are rich in vitamin C and flavonoid antioxidants. Their juice brightens food, allowing you to use less salt. Limes famously protected British sailors from scurvy. They are essential to Mexican, Thai and Caribbean cooking.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Flavonoids
  • Citric acid

Potential benefits

Immunity (Traditional use)

Vitamin C supports immune function and skin repair.

Iron absorption (Some research)

Lime juice boosts absorption of plant-based iron.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Lime flavonoids like hesperidin protect cells.

Best ways to eat it

  • Squeeze over tacos, curries and salads.
  • Add to water with mint.
  • Use in salsas and guacamole.
  • Make lime and chili dressings.

Typical serving

Juice of 1 lime (about 30 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Lime juice on skin in the sun can cause painful burns (phytophotodermatitis) — wash hands after handling.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
