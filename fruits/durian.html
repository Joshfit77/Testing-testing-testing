Durian

Durio zibethinus

What it is

Durian is a fruit from the Malvaceae family (mallow family), known botanically as Durio zibethinus. In season: summer.

Durian is the famous 'king of fruits' of Southeast Asia, known for its powerful smell. Its creamy, custard-like flesh is rich in energy, fiber and B vitamins. It provides potassium, vitamin C and healthy plant compounds. It is very filling and traditionally eaten as a special treat. Its strong aroma has gotten it banned on many trains and hotels.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • B vitamins
  • Potassium
  • Healthy fats

Potential benefits

Energy (Traditional use)

Durian is calorie-dense and traditionally eaten to restore strength.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Durian contains polyphenols and flavonoids with antioxidant activity.

Gut health (Traditional use)

Its fiber supports regular digestion.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh pods with a spoon.
  • Blend into smoothies or ice cream.
  • Use in Southeast Asian desserts like durian sticky rice.
  • Freeze pods for a creamy treat.

Typical serving

½ cup (about 120 g).

When to eat it

Morning, or as a snack before activity, for natural energy.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone wanting steadier energy

Possible downsides

  • Avoid drinking alcohol with durian — its sulfur compounds may slow alcohol breakdown and cause nausea and flushing. High in calories and sugar.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
