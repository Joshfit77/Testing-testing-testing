Quince

Cydonia oblonga · Rosaceae (rose family) · In season: Autumn

What it does for you

Quince is an aromatic golden fruit that must be cooked to be enjoyed. Cooking turns its hard, astringent flesh soft, sweet and rosy pink. It is rich in pectin, fiber and vitamin C. Quince is a traditional remedy for coughs and digestive upset. It makes famous jams and Spanish membrillo paste.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber (pectin)
  • Vitamin C
  • Copper
  • Polyphenols

Benefits explained

Digestion (Traditional use)

Quince is traditionally used to soothe the stomach and ease diarrhea.

Reflux (Some research)

A quince syrup reduced reflux symptoms in children in a small trial.

Coughs (Traditional use)

Quince seed mucilage is a traditional throat soother.

How to use it

  • Poach slowly in syrup.
  • Make membrillo (quince paste).
  • Bake with apples.
  • Add to stews.

How much to eat

One serving: ½ cooked quince (about 90 g).

Choosing & storing

Choose fragrant, yellow quince. Store at room temperature for a week or refrigerate for longer.

Safety & cautions

Do not eat raw in large amounts — very astringent. Seeds contain amygdalin.
