Nectarine

Prunus persica var. nucipersica

What it is

Nectarine is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Prunus persica var. nucipersica. In season: summer.

Nectarines are smooth-skinned peaches with juicy, sweet flesh. They provide vitamin C, vitamin A and fiber. Their skin holds most of their antioxidant polyphenols. They are hydrating and low in calories. They are perfect fresh, grilled or baked.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A
  • Fiber
  • Potassium
  • Polyphenols

Potential benefits

Skin (Some research)

Vitamins A and C support collagen and protect skin.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Fiber in the skin supports regularity.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Nectarine polyphenols protect cells in studies.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh with the skin.
  • Grill halves and serve with yogurt.
  • Slice into salads with burrata.
  • Bake into crisps.

Typical serving

1 medium nectarine (about 140 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • Do not eat the pit kernel. May cross-react with birch pollen allergy.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
