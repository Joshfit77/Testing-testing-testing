Nectarine

Prunus persica var. nucipersica · Rosaceae (rose family) · In season: Summer

What it does for you

Nectarines are smooth-skinned peaches with juicy, sweet flesh. They provide vitamin C, vitamin A and fiber. Their skin holds most of their antioxidant polyphenols. They are hydrating and low in calories. They are perfect fresh, grilled or baked.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A
  • Fiber
  • Potassium
  • Polyphenols

Benefits explained

Skin (Some research)

Vitamins A and C support collagen and protect skin.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Fiber in the skin supports regularity.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Nectarine polyphenols protect cells in studies.

How to use it

  • Eat fresh with the skin.
  • Grill halves and serve with yogurt.
  • Slice into salads with burrata.
  • Bake into crisps.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 medium nectarine (about 140 g).

Choosing & storing

Choose fragrant fruit that gives slightly. Ripen at room temperature, then refrigerate.

Safety & cautions

Do not eat the pit kernel. May cross-react with birch pollen allergy.
