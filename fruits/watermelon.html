Watermelon

Citrullus lanatus

What it is

Watermelon is a fruit from the Cucurbitaceae family (gourd family), known botanically as Citrullus lanatus. In season: summer.

Watermelon is about 92% water, making it one of the most hydrating foods. It is rich in lycopene, the same red antioxidant found in tomatoes. It contains citrulline, which may improve blood flow and exercise recovery. It is low in calories and naturally sweet. It's the perfect summer snack.

Key nutrients

  • Water (92%)
  • Lycopene
  • Citrulline
  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A

Potential benefits

Hydration (Some research)

Its water and electrolytes help keep you hydrated in hot weather.

Blood flow (Some research)

Watermelon's citrulline is converted to arginine, which helps blood vessels relax; it lowered blood pressure in small studies.

Muscle soreness (Some research)

Watermelon juice reduced muscle soreness after exercise in a small study.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat chilled slices.
  • Blend into agua fresca.
  • Toss with feta and mint.
  • Freeze into popsicles.

Typical serving

2 cups cubes (about 300 g).

When to eat it

Morning, or as a snack before activity, for natural energy.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone wanting steadier energy

Possible downsides

  • High glycemic index — pair with protein if you have diabetes.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
