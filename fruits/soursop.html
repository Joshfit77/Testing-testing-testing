Soursop

Annona muricata

What it is

Soursop is a fruit from the Annonaceae family (custard apple family), known botanically as Annona muricata. In season: summer.

Soursop, or graviola, is a spiky green fruit with creamy white flesh. It tastes like strawberry, pineapple and citrus combined. It provides vitamin C and fiber. Lab studies have looked at its compounds, but no human studies show it treats disease. It is popular in juices across Latin America and the Caribbean.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Potassium

Potential benefits

Immunity (Some research)

A good source of vitamin C.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Provides fiber.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Contains polyphenols.

Best ways to eat it

  • Scoop the flesh and remove seeds.
  • Blend into juice or smoothies.
  • Make ice cream.
  • Use in desserts.

Typical serving

½ cup (about 112 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • The seeds and leaves contain annonacin, linked to nerve damage — do not eat seeds or drink large amounts of leaf tea. Not a cancer treatment.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
