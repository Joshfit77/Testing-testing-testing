Starfruit

Averrhoa carambola · Oxalidaceae · In season: Autumn to winter

What it does for you

Starfruit, or carambola, is a juicy tropical fruit shaped like a star when sliced. It is low in calories and provides vitamin C and fiber. Its antioxidants include quercetin and epicatechin. It has a crisp, sweet-tart flavor. However, it can be dangerous for people with kidney disease.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Copper
  • Polyphenols

Benefits explained

Immunity (Some research)

Provides vitamin C.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Contains quercetin and gallic acid.

Hydration (Traditional use)

Juicy and refreshing.

How to use it

  • Slice into stars for salads.
  • Garnish drinks and desserts.
  • Eat fresh.
  • Add to salsas.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 medium starfruit (about 91 g).

Choosing & storing

Choose yellow fruit with light brown edges on the ribs. Refrigerate for up to a week.

Safety & cautions

People with kidney disease must not eat starfruit — it contains a neurotoxin (caramboxin) that healthy kidneys remove but damaged kidneys cannot, and it can be fatal. It is also high in oxalate.
