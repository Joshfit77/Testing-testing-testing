Fig

Ficus carica

What it is

Fig is a fruit from the Moraceae family (mulberry family), known botanically as Ficus carica. In season: late summer.

Figs are soft, honey-sweet fruits treasured since biblical times. They are one of the best fruit sources of fiber, supporting regular digestion. Figs provide potassium, calcium and magnesium for heart and bone health. Dried figs are a traditional remedy for constipation. Their natural sweetness makes them a wholesome treat.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Potassium
  • Calcium
  • Magnesium
  • Polyphenols

Potential benefits

Constipation relief (Some research)

A clinical trial found eating dried figs daily improved constipation.

Bone health (Some research)

Figs supply calcium, potassium and magnesium, minerals important for bones.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Fig leaf extract lowered after-meal blood sugar in small studies.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh with yogurt or cheese.
  • Chop dried figs into oatmeal.
  • Roast with honey and walnuts.
  • Add to salads with goat cheese.

Typical serving

2 fresh or 3 dried figs (about 80 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting digestion and regularity
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Women at any life stage

Possible downsides

  • Figs can have a laxative effect in large amounts. Fig sap can irritate skin. People with birch pollen or latex allergy may react.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
