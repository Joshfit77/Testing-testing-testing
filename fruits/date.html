Date

Phoenix dactylifera

What it is

Date is a fruit from the Arecaceae family (palm family), known botanically as Phoenix dactylifera. In season: autumn.

Dates are the sweet fruit of the date palm, eaten for thousands of years in the Middle East. They provide quick natural energy along with fiber, potassium and magnesium. Their fiber helps keep digestion regular. Studies suggest eating dates in late pregnancy may help labor progress. They are a wholesome natural sweetener for snacks and baking.

Key nutrients

  • Natural sugars
  • Fiber
  • Potassium
  • Magnesium
  • Copper

Potential benefits

Quick energy (Some research)

Their natural sugars provide fast fuel, which is why dates are used to break the fast in Ramadan.

Late pregnancy (Some research)

Several studies found women who ate about six dates a day in late pregnancy had more favorable labor — talk to your midwife.

Regularity (Traditional use)

Fiber in dates supports healthy digestion.

Best ways to eat it

  • Stuff with nut butter or almonds.
  • Blend into smoothies as a sweetener.
  • Make energy balls with nuts and oats.
  • Chop into baking instead of sugar.

Typical serving

2–3 dates (about 48 g).

When to eat it

Morning, or as a snack before activity, for natural energy.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone wanting steadier energy
  • People supporting digestion and regularity
  • Women at any life stage

Possible downsides

  • Dates are high in natural sugar — keep portions small if you have diabetes.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Pregnant or trying to conceive: Eating dates in late pregnancy may help labor — talk to your midwife.
