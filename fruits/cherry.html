Sweet Cherry

Prunus avium

What it is

Sweet Cherry is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Prunus avium. In season: early summer.

Sweet cherries are juicy summer fruits rich in antioxidants. Their deep red color comes from anthocyanins that calm inflammation. Cherries contain small amounts of natural melatonin, the hormone that regulates sleep. They provide vitamin C, potassium and fiber. Studies suggest cherries may lower uric acid and reduce gout flare-ups.

Key nutrients

  • Anthocyanins
  • Vitamin C
  • Potassium
  • Melatonin
  • Fiber

Potential benefits

Gout and joints (Some research)

A large study found people who ate cherries had about 35% fewer gout attacks.

Inflammation (Some research)

Eating sweet cherries lowered markers of inflammation in healthy adults.

Sleep (Some research)

Cherries contain melatonin and tryptophan, which help regulate sleep.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh as a snack.
  • Pit and add to salads or yogurt.
  • Bake into clafoutis or pies.
  • Freeze for smoothies.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 20 cherries) (about 138 g).

When to eat it

An evening snack about an hour before bed may suit it best.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People working on restful sleep
  • Anyone eating to support joint comfort and calmer inflammation
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure

Possible downsides

  • Do not crush or eat the pits, which contain amygdalin. Watch for choking in young children — always pit for them.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
