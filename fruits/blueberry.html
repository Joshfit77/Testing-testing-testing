Blueberry

Vaccinium corymbosum

What it is

Blueberry is a fruit from the Ericaceae family (heath family), known botanically as Vaccinium corymbosum. In season: summer.

Blueberries are one of the most researched berries for brain and heart health. Their anthocyanins protect blood vessels and may support memory as we age. Studies link regular blueberry eating with healthier blood pressure and better insulin sensitivity. They are low in calories yet rich in fiber, vitamin C and vitamin K. Fresh or frozen, they are an easy daily superfood.

Key nutrients

  • Anthocyanins
  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin K
  • Fiber
  • Manganese

Potential benefits

Brain and memory (Some research)

Trials in older adults found daily blueberries improved some measures of memory and thinking.

Heart and blood vessels (Well researched)

In a 6-month trial, one cup a day improved blood vessel function and cholesterol in people with metabolic syndrome.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Blueberry anthocyanins improved insulin sensitivity in small trials.

Best ways to eat it

  • Add to oatmeal, yogurt or cereal.
  • Blend frozen blueberries into smoothies.
  • Bake into muffins or pancakes.
  • Eat a handful as a snack.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 148 g).

When to eat it

Morning, or as a snack before activity, for natural energy.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone wanting steadier energy
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Very safe. Large amounts may affect blood sugar medication slightly.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
