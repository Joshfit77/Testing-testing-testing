Jabuticaba

Plinia cauliflora

What it is

Jabuticaba is a fruit from the Myrtaceae family (myrtle family), known botanically as Plinia cauliflora. In season: spring.

Jabuticaba is a Brazilian fruit that grows directly on the tree trunk. Its dark skin hides sweet, grape-like white flesh. The skin is rich in anthocyanins and ellagitannins. It is eaten fresh or made into jams, juices and wine. It spoils quickly, so it's rarely found outside Brazil.

Key nutrients

  • Anthocyanins
  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Ellagitannins

Potential benefits

Antioxidants (Some research)

The peel is very rich in anthocyanins.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Jabuticaba peel improved blood sugar response in a small study.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Traditionally used for diarrhea.

Best ways to eat it

  • Pop the flesh out of the skin and eat.
  • Make jam or juice.
  • Blend into smoothies.
  • Use in desserts.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 140 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Generally safe.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
