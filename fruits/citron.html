Citron (Etrog)

Citrus medica

What it is

Citron (Etrog) is a fruit from the Rutaceae family (citrus family), known botanically as Citrus medica. In season: autumn.

Citron is one of the original citrus fruits, with a thick, fragrant peel. It is used as the etrog during the Jewish festival of Sukkot. Its peel is candied for baking. It provides vitamin C and fiber. Its aromatic oils are prized in perfume.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Flavonoids

Potential benefits

Digestion (Traditional use)

Citron peel is traditionally used for nausea and digestion.

Immunity (Some research)

Provides vitamin C.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Contains flavonoids.

Best ways to eat it

  • Candy the peel.
  • Use zest in baking.
  • Infuse in syrups.
  • Slice thin into salads.

Typical serving

1 tbsp candied peel (about 15 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • Candied peel is high in sugar.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
