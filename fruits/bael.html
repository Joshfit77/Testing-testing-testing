Bael (Wood Apple)

Aegle marmelos

What it is

Bael (Wood Apple) is a fruit from the Rutaceae family (citrus family), known botanically as Aegle marmelos. In season: spring.

Bael is a hard-shelled fruit sacred in India, with aromatic orange pulp. It is a classic Ayurvedic remedy for digestion. Unripe bael is used for diarrhea, while ripe bael is used for constipation. It is rich in vitamin C, fiber and tannins. Bael sharbat is a cooling summer drink.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Tannins
  • Mucilage

Potential benefits

Diarrhea (Traditional use)

Unripe bael's tannins and mucilage firm stools.

Constipation (Traditional use)

Ripe bael pulp has a gentle laxative effect.

Antimicrobial (Some research)

Bael extracts fight gut bacteria in lab studies.

Best ways to eat it

  • Make bael sharbat with water and jaggery.
  • Eat ripe pulp.
  • Use bael powder.
  • Make bael candy.

Typical serving

½ cup pulp (about 100 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • May lower blood sugar with diabetes medicine.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
