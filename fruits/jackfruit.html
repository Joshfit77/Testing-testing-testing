Jackfruit

Artocarpus heterophyllus

What it is

Jackfruit is a fruit from the Moraceae family (mulberry family), known botanically as Artocarpus heterophyllus. In season: summer.

Jackfruit is the largest tree fruit in the world, sometimes weighing over 40 kg. Ripe jackfruit is sweet and tastes like a mix of banana, pineapple and mango. Young green jackfruit has a meaty texture and is popular as a plant-based meat substitute. It provides fiber, potassium and vitamin C. Its seeds are also edible when cooked.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Potassium
  • Vitamin B6
  • Magnesium

Potential benefits

Energy (Traditional use)

Ripe jackfruit provides natural carbohydrates for energy.

Digestion (Some research)

Its fiber supports gut health.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Green jackfruit flour lowered blood sugar in a small trial.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat ripe pods fresh.
  • Cook young jackfruit as 'pulled pork' with barbecue sauce.
  • Add to curries.
  • Boil or roast the seeds.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 165 g).

When to eat it

Morning, or as a snack before activity, for natural energy.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone wanting steadier energy
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • Latex allergy may cross-react. Jackfruit seeds must be cooked.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
