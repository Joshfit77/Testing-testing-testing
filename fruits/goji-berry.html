Goji Berry

Lycium barbarum

What it is

Goji Berry is a fruit from the Solanaceae family (nightshade family), known botanically as Lycium barbarum. In season: late summer.

Goji berries, or wolfberries, have been used in Chinese medicine for centuries. They are rich in zeaxanthin, an antioxidant that protects the eyes. Their unique polysaccharides support immune function in studies. Goji berries provide vitamin A, vitamin C and iron. Dried goji berries make a chewy, tangy snack.

Key nutrients

  • Zeaxanthin
  • Vitamin A
  • Vitamin C
  • Iron
  • Polysaccharides

Potential benefits

Eye health (Some research)

Daily goji berries increased protective pigment in the macula of older adults in a trial.

Immune support (Some research)

Goji polysaccharides boosted immune responses in older adults in studies.

Energy and wellbeing (Traditional use)

A small trial found goji juice improved feelings of energy and calm.

Best ways to eat it

  • Add dried berries to trail mix or granola.
  • Soak and add to oatmeal or tea.
  • Blend into smoothies.
  • Add to soups in Chinese cooking.

Typical serving

2 tbsp dried (about 28 g).

When to eat it

Morning, or as a snack before activity, for natural energy.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season
  • Anyone wanting steadier energy

Possible downsides

  • Goji may increase the effect of warfarin — avoid if you take blood thinners. It belongs to the nightshade family.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
