Apricot

Prunus armeniaca

What it is

Apricot is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Prunus armeniaca. In season: early summer.

Apricots are small golden fruits rich in beta-carotene, which the body turns into vitamin A. Vitamin A supports healthy skin, vision and immune defenses. Apricots also provide fiber and potassium for digestion and heart health. Dried apricots are a convenient source of iron and fiber. Their sweet-tart flavor makes them perfect fresh, dried or baked.

Key nutrients

  • Beta-carotene (vitamin A)
  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Potassium

Potential benefits

Eye and skin health (Some research)

Beta-carotene and lutein help protect the eyes and skin from damage by light and free radicals.

Regularity (Traditional use)

Fresh and dried apricots provide fiber that helps keep digestion moving.

Antioxidant protection (Some research)

Apricots contain chlorogenic acid and catechins that help protect cells.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh, skin and all.
  • Chop dried apricots into oatmeal or trail mix.
  • Grill or roast halves with honey.
  • Add to tagines and grain salads.

Typical serving

2 fresh apricots (about 70 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • Do not eat the kernels inside the pit — they contain amygdalin, which can cause cyanide poisoning. Dried apricots are often treated with sulfites, which can trigger asthma in sensitive people.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
