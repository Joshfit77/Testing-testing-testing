Tangerine

Citrus reticulata

What it is

Tangerine is a fruit from the Rutaceae family (citrus family), known botanically as Citrus reticulata. In season: winter.

Tangerines are small, sweet, easy-to-peel citrus fruits. They are rich in vitamin C and beta-cryptoxanthin, which the body turns into vitamin A. They make a convenient snack for adults and children. Their peel is used in Chinese medicine for digestion. They are hydrating and low in calories.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A
  • Beta-cryptoxanthin
  • Fiber

Potential benefits

Immunity (Some research)

Two tangerines provide most of a day's vitamin C.

Bone and joint health (Some research)

Beta-cryptoxanthin is linked with better bone density in studies.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Dried tangerine peel (chenpi) is a traditional digestive aid.

Best ways to eat it

  • Peel and eat as a snack.
  • Add segments to salads.
  • Use zest in baking.
  • Dry the peel for tea.

Typical serving

2 tangerines (about 150 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Generally safe. Citrus may trigger heartburn.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
