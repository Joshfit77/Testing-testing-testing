Tangerine

Citrus reticulata · Rutaceae (citrus family) · In season: Winter

What it does for you

Tangerines are small, sweet, easy-to-peel citrus fruits. They are rich in vitamin C and beta-cryptoxanthin, which the body turns into vitamin A. They make a convenient snack for adults and children. Their peel is used in Chinese medicine for digestion. They are hydrating and low in calories.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A
  • Beta-cryptoxanthin
  • Fiber

Benefits explained

Immunity (Some research)

Two tangerines provide most of a day's vitamin C.

Bone and joint health (Some research)

Beta-cryptoxanthin is linked with better bone density in studies.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Dried tangerine peel (chenpi) is a traditional digestive aid.

How to use it

  • Peel and eat as a snack.
  • Add segments to salads.
  • Use zest in baking.
  • Dry the peel for tea.

How much to eat

One serving: 2 tangerines (about 150 g).

Choosing & storing

Choose heavy, fragrant fruit. Store at room temperature for a week or refrigerate.

Safety & cautions

Generally safe. Citrus may trigger heartburn.
