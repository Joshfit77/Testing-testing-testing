Lemon

Citrus limon · Rutaceae (citrus family) · In season: Year-round

What it does for you

Lemons bring bright flavor and vitamin C to food and drink. Their citric acid may help prevent some kidney stones. Lemon water is an easy way to drink more water through the day. The peel is rich in flavonoids and aromatic oils. A squeeze of lemon also helps the body absorb iron from plant foods.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Citric acid
  • Flavonoids (hesperidin)
  • Fiber (pectin)

Benefits explained

Kidney stones (Some research)

Citrate from lemon juice can raise urine citrate and help prevent calcium kidney stones.

Iron absorption (Some research)

Vitamin C greatly increases absorption of iron from beans, greens and grains.

Sore throats (Traditional use)

Warm lemon and honey is a classic remedy for scratchy throats.

How to use it

  • Squeeze into water or tea.
  • Use juice and zest in dressings and marinades.
  • Finish fish, vegetables and grains with a squeeze.
  • Mix with honey and ginger for a soothing drink.

How much to eat

One serving: Juice of ½ lemon (about 25 g).

Choosing & storing

Choose heavy, glossy lemons. Refrigerate in a bag for up to a month.

Safety & cautions

Lemon's acid can erode tooth enamel — drink lemon water with a straw and rinse after. May trigger heartburn.
