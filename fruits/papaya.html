Papaya

Carica papaya · Caricaceae · In season: Year-round

What it does for you

Papaya is a tropical fruit with sweet orange flesh and a buttery texture. It contains papain, an enzyme that helps digest protein. Papaya is rich in vitamin C, vitamin A and folate. It is traditionally eaten for digestive comfort and constipation. Its antioxidants also support healthy skin.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A
  • Folate
  • Fiber
  • Papain

Benefits explained

Digestion (Some research)

A papaya preparation improved constipation and bloating in a clinical study.

Immunity (Some research)

One cup provides more than a day's vitamin C.

Skin (Some research)

Carotenoids like lycopene and beta-carotene protect the skin.

How to use it

  • Halve, scoop seeds and squeeze lime over it.
  • Blend into smoothies.
  • Make green papaya salad.
  • Use to tenderize meat.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 cup cubes (about 145 g).

Choosing & storing

Ripe papaya is mostly yellow and gives to gentle pressure. Refrigerate ripe fruit for a few days.

Safety & cautions

Unripe (green) papaya contains latex that may trigger contractions — avoid green papaya during pregnancy. Latex allergy may cross-react.
