Banana

Musa acuminata · Musaceae (banana family) · In season: Year-round

What it does for you

Bananas are a convenient source of quick, natural energy. They are famous for potassium, which supports healthy blood pressure and muscle function. Slightly green bananas contain resistant starch that feeds good gut bacteria. Bananas are gentle on the stomach and a classic food during upset digestion. Naturally sweet and portable, they're perfect before exercise.

Key nutrients

  • Potassium
  • Vitamin B6
  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Resistant starch (when green)

Benefits explained

Blood pressure (Well researched)

Potassium helps the kidneys remove sodium and relaxes blood vessel walls, which lowers blood pressure.

Exercise fuel (Some research)

In a study of cyclists, bananas fueled performance as well as a sports drink.

Gut health (Some research)

The resistant starch in greener bananas acts as a prebiotic and supports regularity.

How to use it

  • Eat as a quick snack before or after exercise.
  • Slice onto oatmeal or yogurt.
  • Freeze and blend into creamy 'nice cream.'
  • Mash into pancakes or banana bread to replace some sugar.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 medium banana (about 118 g).

Choosing & storing

Buy slightly green for later and yellow for now. Keep at room temperature; once ripe, the fridge slows ripening (the peel darkens but the fruit stays fine).

Safety & cautions

People with kidney disease or on potassium-sparing medicines may need to limit high-potassium foods. Very ripe bananas raise blood sugar faster than greener ones.
