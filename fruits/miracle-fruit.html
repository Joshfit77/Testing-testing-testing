Miracle Fruit

Synsepalum dulcificum

What it is

Miracle Fruit is a fruit from the Sapotaceae family (plant family), known botanically as Synsepalum dulcificum. In season: year-round.

Miracle fruit is a small red berry from West Africa that makes sour foods taste sweet. Its protein, miraculin, binds to sweet taste receptors for about an hour. Lemons taste like lemonade after eating one. It is being studied to help people reduce sugar. It is also used to help chemotherapy patients with taste changes.

Key nutrients

  • Miraculin
  • Vitamin C
  • Antioxidants

Potential benefits

Reducing sugar (Some research)

Miraculin can make sour foods taste sweet without added sugar.

Taste changes (Some research)

Miracle fruit improved taste in people undergoing chemotherapy in a small study.

Fun food experience (Traditional use)

Popular in 'flavor tripping' parties.

Best ways to eat it

  • Chew a berry for a minute, then taste lemons or vinegar.
  • Use before sour foods.
  • Try tablets of freeze-dried fruit.
  • Pair with plain yogurt.

Typical serving

1 berry (about 2 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day

Possible downsides

  • Generally safe.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
