Pawpaw

Asimina triloba

What it is

Pawpaw is a fruit from the Annonaceae family (custard apple family), known botanically as Asimina triloba. In season: late summer to autumn.

Pawpaw is the largest fruit native to North America, with custardy flesh that tastes like banana and mango. It is rich in vitamin C, magnesium and manganese. It provides more protein than most fruits. It is rarely sold in stores because it bruises easily. It is a beloved wild treat in the eastern United States.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Magnesium
  • Manganese
  • Iron
  • Protein

Potential benefits

Energy (Traditional use)

Rich in natural sugars and nutrients.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Contains polyphenols.

Minerals (Traditional use)

Good source of magnesium and iron.

Best ways to eat it

  • Scoop and eat fresh.
  • Blend into smoothies.
  • Bake into breads.
  • Make ice cream.

Typical serving

½ cup (about 100 g).

When to eat it

Morning, or as a snack before activity, for natural energy.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone wanting steadier energy
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Do not eat the skin or seeds. Some people experience stomach upset.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
