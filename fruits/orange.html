Orange

Citrus × sinensis · Rutaceae (citrus family) · In season: Winter

What it does for you

Oranges are the classic vitamin C fruit, with one orange supplying a full day's needs. They are rich in hesperidin, a flavonoid that supports healthy blood vessels. The fiber in whole oranges supports digestion and steady blood sugar. Orange juice provides citrate, which may help prevent kidney stones. Whole oranges are more filling than juice.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Folate
  • Potassium
  • Fiber
  • Hesperidin

Benefits explained

Immunity (Some research)

Vitamin C supports immune cells and may slightly shorten colds.

Heart health (Some research)

Orange juice and hesperidin improved blood pressure and vessel function in some trials.

Iron absorption (Some research)

Eating oranges with plant foods boosts iron absorption.

How to use it

  • Peel and eat segments.
  • Add to salads with fennel.
  • Squeeze fresh juice.
  • Use zest in baking.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 medium orange (about 130 g).

Choosing & storing

Choose heavy, firm oranges. Store at room temperature for a week or refrigerate for longer.

Safety & cautions

Citrus can trigger heartburn. Choose whole fruit over juice to limit sugar.
