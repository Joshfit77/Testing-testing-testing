Orange

Citrus × sinensis

What it is

Orange is a fruit from the Rutaceae family (citrus family), known botanically as Citrus × sinensis. In season: winter.

Oranges are the classic vitamin C fruit, with one orange supplying a full day's needs. They are rich in hesperidin, a flavonoid that supports healthy blood vessels. The fiber in whole oranges supports digestion and steady blood sugar. Orange juice provides citrate, which may help prevent kidney stones. Whole oranges are more filling than juice.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Folate
  • Potassium
  • Fiber
  • Hesperidin

Potential benefits

Immunity (Some research)

Vitamin C supports immune cells and may slightly shorten colds.

Heart health (Some research)

Orange juice and hesperidin improved blood pressure and vessel function in some trials.

Iron absorption (Some research)

Eating oranges with plant foods boosts iron absorption.

Best ways to eat it

  • Peel and eat segments.
  • Add to salads with fennel.
  • Squeeze fresh juice.
  • Use zest in baking.

Typical serving

1 medium orange (about 130 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure

Possible downsides

  • Citrus can trigger heartburn. Choose whole fruit over juice to limit sugar.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
