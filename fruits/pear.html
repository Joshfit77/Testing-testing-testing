Pear

Pyrus communis · Rosaceae (rose family) · In season: Autumn

What it does for you

Pears are sweet, juicy fruits that are one of the best fruit sources of fiber. Much of their fiber is in the skin, so eat them unpeeled. They are gentle on the stomach and a classic first food for babies. Pears provide vitamin C, vitamin K and copper. Their natural sorbitol and fructose can help relieve constipation.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin K
  • Copper
  • Polyphenols

Benefits explained

Digestion (Some research)

Pears' fiber, fructose and sorbitol help soften stools and relieve constipation.

Heart health (Some research)

Eating pears and apples is linked with lower stroke risk in large studies.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Despite their sweetness, pears have a low glycemic index.

How to use it

  • Eat fresh with the skin.
  • Poach in spiced tea.
  • Slice into salads with walnuts and blue cheese.
  • Bake with oats and cinnamon.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 medium pear (about 178 g).

Choosing & storing

Pears ripen from the inside — check the neck near the stem for slight give. Ripen at room temperature, then refrigerate.

Safety & cautions

Pears may cause bloating in people sensitive to fructose or sorbitol (FODMAPs).
