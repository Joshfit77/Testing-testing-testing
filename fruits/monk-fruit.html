Monk Fruit

Siraitia grosvenorii · Cucurbitaceae (gourd family) · In season: Year-round (dried)

What it does for you

Monk fruit, or luo han guo, is a small Chinese melon used as a natural sweetener. Its mogrosides are about 150–250 times sweeter than sugar with almost no calories. It does not raise blood sugar. In Chinese medicine, it is used in tea for coughs and sore throats. Monk fruit sweetener is popular for low-sugar diets.

Key nutrients

  • Mogrosides
  • Antioxidants

Benefits explained

Zero-calorie sweetness (Some research)

Mogrosides are intensely sweet without raising blood sugar.

Coughs and sore throats (Traditional use)

Dried monk fruit tea is a traditional throat remedy.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Mogrosides have antioxidant activity in lab studies.

How to use it

  • Use monk fruit sweetener in drinks and baking.
  • Simmer dried fruit as tea.
  • Sweeten yogurt and oatmeal.
  • Make sugar-free syrups.

How much to eat

One serving: To taste (small amounts) (about 1 g).

Choosing & storing

Check labels — many blends contain erythritol or other sweeteners.

Safety & cautions

Monk fruit itself is considered safe; some blends contain erythritol, which may be linked to heart risks in some research.
