Tart Cherry

Prunus cerasus

What it is

Tart Cherry is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Prunus cerasus. In season: summer.

Tart cherries are sour cherries well studied for sleep and muscle recovery. They contain more natural melatonin than sweet cherries. Their anthocyanins strongly calm inflammation. Tart cherry juice has helped athletes recover faster and reduced muscle soreness. It may also lower uric acid levels involved in gout.

Key nutrients

  • Anthocyanins
  • Melatonin
  • Vitamin A
  • Vitamin C

Potential benefits

Better sleep (Some research)

In small trials, drinking tart cherry juice twice a day increased sleep time and efficiency.

Muscle recovery (Well researched)

Several studies found tart cherry juice reduced muscle soreness and strength loss after hard exercise.

Gout (Some research)

Tart cherry concentrate lowered uric acid in small studies.

Best ways to eat it

  • Drink 1 cup (240 ml) of unsweetened juice twice a day.
  • Add dried tart cherries to salads and granola.
  • Bake into pies and crumbles.
  • Stir concentrate into sparkling water.

Typical serving

1 cup juice or ½ cup dried (about 240 g).

When to eat it

An evening snack about an hour before bed may suit it best.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People working on restful sleep
  • Anyone eating to support joint comfort and calmer inflammation

Possible downsides

  • Juice is high in natural sugar — choose unsweetened and watch portions if you have diabetes.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
