Kumquat

Citrus japonica · Rutaceae (citrus family) · In season: Winter

What it does for you

Kumquats are tiny citrus fruits eaten whole, peel and all. The peel is sweet while the flesh is tart, giving a delightful burst of flavor. Eating the peel provides extra fiber and antioxidant compounds. They are rich in vitamin C. Kumquats are a traditional symbol of good fortune at Lunar New Year.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Flavonoids
  • Calcium

Benefits explained

Immunity (Some research)

Kumquats provide vitamin C and peel flavonoids.

Digestion (Some research)

Eating the whole fruit including peel gives a good dose of fiber.

Coughs and throats (Traditional use)

In Chinese tradition, kumquats steeped with honey soothe sore throats.

How to use it

  • Eat whole after rolling between your fingers to release the oils.
  • Slice into salads.
  • Candy or make marmalade.
  • Steep with honey for a soothing drink.

How much to eat

One serving: 5 kumquats (about 95 g).

Choosing & storing

Choose firm, bright orange fruit. Refrigerate for up to 2 weeks.

Safety & cautions

Generally safe. The seeds are bitter but harmless.
