Ackee

Blighia sapida · Sapindaceae (soapberry family) · In season: Year-round

What it does for you

Ackee is Jamaica's national fruit, famous in the dish ackee and saltfish. When properly ripened and cooked, its creamy arils taste buttery and mild. It provides healthy fats, protein and fiber. However, unripe ackee contains a dangerous toxin. It must only be eaten when it opens naturally on the tree.

Key nutrients

  • Healthy fats
  • Protein
  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C

Benefits explained

Healthy fats (Traditional use)

Ripe ackee is rich in oleic and linoleic acids.

Protein (Traditional use)

Provides more protein than most fruits.

Cultural staple (Traditional use)

A traditional, nourishing Caribbean food.

How to use it

  • Cook canned ackee with saltfish, onions and peppers.
  • Serve with fried dumplings.
  • Use only canned or properly prepared ackee.
  • Never eat unripe ackee.

How much to eat

One serving: ½ cup cooked (about 100 g).

Choosing & storing

Buy canned ackee from reputable brands.

Safety & cautions

Unripe ackee contains hypoglycin A, which causes 'Jamaican vomiting sickness' and can drop blood sugar fatally. Only eat fruit that has opened naturally, with the seeds and pink membrane removed.
