Raisin

Vitis vinifera (dried)

What it is

Raisin is a fruit from the Vitaceae family (grape family), known botanically as Vitis vinifera (dried). In season: year-round.

Raisins are dried grapes that concentrate sugar, fiber and minerals. They provide quick energy for exercise. They are rich in potassium, iron and polyphenols. Studies suggest raisins may support blood pressure. They are convenient and long-lasting.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Potassium
  • Iron
  • Polyphenols
  • Natural sugar

Potential benefits

Exercise fuel (Some research)

Raisins fueled endurance exercise as well as sports chews in a study.

Blood pressure (Some research)

Eating raisins three times a day lowered blood pressure in a small trial.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Provide fiber.

Best ways to eat it

  • Add to oatmeal.
  • Mix into trail mix.
  • Bake into breads.
  • Add to curries and pilafs.

Typical serving

¼ cup (about 40 g).

When to eat it

Morning, or as a snack before activity, for natural energy.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone wanting steadier energy
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • High in natural sugar; very toxic to dogs.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
