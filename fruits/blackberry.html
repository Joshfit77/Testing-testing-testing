Blackberry

Rubus fruticosus

What it is

Blackberry is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Rubus fruticosus. In season: summer.

Blackberries are juicy, deep purple berries bursting with antioxidants. Their dark color comes from anthocyanins, which help protect cells and blood vessels. They are one of the highest-fiber fruits, supporting digestion and steady blood sugar. Blackberries are also rich in vitamin C and vitamin K. Low in sugar, they're a delicious way to boost nutrition.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin K
  • Manganese
  • Anthocyanins

Potential benefits

Antioxidant protection (Some research)

Anthocyanins and ellagic acid neutralize free radicals and calm inflammation.

Digestion (Some research)

About 8 g of fiber per cup supports regularity and a healthy gut.

Gum and mouth health (Traditional use)

Blackberry leaf tea has long been used as a gargle for mouth sores.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh by the handful.
  • Top yogurt, oatmeal or cereal.
  • Blend into smoothies.
  • Simmer into a quick no-added-sugar compote.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 144 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • Generally very safe. Rinse well and remove any moldy berries.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
