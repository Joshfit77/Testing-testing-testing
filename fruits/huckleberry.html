Huckleberry

Vaccinium membranaceum · Ericaceae (heath family) · In season: Late summer

What it does for you

Huckleberries are wild berries closely related to blueberries, beloved in the mountains of the American West. They have an intense sweet-tart flavor. They are rich in anthocyanins, vitamin C and iron. They cannot be farmed easily, so most are hand-picked. They are prized in jams, pies and pancakes.

Key nutrients

  • Anthocyanins
  • Vitamin C
  • Iron
  • Fiber

Benefits explained

Antioxidants (Some research)

Wild huckleberries are very rich in anthocyanins.

Heart health (Traditional use)

Like blueberries, their polyphenols support blood vessels.

Immunity (Traditional use)

Provide vitamin C.

How to use it

  • Bake into pies and muffins.
  • Make jam.
  • Add to pancakes.
  • Eat fresh if foraged.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 cup (about 140 g).

Choosing & storing

Usually sold frozen or as jam. Refrigerate fresh berries and use quickly.

Safety & cautions

When foraging, be completely sure of identification.
