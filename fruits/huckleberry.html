Huckleberry

Vaccinium membranaceum

What it is

Huckleberry is a fruit from the Ericaceae family (heath family), known botanically as Vaccinium membranaceum. In season: late summer.

Huckleberries are wild berries closely related to blueberries, beloved in the mountains of the American West. They have an intense sweet-tart flavor. They are rich in anthocyanins, vitamin C and iron. They cannot be farmed easily, so most are hand-picked. They are prized in jams, pies and pancakes.

Key nutrients

  • Anthocyanins
  • Vitamin C
  • Iron
  • Fiber

Potential benefits

Antioxidants (Some research)

Wild huckleberries are very rich in anthocyanins.

Heart health (Traditional use)

Like blueberries, their polyphenols support blood vessels.

Immunity (Traditional use)

Provide vitamin C.

Best ways to eat it

  • Bake into pies and muffins.
  • Make jam.
  • Add to pancakes.
  • Eat fresh if foraged.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 140 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • When foraging, be completely sure of identification.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
