Guava

Psidium guajava · Myrtaceae (myrtle family) · In season: Winter

What it does for you

Guava is a tropical fruit with one of the highest vitamin C levels of any fruit. One guava provides more than a day's worth. It is very rich in fiber, supporting digestion and blood sugar. Pink guava contains lycopene for heart health. Guava leaf tea is used in traditional medicine for diarrhea and blood sugar.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C (very high)
  • Fiber
  • Folate
  • Potassium
  • Lycopene (pink)

Benefits explained

Immunity (Some research)

One guava provides about twice the daily requirement of vitamin C.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Guava leaf tea lowered after-meal blood sugar in small studies.

Digestion (Some research)

Guava's fiber supports regularity, and guava leaf extract eased diarrhea in studies.

How to use it

  • Eat whole, skin and seeds included.
  • Blend into juice or smoothies.
  • Make guava paste or jam.
  • Slice with chili and lime.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 guava (about 55 g).

Choosing & storing

Ripe guavas are fragrant and give slightly to pressure. Refrigerate ripe fruit for a few days.

Safety & cautions

Generally very safe. Guava leaf tea may lower blood sugar further in people on diabetes medicine.
