Mangosteen

Garcinia mangostana

What it is

Mangosteen is a fruit from the Clusiaceae family (plant family), known botanically as Garcinia mangostana. In season: summer.

Mangosteen is the 'queen of fruits,' with a purple rind and sweet, tangy white segments. Its rind is rich in xanthones, antioxidants studied for calming inflammation. The flesh provides vitamin C and fiber. Its delicate, peach-and-lychee flavor is prized across Southeast Asia. It is a rare and special treat.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Folate
  • Xanthones (rind)

Potential benefits

Anti-inflammatory (Some research)

Mangosteen xanthones reduce inflammation in laboratory studies and small trials.

Skin (Some research)

A small trial found mangosteen extract improved skin elasticity.

Immunity (Traditional use)

Provides vitamin C.

Best ways to eat it

  • Twist open the rind and eat the white segments.
  • Add to fruit salads.
  • Blend into smoothies.
  • Serve chilled as a dessert.

Typical serving

½ cup segments (about 100 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Concentrated mangosteen supplements may slow blood clotting. Do not eat the rind.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
