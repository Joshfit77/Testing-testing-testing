Amla (Indian Gooseberry)

Phyllanthus emblica

What it is

Amla (Indian Gooseberry) is a fruit from the Phyllanthaceae family (plant family), known botanically as Phyllanthus emblica. In season: winter.

Amla is a sour green fruit central to Ayurveda. It is one of the richest sources of vitamin C. It is the main ingredient in the classic tonic chyawanprash and triphala. Studies suggest amla may help with cholesterol and blood sugar. It is traditionally used for healthy hair and skin.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C (very high)
  • Polyphenols
  • Tannins
  • Fiber

Potential benefits

Cholesterol (Some research)

Amla extract lowered LDL cholesterol in several small trials.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Amla powder lowered blood sugar in small studies.

Hair and skin (Traditional use)

A traditional tonic for strong hair.

Best ways to eat it

  • Take amla powder in water or smoothies.
  • Eat pickled amla.
  • Drink amla juice diluted.
  • Use amla oil for hair.

Typical serving

1 fresh fruit or 1 tsp powder (about 5 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure

Possible downsides

  • Very sour. May slow blood clotting.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
