Olive

Olea europaea · Oleaceae (olive family) · In season: Autumn

What it does for you

Olives are the fruit of the ancient olive tree and the heart of the Mediterranean diet. They are rich in monounsaturated fat and the antioxidant hydroxytyrosol. Extra virgin olive oil is strongly linked to lower heart disease risk. Olives provide vitamin E and iron. They must be cured before eating to remove bitterness.

Key nutrients

  • Monounsaturated fat
  • Vitamin E
  • Hydroxytyrosol
  • Iron

Benefits explained

Heart health (Well researched)

The PREDIMED trial found a Mediterranean diet with extra olive oil reduced heart attacks and strokes by about 30%.

Anti-inflammatory (Some research)

Oleocanthal in olive oil acts on the same enzymes as ibuprofen.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Hydroxytyrosol protects LDL cholesterol from oxidation.

How to use it

  • Snack on a handful of olives.
  • Use extra virgin olive oil for dressings and cooking.
  • Add to salads and pasta.
  • Make tapenade.

How much to eat

One serving: 5–10 olives or 1 tbsp oil (about 30 g).

Choosing & storing

Store cured olives in their brine in the fridge.

Safety & cautions

Cured olives are high in salt — rinse or choose lower-sodium options if you watch your blood pressure.
