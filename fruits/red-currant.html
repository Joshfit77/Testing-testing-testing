Red Currant

Ribes rubrum

What it is

Red Currant is a fruit from the Grossulariaceae family (currant family), known botanically as Ribes rubrum. In season: summer.

Red currants are glossy, jewel-like berries with a bright, tart flavor. They are rich in vitamin C and fiber. They contain antioxidant anthocyanins and flavonols. They are classic in jellies, sauces and summer puddings. Their beauty makes them a favorite garnish.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Vitamin K
  • Anthocyanins

Potential benefits

Immunity (Some research)

A good source of vitamin C.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Provides fiber.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Contains flavonols like quercetin.

Best ways to eat it

  • Make red currant jelly.
  • Garnish desserts.
  • Add to summer pudding.
  • Sprinkle on yogurt.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 112 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Very tart; generally safe.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
