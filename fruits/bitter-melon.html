Bitter Melon

Momordica charantia

What it is

Bitter Melon is a fruit from the Cucurbitaceae family (gourd family), known botanically as Momordica charantia. In season: summer.

Bitter melon is a warty green fruit used as a vegetable across Asia. It is one of the bitterest foods eaten. It contains charantin and polypeptide-p, compounds studied for lowering blood sugar. It provides vitamin C and folate. It is traditionally used to support digestion and blood sugar.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Folate
  • Charantin
  • Fiber

Potential benefits

Blood sugar (Some research)

Bitter melon lowered blood sugar modestly in some studies, though results are mixed.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Bitter foods stimulate digestive juices.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Contains polyphenols.

Best ways to eat it

  • Stir-fry with garlic and egg.
  • Stuff and steam.
  • Add to curries.
  • Salt slices first to reduce bitterness.

Typical serving

½ cup cooked (about 60 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • Can lower blood sugar further with diabetes medicine. Avoid during pregnancy. Seeds can be toxic to children.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

Pregnant or trying to conceive: Avoid during pregnancy.
