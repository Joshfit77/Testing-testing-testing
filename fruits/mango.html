Mango

Mangifera indica

What it is

Mango is a fruit from the Anacardiaceae family (cashew family), known botanically as Mangifera indica. In season: spring to summer.

Mango is the beloved king of fruits in India, sweet, juicy and fragrant. It is rich in vitamin C and vitamin A for immunity, skin and eyes. Mangoes contain mangiferin, a unique antioxidant. A small trial found mango improved constipation more than an equal amount of fiber supplement. It is perfect fresh, in smoothies or in salsas.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin A
  • Folate
  • Fiber
  • Mangiferin

Potential benefits

Digestion (Some research)

In a 4-week trial, eating mango daily improved chronic constipation better than a fiber supplement.

Skin (Some research)

Older women who ate mango regularly had fewer facial wrinkles in a small study.

Immunity (Some research)

One cup provides most of a day's vitamin C.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh slices or cubes.
  • Blend into lassi or smoothies.
  • Make mango salsa with lime and chili.
  • Freeze cubes for sorbet.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 165 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • Mango skin contains urushiol (like poison ivy) and can cause a rash in sensitive people. Peel carefully.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
