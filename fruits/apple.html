Apple

Malus domestica

What it is

Apple is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Malus domestica. In season: late summer to winter.

Apples are one of the world's most loved fruits and a simple daily habit for good health. Their soluble fiber, pectin, feeds healthy gut bacteria and helps lower LDL cholesterol. The skin is packed with quercetin and other polyphenols that protect the heart and blood vessels. People who eat apples regularly tend to have a lower risk of heart disease and type 2 diabetes. Crisp, portable and filling, an apple is one of the easiest healthy snacks there is.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber (pectin)
  • Vitamin C
  • Quercetin
  • Potassium

Potential benefits

Heart health (Some research)

Pectin binds cholesterol in the gut, and apple polyphenols help blood vessels relax. Studies found eating two apples a day modestly lowered cholesterol.

Gut health (Some research)

Apple pectin is a prebiotic that feeds beneficial bacteria, which produce compounds that nourish the gut lining.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Apple fiber and polyphenols slow the absorption of sugar, and regular apple eaters have a lower risk of type 2 diabetes in large studies.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat whole with the skin for the most fiber and antioxidants.
  • Slice with peanut butter or cheese for a balanced snack.
  • Bake with cinnamon for a warm, naturally sweet dessert.
  • Grate into oatmeal, salads or slaws.

Typical serving

1 medium apple (about 180 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • Apple seeds contain amygdalin, which releases cyanide when crushed — avoid chewing large amounts of seeds. Some people with birch pollen allergy feel itching in the mouth from raw apples.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
