Avocado

Persea americana

What it is

Avocado is a fruit from the Lauraceae family (laurel family), known botanically as Persea americana. In season: year-round.

Avocado is a creamy fruit packed with heart-healthy monounsaturated fats. These fats help lower LDL cholesterol and help the body absorb fat-soluble vitamins from other foods. Avocados are rich in fiber, potassium and folate. Their lutein supports eye health, and their healthy fats nourish the skin. Avocado keeps you full and satisfied, making it a smart addition to meals.

Key nutrients

  • Monounsaturated fat (oleic acid)
  • Fiber
  • Potassium
  • Folate
  • Vitamin K

Potential benefits

Heart health (Well researched)

Replacing saturated fats with avocado's oleic acid lowers LDL cholesterol, as shown in controlled feeding studies.

Better nutrient absorption (Some research)

Adding avocado to a salad increased absorption of carotenoids from the vegetables several times over.

Fullness (Some research)

Its fat and fiber slow digestion, helping you feel satisfied longer after meals.

Best ways to eat it

  • Mash on toast with lemon and salt.
  • Slice into salads and grain bowls.
  • Blend into smoothies for creaminess.
  • Make guacamole with lime, onion and cilantro.

Typical serving

⅓ medium avocado (about 50 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone adding healthy fats to their plate

Possible downsides

  • Avocados are high in calories, so watch portions if managing weight. People with latex allergy may react to avocado.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
