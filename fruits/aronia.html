Aronia (Chokeberry)

Aronia melanocarpa

What it is

Aronia (Chokeberry) is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Aronia melanocarpa. In season: late summer.

Aronia berries are dark purple North American berries with one of the highest antioxidant levels of any fruit. They are very rich in anthocyanins and procyanidins. Studies suggest aronia may support healthy blood pressure and cholesterol. Their strong, astringent taste makes them better in juices and baking than eaten raw. They are also known as chokeberries.

Key nutrients

  • Anthocyanins
  • Procyanidins
  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Vitamin K

Potential benefits

Blood pressure (Some research)

Aronia juice lowered blood pressure in small trials.

Cholesterol (Some research)

Aronia extract improved cholesterol in people with metabolic syndrome.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Aronia has among the highest antioxidant capacity of berries tested.

Best ways to eat it

  • Add to smoothies with sweeter fruit.
  • Bake into muffins.
  • Drink diluted juice.
  • Make jam.

Typical serving

¼ cup or 100 ml juice (about 30 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Very astringent. Generally safe.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
