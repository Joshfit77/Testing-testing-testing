Prickly Pear

Opuntia ficus-indica

What it is

Prickly Pear is a fruit from the Cactaceae family (cactus family), known botanically as Opuntia ficus-indica. In season: late summer.

Prickly pear, or tuna, is the sweet fruit of the nopal cactus. It is rich in fiber, magnesium and betalain antioxidants. Studies suggest it may help lower blood sugar. Prickly pear extract has been studied for reducing hangover symptoms. It has a melon-like flavor.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Magnesium
  • Vitamin C
  • Betalains

Potential benefits

Blood sugar (Some research)

Nopal cactus lowered blood sugar spikes after meals in small studies.

Hangovers (Some research)

A prickly pear extract reduced hangover nausea and dry mouth in one trial.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Rich in betalains.

Best ways to eat it

  • Peel and eat chilled.
  • Blend into agua fresca.
  • Make syrup or jam.
  • Add to salads.

Typical serving

1 fruit (about 100 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • People supporting digestion and regularity

Possible downsides

  • Remove all spines. Seeds are hard; large amounts may cause constipation.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
