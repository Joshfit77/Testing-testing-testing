Pumpkin

Cucurbita pepo

What it is

Pumpkin is a fruit from the Cucurbitaceae family (gourd family), known botanically as Cucurbita pepo. In season: autumn.

Pumpkin is botanically a fruit, rich in beta-carotene that turns flesh bright orange. It supports eyes, skin and immunity. It is high in fiber and low in calories. Pumpkin seeds are rich in zinc and magnesium, important for men's health and sleep. It's comforting in soups and baking.

Key nutrients

  • Beta-carotene (vitamin A)
  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Potassium
  • Zinc (seeds)

Potential benefits

Eye health (Some research)

Beta-carotene, lutein and zeaxanthin protect the eyes.

Prostate health (Some research)

Pumpkin seed oil improved urinary symptoms in men in some trials.

Immunity (Some research)

Vitamin A supports the immune barrier.

Best ways to eat it

  • Roast cubes with olive oil.
  • Make pumpkin soup.
  • Add purée to oatmeal or smoothies.
  • Roast the seeds.

Typical serving

1 cup cooked (about 245 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Pumpkin pie and lattes are high in sugar — enjoy the real pumpkin.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
