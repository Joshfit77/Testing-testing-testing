Pumpkin

Cucurbita pepo · Cucurbitaceae (gourd family) · In season: Autumn

What it does for you

Pumpkin is botanically a fruit, rich in beta-carotene that turns flesh bright orange. It supports eyes, skin and immunity. It is high in fiber and low in calories. Pumpkin seeds are rich in zinc and magnesium, important for men's health and sleep. It's comforting in soups and baking.

Key nutrients

  • Beta-carotene (vitamin A)
  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Potassium
  • Zinc (seeds)

Benefits explained

Eye health (Some research)

Beta-carotene, lutein and zeaxanthin protect the eyes.

Prostate health (Some research)

Pumpkin seed oil improved urinary symptoms in men in some trials.

Immunity (Some research)

Vitamin A supports the immune barrier.

How to use it

  • Roast cubes with olive oil.
  • Make pumpkin soup.
  • Add purée to oatmeal or smoothies.
  • Roast the seeds.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 cup cooked (about 245 g).

Choosing & storing

Choose heavy, firm pumpkins. Store whole in a cool place for months.

Safety & cautions

Pumpkin pie and lattes are high in sugar — enjoy the real pumpkin.
