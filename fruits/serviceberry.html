Serviceberry (Saskatoon)

Amelanchier alnifolia

What it is

Serviceberry (Saskatoon) is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Amelanchier alnifolia. In season: early summer.

Serviceberries, or Saskatoon berries, are sweet North American berries with a nutty almond flavor. They are rich in fiber, iron and manganese. They are full of anthocyanins. They were a key ingredient in Indigenous pemmican. They are delicious fresh or baked.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Iron
  • Manganese
  • Anthocyanins

Potential benefits

Antioxidants (Some research)

Rich in anthocyanins.

Iron (Traditional use)

Good fruit source of iron.

Digestion (Traditional use)

High fiber.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh.
  • Bake into pies.
  • Make jam.
  • Add to pancakes.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 145 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Seeds contain small amounts of cyanide-forming compounds — eat whole berries normally but don't grind large amounts of seeds.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
