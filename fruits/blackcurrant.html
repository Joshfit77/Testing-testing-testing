Blackcurrant

Ribes nigrum · Grossulariaceae (currant family) · In season: Summer

What it does for you

Blackcurrants are tart little berries with extraordinary amounts of vitamin C. Just a handful provides more than a day's worth. They are rich in anthocyanins that support circulation and eye comfort. Blackcurrant seed oil contains GLA, an anti-inflammatory fat. Their bold flavor shines in jams, cordials and baking.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C (very high)
  • Anthocyanins
  • Fiber
  • Potassium

Benefits explained

Immune support (Some research)

Their exceptional vitamin C content supports immune cell function.

Circulation and eyes (Some research)

Blackcurrant anthocyanins improved blood flow and reduced eye fatigue from screen work in small studies.

Exercise recovery (Some research)

Blackcurrant extract improved recovery and performance in some sports studies.

How to use it

  • Cook into jam or a lightly sweetened compote.
  • Add to smoothies.
  • Bake into crumbles with apples.
  • Make a cordial to mix with sparkling water.

How much to eat

One serving: ½ cup (about 56 g).

Choosing & storing

Choose firm, shiny, dark berries. Refrigerate and use within a few days, or freeze.

Safety & cautions

Very tart raw — usually sweetened. Blackcurrant products may slow blood clotting slightly in large amounts.
