Boysenberry

Rubus ursinus × idaeus

What it is

Boysenberry is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Rubus ursinus × idaeus. In season: summer.

Boysenberries are large, juicy hybrid berries made from blackberries and raspberries. They are rich in anthocyanins, fiber and vitamin C. Studies suggest boysenberry polyphenols may support lung and blood vessel health. They are sweet-tart and delicious in pies and jams. They were made famous by Knott's Berry Farm in California.

Key nutrients

  • Anthocyanins
  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin K

Potential benefits

Antioxidants (Some research)

Very high in anthocyanins.

Blood vessels (Some research)

Boysenberry juice improved blood vessel function in animal studies.

Digestion (Traditional use)

High fiber.

Best ways to eat it

  • Bake into pies.
  • Make jam.
  • Top yogurt.
  • Blend into smoothies.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 132 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Generally safe.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
