Acerola Cherry

Malpighia emarginata

What it is

Acerola Cherry is a fruit from the Malpighiaceae family (plant family), known botanically as Malpighia emarginata. In season: summer.

Acerola, or Barbados cherry, is one of the richest natural sources of vitamin C on Earth. A single small cherry can provide a full day's vitamin C. It is often dried into powder for natural vitamin C supplements. It also contains carotenoids and anthocyanins. Its tart flavor shines in juices and smoothies.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C (extremely high)
  • Vitamin A
  • Anthocyanins
  • Magnesium

Potential benefits

Immunity (Some research)

Acerola's vitamin C supports immune cells and antioxidant defenses.

Skin (Some research)

Vitamin C is essential for collagen production.

Vitamin C absorption (Some research)

Natural acerola vitamin C was absorbed slightly better than synthetic vitamin C in a small study.

Best ways to eat it

  • Blend into juices and smoothies.
  • Stir acerola powder into water or yogurt.
  • Make jam or sorbet.
  • Eat fresh where available.

Typical serving

3–4 cherries or ¼ tsp powder (about 20 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Very high doses of vitamin C can upset the stomach and increase kidney stone risk in prone people.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
