Camu Camu

Myrciaria dubia

What it is

Camu Camu is a fruit from the Myrtaceae family (myrtle family), known botanically as Myrciaria dubia. In season: year-round (powder).

Camu camu is a sour Amazonian berry with one of the highest vitamin C contents of any food. It is usually sold as powder. It is rich in polyphenols and ellagic acid. Small studies suggest it may calm inflammation. It is a natural way to boost vitamin C.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C (extremely high)
  • Polyphenols
  • Ellagic acid

Potential benefits

Inflammation (Some research)

Camu camu juice lowered inflammation markers in smokers in a small study.

Immunity (Some research)

Very high vitamin C.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Rich in polyphenols.

Best ways to eat it

  • Stir ¼–½ tsp powder into smoothies or juice.
  • Add to yogurt.
  • Mix into water with honey.
  • Blend into energy balls.

Typical serving

½ tsp powder (about 2 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Too much may cause stomach upset due to high vitamin C.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
