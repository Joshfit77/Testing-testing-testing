Pomegranate

Punica granatum

What it is

Pomegranate is a fruit from the Lythraceae family (plant family), known botanically as Punica granatum. In season: autumn to winter.

Pomegranate is an ancient fruit filled with jewel-like seeds called arils. It is rich in punicalagins, powerful antioxidants found mostly in the juice. Studies suggest pomegranate juice may lower blood pressure. It provides vitamin C, vitamin K and fiber. Pomegranate has symbolized abundance and fertility for thousands of years.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin K
  • Fiber
  • Punicalagins
  • Folate

Potential benefits

Blood pressure (Some research)

A review found pomegranate juice lowered systolic blood pressure by about 5 mmHg.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Pomegranate juice has some of the highest antioxidant activity of any fruit juice.

Exercise recovery (Some research)

Pomegranate extract reduced muscle soreness in some studies.

Best ways to eat it

  • Sprinkle arils on salads and yogurt.
  • Drink unsweetened juice.
  • Use pomegranate molasses in dressings.
  • Add to grain bowls.

Typical serving

½ cup arils (about 87 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Pomegranate juice may interact with some medicines, like grapefruit — ask your pharmacist if you take blood thinners or statins.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
