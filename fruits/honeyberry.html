Honeyberry (Haskap)

Lonicera caerulea

What it is

Honeyberry (Haskap) is a fruit from the Caprifoliaceae family (honeysuckle family), known botanically as Lonicera caerulea. In season: early summer.

Honeyberries, or haskap, are elongated blue berries from cold northern regions. They taste like a mix of blueberry and raspberry. They are very rich in anthocyanins and vitamin C. They ripen earlier than any other berry. They are popular in Japan and Canada.

Key nutrients

  • Anthocyanins
  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Vitamin A

Potential benefits

Antioxidants (Some research)

Higher anthocyanin levels than blueberries.

Memory (Some research)

Haskap extract improved some memory measures in a small trial.

Immunity (Some research)

Good source of vitamin C.

Best ways to eat it

  • Eat fresh.
  • Bake into muffins.
  • Make jam.
  • Add to smoothies.

Typical serving

1 cup (about 140 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting heart health and blood pressure
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Only eat edible haskap varieties — many other honeysuckle berries are toxic.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
