Cantaloupe

Cucumis melo var. cantalupensis · Cucurbitaceae (gourd family) · In season: Summer

What it does for you

Cantaloupe is a sweet, juicy melon that is about 90% water, making it wonderfully hydrating. Its orange flesh is rich in beta-carotene for healthy eyes, skin and immunity. It also provides vitamin C and potassium. Low in calories and naturally sweet, it is a refreshing summer treat. It makes a perfect breakfast fruit or post-exercise snack.

Key nutrients

  • Beta-carotene (vitamin A)
  • Vitamin C
  • Potassium
  • Water

Benefits explained

Hydration (Some research)

Its high water and potassium content helps replenish fluids and electrolytes.

Eye and skin health (Some research)

Beta-carotene supports vision and protects skin from sun damage.

Immunity (Traditional use)

One cup supplies most of a day's vitamin C.

How to use it

  • Eat chilled slices for breakfast.
  • Wrap with prosciutto or pair with feta and mint.
  • Blend into a cold melon soup or smoothie.
  • Add to fruit salads.

How much to eat

One serving: 1 cup cubes (about 160 g).

Choosing & storing

A ripe cantaloupe smells sweet at the stem end and feels heavy. Store whole at room temperature until ripe, then refrigerate cut melon for 3–5 days.

Safety & cautions

Scrub the rind before cutting — bacteria on the netted rind can be carried into the flesh. Refrigerate cut melon promptly.
