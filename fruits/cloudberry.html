Cloudberry

Rubus chamaemorus

What it is

Cloudberry is a fruit from the Rosaceae family (rose family), known botanically as Rubus chamaemorus. In season: late summer.

Cloudberries are rare amber berries that grow in Arctic bogs. They are prized in Scandinavia and contain about four times more vitamin C than oranges. They are rich in ellagitannins and vitamin E. Their seed oil is used in skin care. They are usually enjoyed as jam with waffles or cheese.

Key nutrients

  • Vitamin C
  • Vitamin E
  • Ellagitannins
  • Omega fatty acids (seeds)

Potential benefits

Immunity (Some research)

Very high in vitamin C.

Skin (Some research)

Cloudberry seed oil is rich in omega-3, omega-6 and vitamin E.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Rich in ellagitannins.

Best ways to eat it

  • Spoon cloudberry jam over waffles or cheese.
  • Top desserts.
  • Eat fresh if foraged.
  • Mix into yogurt.

Typical serving

½ cup (about 70 g).

When to eat it

Any time of day — with breakfast, as a snack or as a naturally sweet dessert.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Generally safe.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
