Dragon Fruit

Selenicereus undatus

What it is

Dragon Fruit is a fruit from the Cactaceae family (cactus family), known botanically as Selenicereus undatus. In season: summer to autumn.

Dragon fruit, or pitaya, is the striking fruit of a climbing cactus. Its mild, sweet flesh is speckled with tiny edible seeds. It provides fiber and prebiotics that feed beneficial gut bacteria. Red-fleshed varieties are rich in betalain antioxidants. It is low in calories and beautiful in smoothie bowls.

Key nutrients

  • Fiber
  • Vitamin C
  • Magnesium
  • Iron
  • Betalains (red types)

Potential benefits

Gut health (Some research)

Dragon fruit oligosaccharides acted as prebiotics in studies, feeding probiotic bacteria.

Antioxidants (Some research)

Red-fleshed pitaya contains betalains, the same antioxidants found in beets.

Iron (Traditional use)

Dragon fruit provides iron along with vitamin C, which helps the body absorb it.

Best ways to eat it

  • Scoop and eat with a spoon.
  • Blend into a bright pink smoothie bowl.
  • Cube into fruit salads.
  • Freeze for sorbet.

Typical serving

1 cup cubes (about 227 g).

When to eat it

With or after meals, or as a between-meal snack.

Who may benefit

  • Anyone working toward more fruits and vegetables each day
  • People supporting digestion and regularity
  • Anyone supporting their immune system, especially in cold season

Possible downsides

  • Red dragon fruit can harmlessly turn urine or stool pink. Rarely causes allergy.

Pregnancy & breastfeeding

As a food, it is generally considered fine during pregnancy and breastfeeding as part of a varied diet. Wash well before eating.
