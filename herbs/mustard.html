Mustard Seed

Brassica juncea · Brassicaceae (cabbage family) · Culinary, Breathing & Throat, Aches & Joints

Benefits at a glance

Mustard seed is a tiny seed with warming power. Mustard plasters were traditionally used to ease chest congestion and sore muscles by increasing blood flow. Its glucosinolates turn into isothiocyanates, compounds studied for protecting cells. It stimulates saliva and digestive juices. It brings bold flavor to cooking while supporting digestion.

Overview

Jesus compared faith to a mustard seed — the smallest seed that grows into a great plant. Mustard has been used since ancient times as a condiment and in mustard plasters, a traditional warming remedy placed on the chest for congestion or on sore muscles.

What it does in your body

  • Skin & circulation: Warms and draws blood to the skin.
  • Digestive system: Stimulates saliva and digestion.
  • Cells: Protective isothiocyanates.

How it works chemically

  • Sinigrin → allyl isothiocyanate (Glucosinolate → isothiocyanate): When mustard is crushed and wet, the enzyme myrosinase turns sinigrin into allyl isothiocyanate, which activates TRPA1 heat receptors (the sharp burn).
  • Sinalbin (white mustard) (Glucosinolate): Gives a milder heat.
  • Omega-3 ALA & selenium (Nutrients): Anti-inflammatory nutrients.

Benefits explained

Warming plasters (Traditional use)

Mustard plasters increase blood flow to the skin, traditionally used to ease chest congestion and muscle aches.

Protective compounds (Some research)

Mustard seeds contain glucosinolates, which are converted into isothiocyanates — compounds studied for protective effects on cells.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Mustard stimulates saliva and digestive juices.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not taken as capsules — use in food.

The herb by itself: 1–5 g (Mustard seed or prepared mustard in food), With meals.

Traditional uses

  • Tempering whole seeds in hot oil for Indian dishes
  • Traditional warming plasters for chest congestion and aches
  • Making homemade mustard

How to prepare

Pop brown mustard seeds in hot oil until they crackle. Grind and mix with vinegar and water for mustard.

Safety & cautions

Mustard plasters can burn the skin — never leave on longer than 15 minutes, and not for children. Mustard is a common allergen.
