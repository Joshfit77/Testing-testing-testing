Elderberry

Sambucus nigra · Viburnaceae (elder family) · Immune Support, Breathing & Throat

Benefits at a glance

Elderberry is a deep purple berry beloved for cold and flu season. Several studies found elderberry extract shortened colds and flu by two to four days when started early. Air travelers who took it had shorter, milder colds. Its anthocyanins are powerful antioxidants that support the immune system. Cooked into syrup with honey and spices, it is a delicious way to stay well.

Overview

Hippocrates called the elder tree his 'medicine chest.' Its dark berries are rich in anthocyanin antioxidants. Several small studies suggest elderberry extracts may shorten the length of colds and flu symptoms.

What it does in your body

  • Immune system: Shortens colds and flu.
  • Respiratory system: Eases cold symptoms.
  • Whole body: Antioxidant.

How it works chemically

  • Anthocyanins (cyanidin glycosides) (Flavonoids): Strong antioxidants that may stop flu viruses from entering cells and stimulate immune messengers.
  • Flavonols (quercetin, rutin) (Flavonoids): Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.
  • Sambunigrin (Cyanogenic glycoside): In raw berries, leaves and stems; can release cyanide — destroyed by cooking.

Benefits explained

Shorter colds and flu (Some research)

Several clinical trials found elderberry extract shortened flu and cold symptoms by two to four days, especially when started within the first 48 hours.

Travel colds (Some research)

In a study of air travelers, those taking elderberry had shorter, milder colds.

Antioxidant (Some research)

Elderberries are packed with anthocyanins, the deep purple pigments that give strong antioxidant activity.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 300 mg (Elderberry extract), 2–3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1 tablespoon (15 ml) of cooked syrup. Homemade syrup — never raw berries.

Traditional uses

  • Homemade elderberry syrup during winter
  • Supporting the immune system when others around you are sick
  • Jams, pies and cordials

How to prepare

Simmer 1 cup of dried berries in 4 cups of water with ginger and cinnamon until reduced by half; strain, cool and stir in honey.

Safety & cautions

Raw or unripe berries, leaves, stems and seeds can cause nausea and vomiting — always cook berries well. Avoid with autoimmune conditions or immune-suppressing medicine. Don't give honey to children under 1.
