Garlic

Allium sativum · Amaryllidaceae (onion family) · Heart & Circulation, Immune Support, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Garlic is one of the best-studied foods for heart health. Its sulfur compounds help blood vessels relax, and aged garlic extract lowered blood pressure in people with hypertension. Garlic modestly improves cholesterol and makes platelets less sticky. Allicin fights germs, and one study found daily garlic users caught fewer colds. Crushing it and letting it rest for ten minutes before cooking boosts these benefits.

Overview

Garlic was fed to the pyramid builders of Egypt and to Greek Olympic athletes. When crushed, it forms allicin, the compound responsible for its smell and much of its studied activity. Research suggests garlic may modestly support healthy blood pressure and cholesterol.

What it does in your body

  • Heart & blood vessels: Relaxes vessels to lower blood pressure; improves cholesterol.
  • Blood: Makes platelets less sticky.
  • Immune system: Antimicrobial and immune supporting.

How it works chemically

  • Allicin (Sulfur compound (thiosulfinate)): Formed when alliin meets the enzyme alliinase as garlic is crushed; antimicrobial and breaks down into other active sulfur compounds.
  • S-allyl cysteine (Sulfur amino acid): The stable active in aged garlic; helps blood vessels make nitric oxide so they relax.
  • Ajoene (Sulfur compound): Stops platelets from clumping, thinning the blood.

Benefits explained

Healthy blood pressure (Well researched)

Allicin and the sulfur compounds it forms help blood vessels relax. Reviews of clinical trials found aged garlic extract lowered systolic blood pressure by around 8 mmHg in people with high blood pressure.

Cholesterol (Some research)

Garlic modestly lowers total and LDL ('bad') cholesterol in several studies, especially in people whose levels are high.

Immune support (Some research)

In one trial, people taking garlic daily caught fewer colds. Allicin is antimicrobial in laboratory studies.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 600–1200 mg (Aged garlic extract), Once a day with food.

The herb by itself: 3–4 g (1 fresh clove, crushed, rested 10 minutes), Once a day.

Traditional uses

  • Supporting heart and circulatory health as part of a balanced diet
  • A traditional ally during cold and flu season
  • Flavoring nearly every savory dish

How to prepare

Crush or chop cloves and let them rest 10 minutes before cooking so allicin can form. Raw garlic is strongest.

Safety & cautions

Large amounts or supplements may increase bleeding risk — stop before surgery and talk to your doctor if you take blood thinners or certain HIV medicines. Raw garlic can upset the stomach.
