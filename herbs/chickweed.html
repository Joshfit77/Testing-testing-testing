Chickweed

Stellaria media · Caryophyllaceae (pink family) · Skin & Hair, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Chickweed is a tender green with starry flowers that soothes the skin. It is a cooling remedy used in salves for itchy rashes, eczema and bites. It is a nutritious wild food rich in vitamin C and minerals. It has been eaten as a spring tonic to support the body's elimination. Its mild flavor makes it a fresh addition to salads.

Overview

Chickweed's name comes from its use as feed for chickens. Its tiny star-like flowers give it the Latin name Stellaria. It's a nutritious wild salad green and a traditional cooling remedy for itchy skin.

What it does in your body

  • Skin: Cools and relieves itching.
  • Whole body: Nutritious green.
  • Urinary system: Gentle diuretic.

How it works chemically

  • Saponins (Glycosides): Soften and soothe skin; mildly diuretic.
  • Apigenin C-glycosides (Flavonoids): Anti-inflammatory.
  • Vitamin C & minerals (Nutrients): Nourishing.

Benefits explained

Itchy skin (Traditional use)

Chickweed is cooling and soothing, used in salves for itchy rashes, eczema and insect bites.

Nutritious green (Traditional use)

Chickweed is rich in vitamin C, minerals and fiber.

Gentle diuretic (Traditional use)

Traditionally eaten as a spring tonic to support the body's elimination.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — eat fresh or use as tea or salve.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried herb steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Cooling salves and poultices for itchy skin and rashes
  • Adding fresh greens to salads and sandwiches
  • A spring tonic green

How to prepare

Eat fresh, tender tops raw. Infuse fresh chickweed in oil to make an itch-relief salve.

Safety & cautions

Generally safe. Learn to identify it carefully — look for a single line of tiny hairs along the stem.
