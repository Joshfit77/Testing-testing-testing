Maca

Lepidium meyenii · Brassicaceae (cabbage family) · Men's Health, Women's Health, Energy & Focus

Benefits at a glance

Maca is a nourishing Andean root eaten for energy and libido. In randomized trials it improved sexual desire in men after 8–12 weeks. Those same trials found it did not change testosterone or estrogen levels, so it seems to work on desire through other pathways. Small studies also found improved sperm count and movement and better mood. It's a good choice for drive and energy, but not for raising testosterone itself.

Overview

Maca is a radish-like root grown on the high plateaus of the Andes, more than 13,000 feet up, where little else will grow. The Inca valued it as a food for strength and fertility. Today it is usually eaten as a malty powder stirred into smoothies and oatmeal.

What it does in your body

  • Brain: Supports libido and mood.
  • Reproductive system: Supports sperm health.
  • Energy: A nourishing source of carbohydrates, protein and minerals.

How it works chemically

  • Macamides (Fatty acid amides): Unique to maca; may act on the body's endocannabinoid system, influencing mood and desire.
  • Glucosinolates (Sulfur compounds): Break down into isothiocyanates — and are why raw maca can affect the thyroid.
  • Macaenes (Polyunsaturated fatty acids): May contribute to the effect on energy and libido.

Benefits explained

Libido (Some research)

Randomized trials found 1.5–3 g of maca a day improved sexual desire in healthy men after 8–12 weeks, with similar benefits reported in women.

Testosterone — no change (Some research)

In the same trials, maca did not change blood testosterone, estrogen or other reproductive hormones — its effect on desire seems to work independently of hormones.

Sperm quality (Some research)

Small studies found improved semen volume, sperm count and motility after 3–4 months.

Mood and energy (Some research)

Small trials found maca eased anxiety and low mood and improved energy, especially in women after menopause.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 1500–3000 mg (Gelatinized maca root), Once a day (usually 3–6 capsules).

The herb by itself: 3–5 g (Gelatinized powder in smoothies, oatmeal or coffee), Once a day.

Traditional uses

  • Supporting libido in men and women
  • A nourishing food for energy and stamina
  • Traditional support for fertility

How to prepare

Stir gelatinized (cooked) maca powder into smoothies, oatmeal or coffee. Gelatinized maca is easier to digest than raw.

Safety & cautions

Generally safe as a food. Raw maca can upset the stomach and contains goitrogens — use cooked (gelatinized) maca if you have thyroid problems. Avoid concentrated supplements in pregnancy, breastfeeding and hormone-sensitive conditions.
