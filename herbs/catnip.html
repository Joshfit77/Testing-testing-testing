Catnip

Nepeta cataria · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Calm & Mood, Digestion, Restful Sleep

Benefits at a glance

Catnip excites cats but has a calming effect on people. Its nepetalactone gently relaxes and is a traditional remedy for restlessness. It soothes gas, colic and an upset stomach. In lab tests, nepetalactone repelled mosquitoes as well as DEET. It makes a mild, pleasant tea for winding down in the evening.

Overview

While catnip sends many cats into a frenzy, it has the opposite effect in people. It was a common English tea before Chinese tea became popular, and is a gentle traditional remedy for restlessness, upset stomach and colic.

What it does in your body

  • Nervous system: Gentle relaxation.
  • Digestive system: Eases gas and colic.
  • Skin (outdoors): Repels mosquitoes.

How it works chemically

  • Nepetalactone (Iridoid): Mildly sedative in humans (stimulating in cats) and repels insects.
  • β-Caryophyllene (Sesquiterpene): Anti-inflammatory via CB2 receptors.
  • Rosmarinic acid (Polyphenol): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Gentle calm (Traditional use)

Nepetalactone, the compound that excites cats, has mild calming effects in people. Catnip tea is a traditional remedy for restlessness, including in children.

Digestive comfort (Traditional use)

Used for colic, gas and an upset stomach.

Insect repellent (Some research)

In laboratory tests, nepetalactone repelled mosquitoes as effectively as or better than DEET.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 400–500 mg (Powdered catnip herb), 1–3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A gentle relaxing tea before bed
  • Easing gas and indigestion
  • A natural mosquito-repelling garden plant

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons dried leaf for 10 minutes; cover the cup to keep aromatics in.

Safety & cautions

Avoid during pregnancy. Can cause drowsiness in large amounts.
