Fenugreek

Trigonella foenum-graecum · Fabaceae (pea family) · Digestion, Women's Health, Men's Health, Culinary, Heart & Circulation

Benefits at a glance

Fenugreek is a maple-scented seed that supports healthy blood sugar. Its soluble fiber slows the absorption of sugar, and studies found it lowered blood sugar in type 2 diabetes. It is one of the most widely used herbs for supporting breast milk supply. Some studies suggest fenugreek extracts may support libido and testosterone in men. Soaked seeds also soothe the stomach and ease constipation.

Overview

Fenugreek is one of the oldest cultivated plants, used in ancient Egypt and throughout India, where both the seeds (methi) and leaves are common in cooking. It is a widely used traditional herb for supporting breast milk supply, and has been studied for blood sugar support.

What it does in your body

  • Metabolism: Slows sugar absorption and lowers blood sugar.
  • Breast tissue: Traditionally increases milk supply.
  • Digestive system: Soothes and softens stools.

How it works chemically

  • Galactomannan (Soluble fiber): Forms a gel in the gut that slows the absorption of sugar and fat.
  • 4-Hydroxyisoleucine (Amino acid): Stimulates the pancreas to release insulin when blood sugar is high.
  • Diosgenin (Steroidal saponin): A plant steroid with hormone-like effects; may explain the effect on milk supply.
  • Sotolon (Lactone): Gives the maple-syrup smell to sweat and urine.

Benefits explained

Blood sugar balance (Some research)

Fenugreek's soluble fiber (galactomannan) slows the absorption of carbohydrates. Several studies found it lowered blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes.

Breast milk supply (Some research)

Some small studies suggest fenugreek may increase breast milk production, though results are mixed.

Soothing the gut (Traditional use)

Soaked fenugreek seeds become gel-like and have long been used to soothe the stomach and ease constipation.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 1000–2000 mg (Powdered fenugreek seed (usually 2–3 capsules of 500–610 mg)), 3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Seed powder or soaked seeds), 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditionally used to support breast milk supply
  • Soothing digestion when soaked seeds are eaten
  • Flavoring curries, dals, pickles and flatbreads

How to prepare

Soak 1 teaspoon of seeds overnight and drink the water or eat the softened seeds. Toast lightly before grinding — burnt seeds turn bitter.

Safety & cautions

Avoid during pregnancy (it may stimulate contractions). May cause allergy in people allergic to peanuts or chickpeas. It can lower blood sugar and make sweat and urine smell like maple syrup.
