Echinacea

Echinacea purpurea · Asteraceae (daisy family) · Immune Support, Breathing & Throat

Benefits at a glance

Echinacea is a popular herb for supporting the immune system during cold season. It activates immune cells, and reviews suggest it may slightly reduce the chance of catching a cold. Taken at the first sign of illness, it may shorten colds a little. An echinacea and sage spray eased sore throats as well as a standard spray. It is best used for short periods when you need extra support.

Overview

Echinacea was the most widely used medicinal plant among the Native American peoples of the Great Plains. Today it's one of the best-selling herbal supplements. Research is mixed, but some studies suggest it may slightly reduce the risk or length of colds when taken at the first sign.

What it does in your body

  • Immune system: Activates immune cells at the start of a cold.
  • Respiratory system: May shorten colds.
  • Throat: Soothes sore throats.

How it works chemically

  • Alkamides (Fatty acid amides): Bind CB2 receptors on immune cells and adjust inflammatory messengers (cytokines); cause the tongue tingle.
  • Cichoric acid (Caffeic acid derivative): Antioxidant and supports immune cell activity.
  • Polysaccharides (Complex sugars): Activate macrophages, immune cells that swallow germs.

Benefits explained

Fewer and shorter colds (Some research)

Reviews of clinical trials suggest echinacea may modestly reduce the chance of catching a cold and may shorten colds slightly when started early. Results vary by product.

Activating immune cells (Some research)

Alkamides and polysaccharides in echinacea stimulate immune cells in laboratory studies.

Sore throats (Some research)

In one trial, an echinacea-and-sage throat spray worked about as well as a standard medicated spray.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 300–500 mg (Echinacea extract), 3 times a day, up to 10 days.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried herb or root steeped as tea (per cup)), 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Taking at the first sign of a cold
  • Gargling for sore throats
  • A beautiful, pollinator-friendly garden flower

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons of dried root or flowers in hot water for 10–15 minutes. A good tincture causes a tingling on the tongue.

Safety & cautions

Avoid if allergic to daisy-family plants. Talk to your doctor if you have an autoimmune condition or take immune-suppressing medicine. Best used short-term.
