Tea Tree

Melaleuca alternifolia · Myrtaceae (myrtle family) · Skin & Hair

Benefits at a glance

Tea tree oil is a powerful natural antiseptic for the skin. A 5% tea tree gel reduced acne in clinical trials with less dryness than benzoyl peroxide. It helped clear athlete's foot and improved nail fungus at higher strengths. Its terpinen-4-ol kills many bacteria and fungi. It must always be diluted and never swallowed.

Overview

Tea tree oil is distilled from the leaves of an Australian tree. Studies suggest diluted tea tree oil gels can help with mild acne, and it's widely used for its antiseptic properties on minor cuts and fungal infections like athlete's foot.

What it does in your body

  • Skin: May help reduce acne.
  • Skin & nails: Has antifungal activity.
  • Skin: Antiseptic for minor cuts.

How it works chemically

  • Terpinen-4-ol (Monoterpene alcohol): Damages the membranes of bacteria and fungi and reduces histamine-driven skin inflammation.
  • γ-Terpinene & α-terpinene (Monoterpenes): Support the antimicrobial action.
  • 1,8-Cineole (Monoterpene oxide): Can irritate skin; good oils keep it low.

Benefits explained

Acne (Some research)

In clinical trials, 5% tea tree oil gel reduced acne lesions compared with placebo. It works more slowly than benzoyl peroxide but with less dryness.

Athlete's foot and nails (Some research)

Tea tree oil reduced symptoms of athlete's foot in trials, and higher strengths improved nail fungus.

Antiseptic (Some research)

Terpinen-4-ol, its main compound, kills many bacteria and fungi.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Never swallow tea tree — it is for use on the skin only.

For use on the skin only — do not swallow.

Traditional uses

  • A dab of diluted oil on blemishes
  • Supporting clear feet and nails in athlete's foot
  • Adding to natural household cleaners

How to prepare

Dilute to about 5% — roughly 1 drop in a teaspoon of carrier oil or aloe gel — before applying to skin.

Safety & cautions

For external use only — toxic if swallowed. Always dilute; it can cause skin irritation and allergy, especially as old oil oxidizes. Highly toxic to cats and dogs.
