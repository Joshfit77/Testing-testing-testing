Moringa

Moringa oleifera · Moringaceae · Energy & Focus, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Moringa is called the miracle tree because its leaves are so nutritious. They contain protein, iron, calcium, and vitamins A and C, and are used to fight malnutrition. Small studies found moringa lowered blood sugar after meals. Its quercetin and chlorogenic acid act as antioxidants and calm inflammation. A spoonful of leaf powder easily boosts smoothies and soups.

Overview

Moringa grows quickly in hot, dry places across Africa and Asia, and its leaves are used to fight malnutrition because they are rich in protein, iron, calcium and vitamins. It's eaten as a vegetable in India and the Philippines.

What it does in your body

  • Whole body: Supplies protein, iron, calcium and vitamins.
  • Metabolism: May lower blood sugar after meals.
  • Whole body: Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant.

How it works chemically

  • Moringin (glucomoringin isothiocyanate) (Isothiocyanate): Switches on Nrf2 antioxidant defenses and calms inflammation.
  • Quercetin & chlorogenic acid (Polyphenols): Antioxidant; chlorogenic acid slows sugar absorption.
  • Protein, iron, calcium, vitamins A & C (Nutrients): Nourish the body, which is why it fights malnutrition.

Benefits explained

Nutrition (Some research)

Moringa leaves contain protein with all essential amino acids, plus iron, calcium, and vitamins A and C — which is why they are used to fight malnutrition.

Blood sugar balance (Some research)

Small studies found moringa leaf powder lowered blood sugar after meals.

Antioxidant (Some research)

Moringa is rich in quercetin and chlorogenic acid, which protect cells.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 800–2000 mg (Moringa leaf powder (usually 2–4 capsules)), 1–2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–3 g (Leaf powder in food or smoothies), 1–2 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Adding leaf powder to smoothies for extra nutrients
  • Supporting energy as part of a balanced diet
  • Cooking fresh leaves and pods in soups and curries

How to prepare

Stir 1 teaspoon of leaf powder into smoothies, soups or yogurt. Start small; the taste is strong.

Safety & cautions

The leaves are considered safe as food. Avoid the root and bark, especially during pregnancy. May lower blood sugar and blood pressure and interact with thyroid medicine.
