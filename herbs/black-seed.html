Black Seed

Nigella sativa · Ranunculaceae (buttercup family) · Immune Support, Culinary, Breathing & Throat

Benefits at a glance

Black seed is a tiny seed treasured in Middle Eastern tradition. Its thymoquinone calms inflammation and the release of histamine. Trials found black seed oil improved allergy symptoms and asthma control. Reviews found it modestly lowered blood sugar and cholesterol. It is a powerful antioxidant that adds a peppery, smoky flavor to bread.

Overview

Black seed (also called black cumin or kalonji) was found in Tutankhamun's tomb and is highly valued in Middle Eastern and South Asian traditions. It contains thymoquinone and has been studied for allergies, asthma, blood sugar and cholesterol.

What it does in your body

  • Immune system: Calms allergies and asthma.
  • Metabolism: Modestly lowers blood sugar and cholesterol.
  • Whole body: Antioxidant.

How it works chemically

  • Thymoquinone (Quinone): Blocks NF-κB and 5-lipoxygenase and reduces histamine release, calming allergic inflammation.
  • Nigellone (Dimer of thymoquinone): Relaxes airway muscle.
  • Linoleic acid (Omega-6 fatty acid): Main fat in the oil.

Benefits explained

Allergies and asthma (Some research)

Thymoquinone reduces inflammation. Trials found black seed oil improved allergic rhinitis symptoms and asthma control.

Blood sugar and cholesterol (Some research)

Reviews of studies found black seed modestly lowered blood sugar and cholesterol.

Antioxidant (Some research)

Thymoquinone is a powerful antioxidant and anti-inflammatory compound.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1500 mg (Black seed oil softgels), 1–2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Ground seeds in food), 1–2 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional support for wellness and immunity
  • Seasonal support during allergy season
  • Topping naan, breads and pickles

How to prepare

Toast seeds lightly and sprinkle on bread, salads and vegetables, or take 1/2 teaspoon of oil with honey.

Safety & cautions

Food amounts are safe. Avoid large amounts in pregnancy. May lower blood pressure and blood sugar and slow clotting. The oil can irritate skin.
