Sage

Salvia officinalis · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Breathing & Throat, Women's Health, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Sage is a traditional herb for the throat, the mind and women's health. A sage gargle or spray eases sore-throat pain and fights germs in the mouth. Small studies found sage extract reduced hot flushes and night sweats during menopause. Early research suggests it may also support memory and alertness. Its astringent tannins help reduce excessive sweating.

Overview

Its Latin name Salvia comes from salvere, 'to be healthy.' A medieval saying asked, 'Why should a man die whilst sage grows in his garden?' Sage gargles are a long-standing European throat remedy, and sage has been studied for reducing hot flushes and night sweats during menopause.

What it does in your body

  • Hormonal / nervous system: Reduces hot flushes and sweating.
  • Mouth & throat: Soothes and disinfects sore throats.
  • Brain: May support memory and attention.

How it works chemically

  • Rosmarinic acid & carnosol (Polyphenol / diterpene): Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and inhibit acetylcholinesterase (supporting memory signaling).
  • Tannins (Polyphenols): Astringent: tighten tissue and reduce secretions, including sweat.
  • Thujone (Monoterpene ketone): Blocks GABA-A receptors — harmless in food amounts, but too much can cause seizures.

Benefits explained

Hot flushes and sweating (Some research)

In small studies, a fresh sage extract reduced the number and intensity of menopausal hot flushes over eight weeks. Sage has long been used for excessive sweating in general.

Sore throats (Some research)

A sage throat spray reduced sore-throat pain in clinical trials. Its tannins tighten and soothe tissue, and its oils are antimicrobial.

Memory and mood (Some research)

Early trials suggest sage may improve memory and alertness in healthy adults, possibly by protecting acetylcholine, a brain messenger important for learning.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 280–600 mg (Sage leaf extract), 1–2 times a day (max 4 weeks).

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A warm gargle for sore throats and mouth irritation
  • Traditional support for night sweats during menopause
  • Flavoring stuffing, beans, butter sauces and squash

How to prepare

For a gargle, steep 1 teaspoon dried sage in a cup of hot water for 10 minutes, cool and gargle several times a day. Fry fresh leaves in butter for pasta.

Safety & cautions

Contains thujone, which can be harmful in large amounts. Avoid medicinal doses for long periods, during pregnancy, while breastfeeding (it may reduce milk), or if you have a seizure disorder.
