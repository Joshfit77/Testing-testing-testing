Red Raspberry Leaf

Rubus idaeus · Rosaceae (rose family) · Women's Health

Benefits at a glance

Red raspberry leaf is a traditional tonic for women's health. It is believed to tone the muscles of the uterus and pelvis. It has long been used to ease menstrual cramps. Some midwives recommend it in late pregnancy, and one study suggested a slightly shorter labor. It tastes like a mild black tea and is rich in minerals.

Overview

Raspberry leaf tea has been passed between midwives and mothers for generations as a uterine tonic, especially in late pregnancy. It tastes like a mild black tea and is rich in tannins and minerals. Research is limited but generally reassuring.

What it does in your body

  • Uterus: Traditionally tones the uterine muscle.
  • Reproductive system: Eases menstrual cramps.
  • Digestive system: Astringent; settles loose stools.

How it works chemically

  • Ellagitannins (Tannins): Astringent — tighten tissue and reduce secretions.
  • Fragarine (Alkaloid (proposed)): Thought to tone pelvic smooth muscle, though not well studied.
  • Flavonoids (kaempferol, quercetin) (Polyphenols): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Toning the uterus (Traditional use)

Raspberry leaf contains fragarine, believed to tone the muscles of the pelvic area. It's a traditional women's tonic.

Preparing for labor (Some research)

One study suggested a slightly shorter pushing stage of labor and no harmful effects, but evidence is limited. Only use in pregnancy with your midwife's guidance.

Period cramps (Traditional use)

Used traditionally to ease menstrual cramps.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 400–1200 mg (Raspberry leaf (in pregnancy only with your midwife)), 2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1.5–2.5 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional support for menstrual comfort
  • Used by some midwives in late pregnancy
  • A pleasant caffeine-free alternative to black tea

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons dried leaf for 10 minutes. Blends well with nettle and peppermint.

Safety & cautions

Only use during pregnancy with your midwife's or doctor's guidance — usually not before the third trimester. May affect blood sugar.
