Mullein

Verbascum thapsus · Scrophulariaceae (figwort family) · Breathing & Throat

Benefits at a glance

Mullein is a soft, fuzzy herb traditionally used for the lungs. Its mucilage soothes the throat, and its saponins help loosen mucus. It is a classic remedy for dry, irritating coughs and chest colds. Herbal ear drops containing mullein eased ear pain in children in one study. Its tea should always be strained well to remove tiny hairs.

Overview

Mullein's tall flower spikes were once dipped in tallow and used as torches. Its velvety leaves have been used in Europe and North America for centuries for coughs and chest congestion, and mullein flower oil is a traditional ear remedy.

What it does in your body

  • Respiratory system: Soothes coughs and loosens mucus.
  • Ears: Eases ear pain (oil drops, intact eardrum only).
  • Throat: Soothing.

How it works chemically

  • Mucilage (Polysaccharides): Coats and soothes the throat.
  • Saponins (Glycosides): Loosen mucus by mildly irritating the stomach, which triggers a reflex that thins airway secretions.
  • Verbascoside & aucubin (Phenylpropanoid / iridoid): Anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial.

Benefits explained

Dry coughs (Traditional use)

Mullein's mucilage soothes, and its saponins help loosen mucus, making it a classic lung herb.

Ear pain (Some research)

In a study of children with ear pain, herbal ear drops containing mullein relieved pain as well as anesthetic ear drops (only use if the eardrum is intact).

Respiratory support (Traditional use)

Traditionally used for bronchitis and chest colds.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the leaf as tea.

The herb by itself: 1.5–2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea, strained through cloth (per cup)), 3–4 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A warm tea for dry, irritating coughs
  • Supporting clear breathing during chest colds
  • Traditional mullein flower oil for earaches

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons dried leaf in hot water for 10–15 minutes, then strain through a coffee filter or fine cloth to remove the tiny hairs.

Safety & cautions

Always strain the tea well — the fine hairs can irritate the throat. Do not put anything in the ear if the eardrum may be damaged; see a doctor for ear pain.
