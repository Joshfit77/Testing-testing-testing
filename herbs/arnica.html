Arnica

Arnica montana · Asteraceae (daisy family) · Aches & Joints, Skin & Hair

Benefits at a glance

Arnica is a mountain flower used on the skin for bumps and bruises. Studies found topical arnica reduced bruising and swelling after surgery. In a trial, arnica gel eased hand arthritis pain as well as ibuprofen gel. It soothes sore muscles and sprains after activity. It must only be used on unbroken skin and never swallowed.

Overview

Arnica grows in the mountains of Europe, where it has been used for centuries on bruises and sprains. Arnica gels and creams are popular among athletes; some studies suggest topical arnica may help with bruising and joint discomfort.

What it does in your body

  • Skin & tissue: Reduces bruising and swelling.
  • Joints: Eases arthritis pain.
  • Muscles: Soothes soreness.

How it works chemically

  • Helenalin & its esters (Sesquiterpene lactones): Block NF-κB to reduce swelling and pain — but are toxic if swallowed.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Anti-inflammatory.
  • Thymol derivatives (Phenolic terpenes): Mildly antiseptic.

Benefits explained

Bruises (Some research)

Some studies found topical arnica reduced bruising and swelling, for example after cosmetic surgery.

Arthritis pain (Some research)

In a trial of people with hand arthritis, arnica gel relieved pain about as well as ibuprofen gel.

Sore muscles (Traditional use)

Arnica has long been rubbed on aching muscles and sprains.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Never swallow arnica — it is poisonous by mouth (except highly diluted homeopathic pills).

For use on the skin only — do not swallow.

Traditional uses

  • Gels or creams on bruises and minor bumps
  • Easing sore muscles after exercise
  • Supporting comfort in stiff joints

How to prepare

Apply arnica cream or gel gently to unbroken skin up to a few times daily.

Safety & cautions

External use only on unbroken skin — arnica is poisonous if swallowed (except in highly diluted homeopathic forms). Stop if a rash develops. Avoid with daisy-family allergies.
