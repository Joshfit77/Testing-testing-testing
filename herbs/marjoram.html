Sweet Marjoram

Origanum majorana · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Culinary, Digestion, Calm & Mood

Benefits at a glance

Sweet marjoram is a gentle cousin of oregano that supports digestion. Its oils relieve gas and help the body digest food. A small trial in women with PCOS found marjoram tea improved insulin sensitivity. It is a traditional calming tea for nervous tension and headaches. It adds sweet, floral flavor to many dishes.

Overview

Greek and Roman couples were crowned with marjoram as a symbol of love and happiness, and the goddess Aphrodite was said to have grown it. In the kitchen it's softer and sweeter than oregano and is a key part of herbes de Provence and za'atar blends.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Relieves gas and aids digestion.
  • Hormonal system: May improve insulin sensitivity in PCOS.
  • Nervous system: Calming.

How it works chemically

  • Terpinen-4-ol & sabinene hydrate (Monoterpenes): Antispasmodic and antimicrobial.
  • Arbutin (Phenolic glycoside): Found in marjoram; may affect hormone balance.
  • Rosmarinic acid (Polyphenol): Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.

Benefits explained

Digestion (Traditional use)

Marjoram's aromatic oils relieve gas and stimulate digestion.

Hormone balance (Some research)

A small trial in women with PCOS found marjoram tea improved insulin sensitivity and lowered certain male hormones.

Calming (Traditional use)

A traditional soothing tea for nervous tension and headaches.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the leaf as tea.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), 2 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Seasoning poultry, sausages, soups and vegetables
  • A soothing tea for digestion
  • A calming tea for nervous tension

How to prepare

Add fresh marjoram near the end of cooking; its flavor is delicate. Steep 1 teaspoon dried for tea.

Safety & cautions

Safe in food amounts. Avoid medicinal amounts during pregnancy.
