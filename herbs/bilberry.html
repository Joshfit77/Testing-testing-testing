Bilberry

Vaccinium myrtillus · Ericaceae (heath family) · Heart & Circulation, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Bilberry is a wild European blueberry packed with antioxidants. Its anthocyanins support the retina and may reduce eye fatigue. It strengthens tiny blood vessels and may ease symptoms of poor vein circulation. Dried bilberries are a traditional remedy for loose stools. Fresh or frozen, they are a delicious way to add antioxidants to your diet.

Overview

Bilberries are smaller and darker than cultivated blueberries, with deep blue flesh all the way through. Legend says British pilots in World War II ate bilberry jam to improve night vision. They're very rich in anthocyanins, studied for eye and blood vessel health.

What it does in your body

  • Eyes: Supports the retina and reduces eye fatigue.
  • Blood vessels: Strengthens capillaries.
  • Digestive system: Dried berries settle loose stools.

How it works chemically

  • Anthocyanosides (delphinidin, cyanidin glycosides) (Anthocyanins): Stabilize collagen in tiny blood-vessel walls and help regenerate rhodopsin, the light-sensing pigment in the eye.
  • Tannins (dried berries) (Polyphenols): Astringent — firm loose stools.
  • Resveratrol & quercetin (Polyphenols): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Eye health (Some research)

Bilberry anthocyanins support the retina and may reduce eye fatigue, though the wartime 'night vision' story was likely a myth.

Circulation (Some research)

Bilberry extracts may strengthen tiny blood vessels and ease symptoms of poor vein circulation.

Antioxidant (Some research)

Bilberries are among the richest fruits in anthocyanin antioxidants.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 80–160 mg (Bilberry extract (25% anthocyanosides)), 2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 20–60 g (Fresh or frozen berries), Once a day.

Traditional uses

  • Antioxidant-rich support for eyes and circulation
  • Traditional dried-berry tea for mild diarrhea
  • Jams, pies and Nordic soups

How to prepare

Eat fresh or frozen berries, or simmer 1 tablespoon dried berries for 10 minutes.

Safety & cautions

The berries are safe as food. Avoid long-term use of bilberry leaf. Large amounts of extract may increase bleeding risk with blood thinners.
