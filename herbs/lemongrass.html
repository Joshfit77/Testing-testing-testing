Lemongrass

Cymbopogon citratus · Poaceae (grass family) · Digestion, Calm & Mood, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Lemongrass is a zesty tropical herb that supports digestion. Its citral oil relaxes the stomach and eases cramps and indigestion. Lemongrass oil fights fungi and bacteria in laboratory studies. In Brazil its tea is a beloved drink for calm and relaxation. It brings bright flavor to soups, curries and refreshing teas.

Overview

Lemongrass is a staple of Thai, Vietnamese and Indonesian cooking. In Brazil, its tea (capim-santo) is a popular calming drink. Its citral oil gives the lemon aroma and is also used to scent soaps and insect repellents.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Calms the stomach and eases cramps.
  • Nervous system: Traditionally calming.
  • Immune system: Antimicrobial and antifungal.

How it works chemically

  • Citral (geranial + neral) (Monoterpene aldehydes): Antispasmodic, antimicrobial and responsible for the lemon aroma.
  • Myrcene (Monoterpene): Mild pain-relieving and relaxing effects in studies.
  • Geraniol (Monoterpene alcohol): Antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory.

Benefits explained

Digestive aid (Traditional use)

Citral, lemongrass's main oil, relaxes the stomach. It is a traditional remedy for indigestion and cramps in Brazil and Southeast Asia.

May help fight germs (Some research)

Lemongrass oil is active against many fungi and bacteria in laboratory studies, which is why it's popular in natural cleaners.

Relaxation (Traditional use)

In Brazilian folk medicine, lemongrass tea (capim-santo) is a beloved calming drink, though human studies are mixed.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the stalk as tea.

The herb by itself: 2–3 g (Fresh or dried stalk steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A refreshing tea to aid digestion after meals
  • A calming evening tea
  • Flavoring soups like tom yum, curries and marinades

How to prepare

Bruise the stalk with the back of a knife and simmer in soups or hot water. Remove woody pieces before eating.

Safety & cautions

Safe in food and tea amounts. Avoid large medicinal amounts during pregnancy. The essential oil can irritate skin.
