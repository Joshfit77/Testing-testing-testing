Ginkgo

Ginkgo biloba · Ginkgoaceae · Energy & Focus, Heart & Circulation

Benefits at a glance

Ginkgo comes from one of the oldest tree species on Earth. Its ginkgolides improve blood flow and make platelets less sticky. It modestly increased walking distance in people with poor leg circulation. Some studies show small benefits for memory in people with dementia. Its flavonoids help protect nerve cells and blood vessels from damage.

Overview

Ginkgo trees have existed almost unchanged for over 200 million years, and some trees in China are more than 1,000 years old. Standardized ginkgo leaf extract is among the most researched herbs for memory and circulation, although large studies have not confirmed it prevents dementia.

What it does in your body

  • Circulation: Improves blood flow, especially to the legs and brain.
  • Blood: Reduces platelet stickiness.
  • Brain: Protects nerve cells.

How it works chemically

  • Ginkgolides A, B, C (Terpene lactones): Block platelet-activating factor (PAF), so blood flows more easily and clots less.
  • Bilobalide (Terpene lactone): Protects nerve cells and supports their energy production.
  • Flavone glycosides (Flavonoids): Antioxidants that protect blood vessels and the brain.

Benefits explained

Circulation (Some research)

Ginkgolides improve blood flow and make platelets less sticky. Studies found ginkgo modestly increased pain-free walking distance in people with poor leg circulation.

Memory (Some research)

Some studies show small benefits for memory and thinking in people with dementia, but large trials found ginkgo did not prevent dementia in healthy older adults.

Protecting nerve cells (Some research)

Ginkgo flavonoids protect nerve cells from oxidative stress in laboratory studies.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 60–120 mg (Standardized ginkgo leaf extract (EGb 761)), 2 times a day.

The herb by itself: Not recommended. Homemade leaf tea is too weak and inconsistent — use the extract.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional support for memory and mental clarity
  • Supporting healthy circulation to the hands and feet
  • A beautiful, pollution-tolerant street tree

How to prepare

Use standardized extracts as directed. Home-made leaf tea is weak and not recommended as a substitute.

Safety & cautions

Increases bleeding risk — avoid with blood thinners, aspirin and before surgery. May interact with antidepressants and seizure medicines. Raw ginkgo seeds are toxic.
