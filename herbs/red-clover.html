Red Clover

Trifolium pratense · Fabaceae (pea family) · Women's Health, Skin & Hair, Breathing & Throat

Benefits at a glance

Red clover is a meadow flower used to support women through menopause. Its isoflavones have mild estrogen-like effects and may slightly reduce hot flushes. Some studies suggest it may help slow bone loss after menopause. It has long been used in traditional remedies for skin conditions and coughs. It makes a mild, sweet tea that is gentle on the body.

Overview

Red clover is a common meadow flower and a favorite of bees. It contains isoflavones, plant compounds with weak estrogen-like activity, and has been studied for easing hot flushes during menopause with mixed results. It was a traditional ingredient in skin and cough remedies.

What it does in your body

  • Hormonal system: Weak estrogen-like support during menopause.
  • Bones: May slow bone loss after menopause.
  • Skin: Traditional skin herb.

How it works chemically

  • Biochanin A & formononetin (Isoflavones): Converted in the body into genistein and daidzein, which bind estrogen receptors (especially ERβ) weakly.
  • Coumarins (Benzopyrones): Can slightly thin the blood.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Menopause support (Some research)

Red clover's isoflavones are plant compounds with weak estrogen-like effects. Some trials found they slightly reduced hot flushes; others found no effect.

Bone health (Some research)

Some studies suggest red clover isoflavones may slow bone loss after menopause.

Skin and coughs (Traditional use)

Traditionally used in remedies for eczema, psoriasis and coughs.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 40–80 mg (Red clover isoflavone extract), Once a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried blossoms steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional support during menopause
  • A gentle tea for skin conditions
  • A soothing traditional cough tea

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons of dried blossoms in hot water for 10–15 minutes.

Safety & cautions

Avoid during pregnancy and breastfeeding, and with hormone-sensitive conditions such as breast cancer or endometriosis. May increase bleeding risk with blood thinners.
