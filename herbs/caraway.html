Caraway

Carum carvi · Apiaceae (carrot family) · Digestion, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Caraway is a well-studied seed for indigestion and bloating. Combined with peppermint oil, it relieved fullness and pain in clinical trials for ongoing indigestion. It relaxes the gut and is a traditional remedy for gas and colic. Caraway oil fights bacteria and fungi in laboratory studies. It adds its distinctive flavor to rye bread and sauerkraut.

Overview

Caraway is one of the oldest cultivated spices in Europe, with seeds found at Stone Age sites. Combined with peppermint oil, it has been studied for relieving indigestion and bloating, and caraway water has long been given for colic.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Relieves indigestion and bloating.
  • Gut: Eases gas and colic.
  • Immune system: Antimicrobial.

How it works chemically

  • Carvone (Monoterpene ketone): Relaxes gut muscle by blocking calcium channels.
  • Limonene (Monoterpene): Supports digestion.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Indigestion (Well researched)

Clinical trials found caraway oil combined with peppermint oil relieved fullness, bloating and pain in functional dyspepsia.

Gas and colic (Traditional use)

Caraway relaxes the digestive tract and is a traditional remedy for gas and colic.

Fights germs (Some research)

Caraway oil shows antibacterial and antifungal activity in laboratory studies.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 140 mg (Caraway oil 50 mg + peppermint oil 90 mg (combined capsule)), 2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Crushed seed steeped as tea (per cup)), 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Easing bloating, gas and indigestion
  • Flavoring rye bread, sauerkraut, cabbage and cheese
  • A digestive tea after heavy meals

How to prepare

Crush 1 teaspoon of seeds and steep in hot water for 10 minutes.

Safety & cautions

Safe in food. May lower blood sugar in large amounts. Do not use caraway essential oil internally without guidance.
