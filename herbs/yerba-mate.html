Yerba Mate

Ilex paraguariensis · Aquifoliaceae (holly family) · Energy & Focus

Benefits at a glance

Yerba mate is a South American tea that gives smooth, steady energy. Its caffeine and theobromine sharpen focus and alertness. Studies found regular mate drinking lowered LDL cholesterol. It is very rich in antioxidant chlorogenic acids. It is best enjoyed warm rather than scalding hot.

Overview

Mate is shared from a hollow gourd through a metal straw (bombilla) in Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay and southern Brazil. It contains caffeine, theobromine and many antioxidants, giving it a reputation for focused, steady energy.

What it does in your body

  • Brain: Energy and focus.
  • Heart & blood: May lower LDL cholesterol.
  • Whole body: Antioxidant.

How it works chemically

  • Caffeine (Methylxanthine alkaloid): Blocks adenosine receptors for alertness (about 70–80 mg per cup).
  • Theobromine (Methylxanthine alkaloid): Gentle stimulant (also in chocolate) that relaxes blood vessels.
  • Chlorogenic acids (Polyphenols): Antioxidants that may improve cholesterol and blood sugar.

Benefits explained

Energy and focus (Some research)

Yerba mate combines caffeine with theobromine (also found in chocolate), giving a steady lift that many describe as smoother than coffee.

Cholesterol (Some research)

Studies found daily mate drinking lowered LDL cholesterol, especially when combined with healthy eating.

Antioxidant (Some research)

Mate is very rich in chlorogenic acid and other polyphenols.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1000 mg (Yerba mate extract (contains caffeine)), 1–2 times a day, before mid-afternoon.

The herb by itself: 3–5 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), 1–3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • An energizing morning drink
  • Supporting focus during work or study
  • A social drink shared among friends

How to prepare

Fill a gourd two-thirds full, moisten with cool water, then add hot (not boiling) water and refill repeatedly. Or steep 1 tablespoon for 5 minutes like tea.

Safety & cautions

Contains caffeine. Regularly drinking mate very hot has been linked to cancers of the esophagus — let it cool a little. Avoid large amounts in pregnancy.
