Cinnamon

Cinnamomum verum · Lauraceae (laurel family) · Culinary, Digestion, Heart & Circulation

Benefits at a glance

Cinnamon is a warm, sweet spice that may help balance blood sugar. It may improve how cells respond to insulin and slow the absorption of sugar after meals. Cinnamon is very rich in antioxidant polyphenols that protect cells. It warms the digestion and eases gas and nausea. Ceylon cinnamon is the best choice for daily use because it is low in coumarin.

Overview

Cinnamon was once more valuable than gold. 'True' Ceylon cinnamon comes from Sri Lanka; most supermarket cinnamon is cassia, which is stronger and contains much more coumarin. Cinnamon has been studied for supporting healthy blood sugar levels.

What it does in your body

  • Metabolism: May improve insulin sensitivity and blood sugar.
  • Digestive system: Warms digestion and eases gas.
  • Whole body: Antioxidant.

How it works chemically

  • Cinnamaldehyde (Phenylpropanoid aldehyde): Gives the flavor; antimicrobial and stimulates digestion.
  • Type-A procyanidins (Polyphenols): May mimic insulin and help cells take up glucose.
  • Coumarin (mostly in cassia) (Benzopyrone): Can harm the liver in high regular amounts — the reason to prefer Ceylon cinnamon.

Benefits explained

Blood sugar balance (Some research)

Cinnamon may help cells respond better to insulin and slow the absorption of sugar after meals. Some studies show small reductions in fasting blood sugar, though results are inconsistent.

Antioxidant (Some research)

Cinnamon is very rich in polyphenols that protect cells from oxidative stress.

Warming digestion (Traditional use)

Cinnamaldehyde stimulates digestion and has long been used to ease gas, nausea and sluggish digestion.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1000 mg (Ceylon cinnamon powder), 1–2 times a day (cassia: max 1,000 mg a day).

The herb by itself: 0.5–1.3 g (Ground Ceylon cinnamon (¼–½ teaspoon)), 1–2 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Adding warmth and natural sweetness to oats, fruit and coffee
  • A warming tea to aid digestion
  • Part of an overall diet that supports healthy blood sugar

How to prepare

Simmer a cinnamon stick in tea, milk or cider. Sprinkle ground cinnamon on porridge, apples and sweet potatoes.

Safety & cautions

Cassia cinnamon contains coumarin, which can harm the liver in large regular amounts. Choose Ceylon cinnamon for daily use. It may lower blood sugar alongside diabetes medicine.
