Cayenne

Capsicum annuum · Solanaceae (nightshade family) · Aches & Joints, Culinary, Heart & Circulation

Benefits at a glance

Cayenne is a fiery pepper whose heat can actually relieve pain. Its capsaicin depletes the nerve messenger that carries pain signals, which is why capsaicin creams help arthritis and nerve pain. It warms the body by widening blood vessels near the skin. Small studies show it slightly boosts calorie burning and helps you feel full sooner. Used in cooking, it adds heat along with antioxidant carotenoids.

Overview

Cayenne is a hot chili native to the Americas. Its heat comes from capsaicin, which is the active ingredient in many over-the-counter creams for muscle and joint pain. Capsaicin works by temporarily depleting the nerve signals that carry pain.

What it does in your body

  • Nerves (skin): Reduces pain signals when applied to the skin.
  • Metabolism: Slightly increases calorie burning and fullness.
  • Circulation: Warms by widening surface blood vessels.

How it works chemically

  • Capsaicin (Capsaicinoid (alkaloid)): Activates TRPV1 heat-pain receptors; with repeated use, nerve endings run out of substance P, the messenger that carries pain, so pain is reduced.
  • Dihydrocapsaicin (Capsaicinoid): Works the same way as capsaicin.
  • Carotenoids (capsanthin) (Pigments): Antioxidants that give the red color.

Benefits explained

Pain relief on the skin (Well researched)

Capsaicin depletes 'substance P,' a messenger that carries pain signals. Capsaicin creams and patches are approved treatments for arthritis and nerve pain.

Metabolism and appetite (Some research)

Capsaicin slightly increases the calories the body burns and can help you feel full sooner, according to several small studies.

Warming circulation (Some research)

Capsaicin widens blood vessels near the skin, creating the familiar feeling of warmth.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 450–500 mg (Powdered cayenne), 1–3 times a day with meals.

The herb by itself: 0.25–1 g (Ground cayenne in food), With meals.

Traditional uses

  • Capsaicin creams for sore muscles and joint discomfort
  • Warming the body and circulation on cold days
  • Adding heat to soups, eggs, chocolate and sauces

How to prepare

Start with a small pinch of ground cayenne; heat builds quickly. Wash hands thoroughly after handling.

Safety & cautions

Keep away from eyes and broken skin. Can irritate the stomach and worsen reflux. Test capsaicin creams on a small area first.
