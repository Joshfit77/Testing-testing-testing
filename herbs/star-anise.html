Star Anise

Illicium verum · Schisandraceae · Culinary, Breathing & Throat, Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Star anise is a beautiful spice used to warm the body during colds. In Chinese medicine it eases coughs and chest congestion. Its anethole relieves gas and bloating after meals. It is the natural source of shikimic acid, used to make a flu medicine. It brings deep, warming flavor to soups, teas and mulled drinks.

Overview

Star anise is the dried fruit of an evergreen tree from southern China and Vietnam. It is a key ingredient in Chinese five-spice and Vietnamese pho. It's also a major industrial source of shikimic acid, a starting material for an influenza medicine.

What it does in your body

  • Respiratory system: Warming during coughs and colds.
  • Digestive system: Relieves gas.
  • Immune system: Mild antimicrobial.

How it works chemically

  • trans-Anethole (Phenylpropanoid): Antispasmodic and expectorant.
  • Shikimic acid (Organic acid): Used as a starting material to make the flu drug oseltamivir; not antiviral as a spice.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Coughs and colds (Traditional use)

Star anise is used in Chinese medicine to warm the body and ease coughs during colds.

Digestive comfort (Traditional use)

Like anise and fennel, its anethole helps relieve gas and bloating.

A source of medicine (Some research)

Shikimic acid extracted from star anise is the starting material for the flu medicine oseltamivir (Tamiflu) — though the spice itself won't treat the flu.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not sold as capsules — use whole stars in tea or broth.

The herb by itself: 1 g (1 whole star in tea or broth), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A warming tea for coughs and colds
  • Easing gas after meals
  • Flavoring pho, braises, mulled wine and poached pears

How to prepare

Add 1–2 whole stars to simmering broths or tea and remove before serving.

Safety & cautions

Buy only from trusted food suppliers: Japanese star anise (Illicium anisatum) looks similar but is toxic. Do not give star anise tea to infants.
