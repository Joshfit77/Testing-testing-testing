Chicory Root

Cichorium intybus · Asteraceae (daisy family) · Digestion, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Chicory root is one of the richest sources of the prebiotic fiber inulin. Inulin feeds beneficial gut bacteria and may help relieve constipation. It slows the absorption of sugar and may help balance blood sugar. Its bitter compounds stimulate digestion. Roasted chicory makes a rich, caffeine-free coffee alternative.

Overview

Roasted chicory root has been blended with coffee in France since Napoleon's time and is famous in New Orleans café au lait. The root is one of the richest sources of inulin, a prebiotic fiber that feeds beneficial gut bacteria.

What it does in your body

  • Gut: Feeds beneficial bacteria; relieves constipation.
  • Metabolism: Steadies blood sugar.
  • Liver & digestion: Bitter tonic.

How it works chemically

  • Inulin (Prebiotic fiber): Fermented by gut bacteria into short-chain fatty acids; adds bulk and softens stools.
  • Lactucin & lactucopicrin (Sesquiterpene lactones): Bitter compounds that stimulate digestion and are mildly calming.
  • Chicoric acid (Caffeic acid derivative): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Gut health (Some research)

Chicory root is one of the richest sources of inulin, which increases beneficial bifidobacteria and may help with constipation.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Inulin slows the absorption of sugar and may help balance blood sugar.

Coffee alternative (Traditional use)

Roasted chicory gives a coffee-like flavor without caffeine.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Usually taken as roasted root or inulin powder rather than capsules.

The herb by itself: 2–4 g (Roasted root brewed like coffee (per cup)), 1–3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A caffeine-free coffee substitute or extender
  • Prebiotic fiber for gut health
  • Leaves (radicchio, endive) as bitter salad greens

How to prepare

Brew 1 tablespoon of roasted chicory granules in hot water like coffee, alone or mixed with coffee.

Safety & cautions

Large amounts of inulin can cause gas and bloating. Avoid with ragweed or daisy allergy, and check with a doctor if you have gallstones.
