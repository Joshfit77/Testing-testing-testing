Neem

Azadirachta indica · Meliaceae (mahogany family) · Skin & Hair

Benefits at a glance

Neem is a tree known in India as the village pharmacy. Neem gel reduced plaque and gum inflammation in clinical studies. It is antibacterial and anti-inflammatory, which may help blemish-prone skin. Neem oil is traditionally used for dandruff and to keep head lice away. It must only be used on the skin and never swallowed.

Overview

In India, neem twigs have been used as toothbrushes for centuries, and the tree is woven into daily life for skin care and natural pest control. Neem oil is used in shampoos and soaps and as an organic garden pesticide.

What it does in your body

  • Gums: Reduces plaque and gingivitis.
  • Skin: Antibacterial for blemishes.
  • Scalp: Traditional for dandruff.

How it works chemically

  • Nimbidin & nimbin (Limonoids (triterpenes)): Anti-inflammatory and antibacterial.
  • Azadirachtin (Limonoid): Insect-repellent and insecticidal; the reason neem is used in organic pest sprays.
  • Quercetin (Flavonoid): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Gum health (Some research)

Neem gel reduced plaque and gum inflammation in clinical studies.

Blemish-prone skin (Some research)

Neem is antibacterial and anti-inflammatory, which may help with acne.

Scalp care (Traditional use)

Neem oil is traditionally used for dandruff and to keep head lice away.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Never swallow neem oil. Neem leaf capsules should only be used with medical guidance.

For use on the skin only — do not swallow.

Traditional uses

  • Neem soaps and creams for blemish-prone skin
  • Scalp care for dandruff
  • An organic spray for garden pests

How to prepare

Use ready-made neem soaps, creams or shampoos, or dilute a few drops of neem oil in a carrier oil.

Safety & cautions

Neem oil is toxic if swallowed, especially for children and infants. Avoid internal use during pregnancy or when trying to conceive. Patch-test on skin first.
