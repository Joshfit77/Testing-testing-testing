Cardamom

Elettaria cardamomum · Zingiberaceae (ginger family) · Digestion, Culinary, Breathing & Throat

Benefits at a glance

Cardamom, the queen of spices, is a fragrant aid to digestion. Its oils relax the gut and relieve gas, bloating and heaviness after rich meals. One small study found cardamom helped lower blood pressure. It fights the bacteria that cause bad breath, making it a natural breath freshener. It adds a lovely warmth to chai, coffee and baking.

Overview

Green cardamom is native to the forests of southern India and is one of the world's most expensive spices by weight, after saffron and vanilla. In Ayurveda it is used to balance heavy foods and freshen the breath; it is central to masala chai and Scandinavian baking.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Relieves gas and heaviness.
  • Heart & blood vessels: May lower blood pressure.
  • Mouth: May help fight bad-breath bacteria.

How it works chemically

  • 1,8-Cineole (Monoterpene oxide): Antispasmodic and antimicrobial; also helps loosen mucus.
  • α-Terpinyl acetate (Ester): The main aroma; relaxes the gut.
  • Limonene (Monoterpene): Supports digestion.

Benefits explained

Easing bloating (Traditional use)

Cardamom's cineole and terpinyl acetate relax the gut and help release trapped gas, making it a classic after-meal spice.

Blood pressure (Some research)

In one small study, people with high blood pressure who took cardamom powder daily saw their blood pressure fall over 12 weeks.

Fresh breath (Some research)

Cardamom's oils fight the mouth bacteria that cause bad breath — which is why chewing a pod after meals is a tradition across India and the Middle East.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1500 mg (Powdered cardamom), 2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 0.5–1.5 g (Ground cardamom in food or drinks), 1–2 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Chewing a pod after meals to freshen breath
  • Easing bloating and heaviness after rich food
  • Flavoring chai, coffee, rice dishes and sweet breads

How to prepare

Crack pods lightly before simmering, or grind the black seeds fresh for the best aroma.

Safety & cautions

Generally safe in food amounts. Large amounts may trigger gallstone pain in those prone to it.
