Cumin

Cuminum cyminum · Apiaceae (carrot family) · Culinary, Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Cumin is an earthy spice that has supported digestion for thousands of years. It stimulates digestive enzymes and bile, and a small study found it eased IBS symptoms. Added to a healthy diet, cumin supported weight loss and better cholesterol in small studies. It is a surprisingly good source of iron for energy. Cumin seed water is a traditional after-meal drink for gas and bloating.

Overview

Cumin has been used for over 4,000 years — seeds have been found at ancient Syrian and Egyptian sites. It is a cornerstone of Indian, Middle Eastern, North African and Mexican cooking, and jeera water (cumin seed water) is a traditional Indian digestive drink.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Stimulates enzymes and bile; eases IBS symptoms.
  • Metabolism: May support weight and cholesterol.
  • Blood: Contributes iron.

How it works chemically

  • Cuminaldehyde (Monoterpene aldehyde): Main aroma; stimulates digestive enzymes and is antimicrobial.
  • γ-Terpinene & p-cymene (Monoterpenes): Antioxidant and antispasmodic.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Digestive comfort (Some research)

Cumin stimulates digestive enzymes and bile. A small trial found cumin extract improved symptoms of irritable bowel syndrome.

Weight and cholesterol (Some research)

Small studies found that adding cumin powder to a healthy diet supported weight loss and improved cholesterol levels.

A source of iron (Traditional use)

One teaspoon of ground cumin supplies a meaningful amount of iron, which is important for energy.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1000 mg (Powdered cumin seed), 2–3 times a day with meals.

The herb by itself: 1–3 g (Ground cumin in food), Daily, split among meals.

Traditional uses

  • Jeera water after meals to ease gas and bloating
  • A traditional aid for sluggish digestion
  • Seasoning lentils, beans, rice, meats and roasted vegetables

How to prepare

Toast whole seeds in a dry pan or hot oil until fragrant, then grind or add whole. For jeera water, simmer 1 teaspoon of seeds in 2 cups of water.

Safety & cautions

Safe in food. May lower blood sugar in larger amounts; use care before surgery.
