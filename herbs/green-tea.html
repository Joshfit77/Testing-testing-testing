Green Tea

Camellia sinensis · Theaceae (tea family) · Energy & Focus, Heart & Circulation

Benefits at a glance

Green tea provides calm, focused energy without the jitters of coffee. Its caffeine and L-theanine work together to improve attention and alertness. Its catechins, especially EGCG, protect cholesterol and blood vessels. People who drink more green tea have lower rates of heart disease in large population studies. It may also slightly boost metabolism and fat burning.

Overview

All true teas come from the same plant; green tea is simply steamed or pan-fired to prevent oxidation. It contains moderate caffeine plus L-theanine, an amino acid associated with relaxed focus. Green tea drinkers in large population studies tend to have better heart health.

What it does in your body

  • Brain: Calm, focused alertness.
  • Heart & blood vessels: Improves cholesterol and vessel health.
  • Metabolism: Slightly increases fat burning.

How it works chemically

  • Caffeine (Methylxanthine alkaloid): Blocks adenosine receptors — adenosine builds up to make you sleepy, so blocking it keeps you alert.
  • L-Theanine (Amino acid): Raises calming alpha brain waves and balances caffeine's jittery effects.
  • EGCG (epigallocatechin gallate) (Catechin (polyphenol)): Antioxidant that protects LDL cholesterol from oxidation and supports blood vessel function.

Benefits explained

Calm focus (Well researched)

Green tea's caffeine combined with L-theanine improves attention and alertness with fewer jitters than coffee, according to multiple studies.

Heart health (Some research)

Catechins such as EGCG may lower LDL cholesterol. Large Japanese population studies found people who drank more green tea had lower rates of death from heart disease.

Metabolism (Some research)

Green tea catechins may slightly increase fat burning, though the effect is small.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 250–500 mg (Green tea extract (keep EGCG under 800 mg a day)), Once a day with food.

The herb by itself: 2–3 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), 2–4 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Steady morning or midday alertness
  • Antioxidant-rich support for heart health
  • Matcha lattes, cooking and baking

How to prepare

Use water at about 175°F (80°C), not boiling, and steep for 2–3 minutes to avoid bitterness.

Safety & cautions

Contains caffeine — limit late in the day and in pregnancy. High-dose green tea extract supplements have been linked to liver injury. Drink between meals if you have low iron.
