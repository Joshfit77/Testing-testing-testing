Spearmint

Mentha spicata · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Digestion, Culinary, Women's Health

Benefits at a glance

Spearmint is a sweeter, gentler mint that soothes digestion after meals. It relaxes the gut and eases gas and fullness without the strong bite of peppermint. Small studies in women with PCOS found two cups of spearmint tea a day lowered excess testosterone. Its oils have mild antibacterial action that naturally freshens the breath. It is a refreshing, caffeine-free tea suitable for daily enjoyment.

Overview

Spearmint has been cultivated around the Mediterranean for thousands of years and is the mint in traditional Moroccan mint tea and many chewing gums. It contains carvone rather than menthol, so it tastes softer than peppermint. Small studies have looked at spearmint tea in women with hormonal imbalances.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Gently relaxes the gut after meals.
  • Hormonal system: May lower free testosterone in women with PCOS.
  • Mouth: Freshens breath.

How it works chemically

  • Carvone (Monoterpene ketone): Relaxes smooth muscle in the digestive tract and gives the sweet minty scent.
  • Rosmarinic acid (Polyphenol): Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.
  • Limonene (Monoterpene): Mild antimicrobial and helps move food through the stomach.

Benefits explained

Gentle digestion (Traditional use)

Like other mints, spearmint relaxes the digestive tract, easing gas and fullness after meals. Its carvone content gives a softer flavor than peppermint, so it suits people who find peppermint too strong.

Hormone balance in women (Some research)

Small trials in women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) found that two cups of spearmint tea a day lowered free testosterone levels, which may help reduce unwanted hair growth. Larger studies are still needed.

Fresh breath (Traditional use)

Spearmint's clean, sweet aroma masks odors, and its oils have mild antibacterial action — which is why it's a classic in chewing gum and toothpaste.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 400–800 mg (Powdered spearmint leaf), 2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), 2 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A gentle digestive tea suitable for those who find peppermint too strong
  • Freshening the breath
  • Flavoring salads, yogurt sauces, lamb and lemonade

How to prepare

Steep a handful of fresh sprigs or 1–2 teaspoons dried in hot water for 5 minutes. Delicious sweetened lightly with honey.

Safety & cautions

Generally very safe in food amounts. Large medicinal amounts may not be suitable for those with liver or kidney disease.
