Eucalyptus

Eucalyptus globulus · Myrtaceae (myrtle family) · Breathing & Throat

Benefits at a glance

Eucalyptus is famous for helping people breathe more easily. Its eucalyptol thins mucus and calms inflammation in the airways. Trials found cineole capsules sped relief from sinusitis and bronchitis. Its steam opens a stuffy nose during colds. It is also used in warming rubs for sore muscles.

Overview

Native to Australia, eucalyptus leaves were used by Aboriginal peoples for fevers and wounds. Its oil is high in eucalyptol (1,8-cineole), a common ingredient in chest rubs, lozenges and inhalants for congestion.

What it does in your body

  • Respiratory system: Thins mucus and eases congestion.
  • Immune system: Antibacterial.
  • Muscles: Warming rubs for aches.

How it works chemically

  • 1,8-Cineole (eucalyptol) (Monoterpene oxide): Reduces inflammatory messengers in the airways, thins mucus and speeds the cilia that sweep it out.
  • α-Pinene (Monoterpene): Antimicrobial and opens airways slightly.
  • Flavonoids & tannins (leaf) (Polyphenols): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Clearing congestion (Some research)

Eucalyptol (1,8-cineole) helps thin mucus and calm airway inflammation. Clinical trials of cineole capsules found faster relief in sinusitis and bronchitis.

May help fight germs (Some research)

Eucalyptus oil is antibacterial and is used in some mouthwashes and wound products.

Muscle aches (Traditional use)

Eucalyptus is a common ingredient in warming rubs for sore muscles.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 200 mg (Enteric-coated cineole (eucalyptol) capsules), 3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1.5–2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Steam inhalation to clear a stuffy nose
  • A eucalyptus bundle hung in the shower for spa-like steam
  • Chest rubs during colds (adults)

How to prepare

Add 2–3 drops of oil (or a few leaves) to a bowl of hot water, cover your head with a towel and breathe gently for 5–10 minutes.

Safety & cautions

Eucalyptus oil is poisonous if swallowed — even small amounts can be dangerous for children. Do not use near the face of infants or young children. Toxic to cats and dogs.
