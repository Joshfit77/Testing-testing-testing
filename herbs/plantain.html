Plantain Leaf

Plantago major · Plantaginaceae · Skin & Hair, Breathing & Throat

Benefits at a glance

Plantain is a common backyard plant that makes excellent first aid. A crushed leaf calms bee stings, insect bites and nettle stings. Its aucubin and allantoin calm inflammation and help skin heal. Its mucilage soothes irritated throats and dry coughs. It is approved in Germany for irritation of the mouth, throat and airways.

Overview

Not related to the banana-like fruit, this common lawn weed was called 'white man's footprint' by Native Americans because it followed European settlers. A chewed leaf placed on bee stings and bug bites is a traditional field remedy. Its leaves contain soothing aucubin and mucilage.

What it does in your body

  • Skin: Soothes stings and helps healing.
  • Respiratory system: Calms coughs and irritated throats.
  • Digestive system: Mild soothing effect.

How it works chemically

  • Aucubin (Iridoid glycoside): Anti-inflammatory (blocks NF-κB) and antimicrobial.
  • Allantoin (Urea derivative): Encourages skin cells to multiply and heal.
  • Mucilage (Polysaccharides): Coats and soothes irritated tissue.

Benefits explained

Bites and stings (Traditional use)

Plantain's aucubin and allantoin calm inflammation and support skin repair, making it a famous field remedy for stings and bites.

Soothing coughs (Some research)

Its mucilage soothes irritated throats. Ribwort plantain is approved in Germany for irritation of the mouth, throat and airways.

Wound healing (Some research)

Laboratory studies show plantain extracts reduce inflammation and help skin heal.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the leaf as tea or on the skin.

The herb by itself: 1.5–2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), 3–4 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A quick poultice for bee stings, insect bites and nettle stings
  • Soothing salves for scrapes and splinters
  • A tea for dry, irritated coughs

How to prepare

Crush or chew a clean leaf and apply directly to the bite. Infuse dried leaves in oil for salves.

Safety & cautions

Generally very safe. Make sure of correct identification. Psyllium (a related Plantago) can cause allergy in some people.
