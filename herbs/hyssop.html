Hyssop

Hyssopus officinalis · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Breathing & Throat, Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Hyssop is an ancient herb traditionally used for the chest and digestion. It is a traditional expectorant that helps loosen phlegm in chest colds. Its aromatic oils relieve gas and bloating. Laboratory studies show it may be active against some viruses. It also attracts bees and makes a beautiful garden hedge.

Overview

Hyssop is mentioned in the Psalms: 'Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean.' (The biblical plant may have been a related herb such as Syrian oregano.) The garden hyssop of Europe has long been used for coughs and congestion, and flavors liqueurs like Chartreuse.

What it does in your body

  • Respiratory system: Loosens chest congestion.
  • Digestive system: Relieves gas.
  • Immune system: Mild antiviral activity.

How it works chemically

  • Pinocamphone & isopinocamphone (Monoterpene ketones): Give the camphor note; in concentrated oil they can trigger seizures.
  • Rosmarinic acid (Polyphenol): Antioxidant and antiviral.
  • Flavonoids (diosmin) (Polyphenols): Anti-inflammatory.

Benefits explained

Coughs and congestion (Traditional use)

Hyssop is a traditional expectorant used to loosen phlegm during chest colds.

Digestion (Traditional use)

Its aromatic oils relieve gas and bloating.

Antiviral activity (Some research)

Laboratory studies show hyssop extracts are active against some viruses, including herpes viruses.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the herb as tea.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried herb steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A warm tea for coughs and chest congestion
  • Easing gas and indigestion
  • A bee-friendly hedge for herb gardens

How to prepare

Steep 1 teaspoon dried herb in hot water for 10 minutes. Blend with honey and thyme.

Safety & cautions

Hyssop essential oil can trigger seizures — never take it internally. Avoid the herb during pregnancy and with epilepsy.
