Tribulus

Tribulus terrestris · Zygophyllaceae (caltrop family) · Men's Health

Benefits at a glance

Tribulus is one of the most common ingredients in testosterone boosters, but the honest evidence is disappointing. A systematic review of human trials found it did not raise testosterone in most studies, apart from a few small studies in men with fertility problems. Where it does seem to help is libido: several trials found improved sexual desire and erectile function scores. Its main compound, protodioscin, was thought to raise the hormone signal that drives testosterone, but this hasn't held up in people. It is best seen as a libido herb, not a testosterone herb.

Overview

Tribulus is a low, sprawling weed whose spiny fruits can puncture bare feet and bike tires, earning it the name puncture vine. It has long been used in Ayurveda (gokshura) and Chinese medicine as a tonic. It became famous in sports supplements, but careful human trials have mostly found it does not raise testosterone.

What it does in your body

  • Reproductive system: May improve libido and erectile function.
  • Hormones: Little effect on testosterone in most human trials.
  • Urinary system: Traditionally a mild diuretic.

How it works chemically

  • Protodioscin (Steroidal saponin): Proposed to raise luteinizing hormone and convert to DHEA; these effects have not been reliably seen in people.
  • Tribulosin & other saponins (Steroidal saponins): May relax blood vessels, supporting erectile function.
  • Kaempferol & quercetin glycosides (Flavonoids): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Libido and erectile function (Some research)

Several randomized trials found tribulus extract (around 750–1,500 mg a day) improved sexual desire and erectile function scores in men and women compared with placebo.

Testosterone — mostly no effect (Some research)

A systematic review found tribulus did not raise testosterone in healthy men or athletes in most trials. A few small studies in men with low testosterone or fertility problems saw modest rises, so results are mixed at best.

Traditional men's tonic (Traditional use)

In Ayurveda (gokshura) and Chinese medicine, tribulus fruit has been used for vitality, urinary health and fertility for centuries.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 250–750 mg (Standardized fruit extract), 1–2 times a day, with food.

The herb by itself: 3–6 g (Dried fruit simmered as tea (Chinese medicine style)), Once a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional tonic for libido and sexual health
  • Sometimes used for erectile function
  • Traditional support for urinary health in Ayurveda

How to prepare

Use a standardized fruit extract (often 40–60% saponins), taken with food.

Safety & cautions

Human trials mostly show it does not raise testosterone, so don't rely on it for that. Can upset the stomach. Rare cases of kidney and liver injury have been reported with high doses. May lower blood sugar and blood pressure. Avoid in pregnancy, breastfeeding, hormone-sensitive conditions and kidney disease.
