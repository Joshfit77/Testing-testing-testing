Horny Goat Weed

Epimedium grandiflorum · Berberidaceae (barberry family) · Men's Health

Benefits at a glance

Horny goat weed has been a Chinese tonic for drive and vitality for centuries. Its key compound, icariin, gently blocks PDE5 — the same enzyme targeted by Viagra — which may help blood flow for erections. In animal studies icariin raised testosterone, but this has not been confirmed in people. A two-year study suggests its flavonoids help protect bone strength after menopause. Think of it as a traditional libido herb with promising lab research rather than a proven testosterone booster.

Overview

Legend says a Chinese goat herder noticed his flock became unusually frisky after grazing on this plant, giving it its name (yin yang huo). Its leaves have been used in Chinese medicine for libido, stamina and strong bones. Its main compound, icariin, is well studied in the lab, but human research is still limited.

What it does in your body

  • Circulation: May improve blood flow for erections.
  • Hormones: Raised testosterone in animal studies.
  • Bones: Supports bone density.

How it works chemically

  • Icariin (Prenylated flavonol glycoside): Weakly inhibits PDE5 and boosts nitric oxide, relaxing blood vessels; raised testosterone in animal studies.
  • Epimedins A, B and C (Flavonoid glycosides): Related compounds that support bone-building cells.
  • Icaritin (Flavonoid): An active breakdown product with mild hormone-like effects.

Benefits explained

Erectile function (Some research)

Icariin weakly blocks PDE5, the enzyme targeted by erectile-dysfunction medicines, and raised nitric oxide in lab studies. Human trials are few and small.

Testosterone (animal studies) (Some research)

Icariin raised testosterone and improved sperm in animal studies; this has not yet been shown in men.

Bone strength (Some research)

A two-year trial in women after menopause found Epimedium flavonoids helped prevent bone loss.

Traditional libido tonic (Traditional use)

Used in Chinese medicine (yin yang huo) for drive, stamina and vitality for centuries.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 250–500 mg (Extract standardized to 10–20% icariin), Once or twice a day.

The herb by itself: 3–6 g (Dried leaf simmered as tea), Once a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional support for libido and erectile function
  • Traditional Chinese tonic for bone strength
  • A common ingredient in men's vitality blends

How to prepare

Simmer dried leaves as tea, or use an extract standardized for icariin.

Safety & cautions

High doses can cause dizziness, dry mouth, nosebleeds, a fast heartbeat and muscle spasms. May lower blood pressure and slow clotting. Do not combine with Viagra-type medicines (sildenafil, tadalafil) or nitrates. Avoid with heart rhythm problems, hormone-sensitive conditions, pregnancy and breastfeeding.
