Milk Thistle

Silybum marianum · Asteraceae (daisy family) · Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Milk thistle is the best-known herb for supporting the liver. Its silymarin helps protect liver cells from toxins and boosts the liver's own antioxidant, glutathione. Studies show improved liver enzyme levels in some liver conditions. It may help lower blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes. It is even used in hospitals as an antidote for death cap mushroom poisoning.

Overview

Milk thistle has been used for over 2,000 years for liver and gallbladder complaints. Its seeds contain silymarin, a group of compounds that has been widely researched for protecting liver cells. It's used in hospitals in Europe as an antidote for death cap mushroom poisoning.

What it does in your body

  • Liver: Protects liver cells and supports repair.
  • Metabolism: May lower blood sugar.
  • Whole body: Raises antioxidant defenses.

How it works chemically

  • Silybin (silibinin) (Flavonolignan): Stabilizes liver cell membranes and blocks toxins from entering liver cells through OATP transporters.
  • Silymarin complex (Flavonolignans): Raises glutathione, the liver's main antioxidant, and boosts protein synthesis for liver repair.
  • Taxifolin (Flavonoid): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Protecting the liver (Some research)

Silymarin stabilizes liver cell membranes and raises glutathione, the liver's main antioxidant. Studies show improved liver enzyme levels in some liver conditions.

Mushroom poisoning antidote (Well researched)

Intravenous silibinin, made from milk thistle, is used in European hospitals to treat death cap mushroom poisoning.

Blood sugar (Some research)

Several trials found milk thistle lowered fasting blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 140–175 mg (Milk thistle extract (70–80% silymarin)), 2–3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 3–5 g (Freshly ground seed in food or tea), 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional support for liver health
  • Supporting digestion of rich foods
  • Ground seeds sprinkled on cereal or smoothies

How to prepare

Grind seeds fresh and add a teaspoon to food, or simmer crushed seeds for tea. Standardized capsules are most common.

Safety & cautions

Avoid with ragweed or daisy allergy. May lower blood sugar. Anyone with liver disease should be under a doctor's care rather than self-treating.
