Fennel

Foeniculum vulgare · Apiaceae (carrot family) · Digestion, Women's Health, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Fennel is a sweet, licorice-flavored seed famous for easing digestion. Its anethole relaxes the intestines, relieving gas, bloating and colic. Several small studies found fennel eased period cramps as well as a common pain reliever. It is a traditional herb for supporting breast milk supply. Chewing a few seeds after meals also freshens the breath.

Overview

In India, roasted fennel seeds (saunf) are offered after meals to freshen the breath and aid digestion. Romans believed fennel sharpened eyesight. The seeds contain anethole, the same aromatic compound found in anise.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Relaxes the gut and expels gas.
  • Reproductive system: Eases menstrual cramps; traditionally supports milk supply.
  • Respiratory system: Loosens mucus.

How it works chemically

  • trans-Anethole (Phenylpropanoid): Relaxes smooth muscle (antispasmodic) and has weak estrogen-like activity.
  • Fenchone (Monoterpene ketone): Gives a bitter-camphor note; antimicrobial and helps loosen mucus.
  • Estragole (Phenylpropanoid): Natural flavor compound; avoid concentrated amounts long-term.

Benefits explained

Bloating and colic (Some research)

Anethole relaxes the muscles of the intestines. A clinical trial found a fennel seed oil emulsion reduced colic in infants, and fennel is widely used for gas and bloating in adults.

Period pain (Some research)

Several small trials found fennel extract eased menstrual cramps about as well as the pain reliever mefenamic acid.

Breastfeeding support (Traditional use)

Fennel is a traditional galactagogue — an herb used to support breast milk — though scientific evidence is limited.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1000 mg (Powdered fennel seed), 2–3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1.5–2.5 g (Crushed seed steeped as tea (per cup)), 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Chewing a pinch of seeds after meals to ease bloating and freshen breath
  • A traditional tea to support breastfeeding
  • Roasting the bulb or shaving it raw into salads

How to prepare

Lightly crush 1 teaspoon of seeds and steep in hot water for 10 minutes. Roast the bulb with olive oil and lemon.

Safety & cautions

Fennel has weak estrogen-like effects — check with your doctor if you have a hormone-sensitive condition. Do not use fennel essential oil internally. Not for prolonged use in infants.
