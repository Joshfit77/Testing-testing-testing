Cranberry

Vaccinium macrocarpon · Ericaceae (heath family) · Women's Health, Culinary, Immune Support

Benefits at a glance

Cranberry is the best-known natural aid for urinary health. Its proanthocyanidins stop E. coli bacteria from sticking to the bladder wall. A major review found cranberry products reduced repeat urinary tract infections. Its polyphenols may also support healthy cholesterol and blood vessels. The same anti-sticking effect may help keep plaque bacteria off the teeth.

Overview

Native Americans used cranberries for food, dye and medicine. Cranberries contain proanthocyanidins (PACs), which may help stop certain bacteria from sticking to the bladder wall. Studies suggest regular cranberry products may reduce the risk of repeat urinary tract infections in some women.

What it does in your body

  • Urinary tract: Helps prevent repeat infections.
  • Heart & blood vessels: May improve cholesterol.
  • Mouth: Reduces plaque bacteria sticking to teeth.

How it works chemically

  • A-type proanthocyanidins (PACs) (Polyphenols): Block the hair-like fimbriae that E. coli bacteria use to cling to the bladder wall, so they are flushed out.
  • Quercetin & myricetin (Flavonoids): Antioxidant.
  • Ursolic acid (Triterpene): Anti-inflammatory.

Benefits explained

Preventing urinary infections (Well researched)

Cranberry's proanthocyanidins stop E. coli bacteria from sticking to the bladder wall. A 2023 Cochrane review found cranberry products reduced repeat urinary tract infections in women, children and people prone to them.

Heart health (Some research)

Cranberry polyphenols may improve cholesterol and blood vessel function.

Oral health (Some research)

The same anti-sticking effect may help prevent plaque bacteria from clinging to teeth.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500 mg (Cranberry extract (36 mg PACs)), 1–2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1 cup (240–300 ml) unsweetened juice. Unsweetened cranberry juice.

Traditional uses

  • May help reduce repeat urinary tract infections
  • A vitamin C-rich addition to the diet
  • Sauces, muffins, salads and trail mix

How to prepare

Choose unsweetened juice diluted with water, or capsules standardized for PACs.

Safety & cautions

Cranberry does not treat an active infection — see a doctor for UTI symptoms. Large amounts may interact with warfarin and increase risk of kidney stones in prone people.
