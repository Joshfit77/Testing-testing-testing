Hibiscus

Hibiscus sabdariffa · Malvaceae (mallow family) · Heart & Circulation, Culinary, Immune Support

Benefits at a glance

Hibiscus is a tart, ruby-red tea with real benefits for heart health. Clinical trials found two to three cups a day lowered blood pressure in people with mildly high readings. It may also modestly improve cholesterol levels. Hibiscus is rich in vitamin C and anthocyanin antioxidants. Hot or iced, it is a refreshing caffeine-free drink.

Overview

Known as agua de Jamaica in Mexico, sorrel in the Caribbean and karkadé in Egypt, hibiscus tea is enjoyed around the world. Several clinical studies found that drinking hibiscus tea daily modestly lowered blood pressure in people with mild hypertension.

What it does in your body

  • Heart & blood vessels: Lowers blood pressure.
  • Kidneys: Mild diuretic.
  • Metabolism: May improve cholesterol.

How it works chemically

  • Delphinidin- & cyanidin-3-sambubioside (Anthocyanins): Inhibit ACE, the enzyme that makes the vessel-tightening hormone angiotensin II — similar to a mild blood pressure medicine.
  • Hibiscus acid & organic acids (Organic acids): Tart flavor and a mild diuretic effect.
  • Vitamin C (Vitamin): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Healthy blood pressure (Well researched)

Hibiscus anthocyanins act a little like a mild ACE inhibitor and diuretic. In clinical trials, two to three cups a day lowered systolic blood pressure by about 7 mmHg in people with mildly high blood pressure.

Cholesterol (Some research)

Some studies show modest improvements in cholesterol levels with regular hibiscus tea.

Antioxidant and vitamin C (Some research)

Hibiscus is rich in vitamin C and anthocyanin antioxidants.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 250–500 mg (Hibiscus extract), 1–2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1.5–2.5 g (Dried calyces steeped as tea (per cup)), 2–3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A daily tea as part of a heart-healthy lifestyle
  • A refreshing, caffeine-free iced drink in summer
  • Rich in vitamin C and antioxidants

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons of dried calyces in hot water for 5–10 minutes, or cold-brew overnight. Sweeten lightly with honey.

Safety & cautions

May lower blood pressure further if you take blood pressure medicine. Avoid during pregnancy. May interact with some medicines, including acetaminophen and chloroquine.
