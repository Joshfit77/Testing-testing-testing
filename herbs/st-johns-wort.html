St. John's Wort

Hypericum perforatum · Hypericaceae · Calm & Mood

Benefits at a glance

St. John's wort is a well-studied herb for mild to moderate low mood. A major review found it worked better than placebo and similarly to standard antidepressants for mild depression. Its red oil is a traditional rub for nerve pain and minor burns. However, it interacts with many medicines, including birth control pills and antidepressants. It should only be used with a doctor's guidance.

Overview

Named because it blooms around St. John's Day in late June, its petals release a red oil when crushed. Many studies found it may help mild to moderate low mood, but it interacts with a very long list of medicines, which makes medical guidance essential.

What it does in your body

  • Brain: Improves mild to moderate low mood.
  • Liver: Speeds up the breakdown of many medicines.
  • Skin: Increases sun sensitivity.

How it works chemically

  • Hyperforin (Phloroglucinol): Raises sodium inside nerve cells (via TRPC6 channels), which reduces reuptake of serotonin, norepinephrine and dopamine. It also switches on the liver enzyme CYP3A4 and the P-glycoprotein pump — the cause of its many drug interactions.
  • Hypericin (Naphthodianthrone): Contributes to the mood effect and makes skin more sensitive to sunlight.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Antioxidant and supportive.

Benefits explained

Mild to moderate low mood (Well researched)

Hyperforin and hypericin affect serotonin, dopamine and norepinephrine. A Cochrane review found St. John's wort worked better than placebo and similarly to standard antidepressants for mild to moderate depression — but only use it with medical guidance because of its many interactions.

Menopausal mood (Some research)

Combined with black cohosh, it improved mood and hot flushes in some menopause trials.

Nerve pain and minor burns (Traditional use)

The red oil made from its flowers is a traditional rub for nerve pain and minor burns.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 300 mg (Standardized extract (0.3% hypericin) — only with medical guidance), 3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 2–4 g (Dried herb as tea, per day — only with medical guidance), Once a day, or split.

Traditional uses

  • Traditionally used for mild low mood
  • Red infused oil for minor burns and nerve aches
  • Supporting seasonal low spirits

How to prepare

Only use standardized products under the guidance of your doctor or pharmacist.

Safety & cautions

Do not use without medical advice. It interacts with birth control pills (making them less effective), antidepressants, blood thinners, HIV medicines, transplant drugs, heart medicines and many others. Causes sun sensitivity. Never use it in place of treatment for depression.
