Licorice Root

Glycyrrhiza glabra · Fabaceae (pea family) · Breathing & Throat, Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Licorice root is a naturally sweet herb that soothes the throat and stomach. It coats and calms sore throats, and a licorice gargle reduced sore throat after surgery. DGL licorice helps protect the stomach lining and eases heartburn. Its compounds have anti-inflammatory and antiviral effects in the lab. Because whole licorice can raise blood pressure, it is best used short-term.

Overview

Licorice root is about 50 times sweeter than sugar thanks to glycyrrhizin. It was found in Tutankhamun's tomb and is one of the most commonly used herbs in Chinese medicine, where it is said to harmonize formulas. DGL (deglycyrrhizinated licorice) has the glycyrrhizin removed.

What it does in your body

  • Throat & airways: Soothes and loosens mucus.
  • Digestive system: Protects the stomach lining.
  • Kidneys & blood pressure: Whole licorice causes salt and water retention and potassium loss.

How it works chemically

  • Glycyrrhizin (Triterpene saponin): Very sweet; turns into glycyrrhetinic acid, which blocks the enzyme 11β-HSD2. Cortisol then acts like aldosterone, holding on to sodium and losing potassium — raising blood pressure.
  • Liquiritigenin & isoliquiritigenin (Flavonoids): Anti-inflammatory and antispasmodic.
  • Glabridin (Isoflavan): Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.

Benefits explained

Soothing sore throats (Some research)

Licorice coats and soothes the throat. In a hospital study, a licorice gargle before surgery reduced sore throat afterwards.

Protecting the stomach lining (Some research)

DGL licorice may help the stomach produce more protective mucus, easing occasional heartburn and indigestion.

Anti-inflammatory (Some research)

Glycyrrhizin has anti-inflammatory and antiviral effects in laboratory studies — but it is also the compound that raises blood pressure.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 380–760 mg (DGL licorice (chewable)), Before meals, 2–3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried root simmered as tea (per cup) — max 4–6 weeks), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Soothing a sore throat and dry cough
  • DGL tablets for occasional heartburn and stomach comfort
  • Sweetening herbal tea blends naturally

How to prepare

Simmer 1 teaspoon of chopped root in a cup of water for 10 minutes, or add a small piece to other teas for sweetness.

Safety & cautions

Regular use of whole licorice can raise blood pressure and lower potassium. Avoid with high blood pressure, heart or kidney disease, during pregnancy, and with diuretics or digoxin. Keep use short unless it's DGL.
