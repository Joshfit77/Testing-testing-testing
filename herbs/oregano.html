Oregano

Origanum vulgare · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Culinary, Immune Support, Breathing & Throat

Benefits at a glance

Oregano is a Mediterranean herb packed with antioxidants. Its carvacrol and thymol strongly fight bacteria and fungi in laboratory studies. Oregano tea and steam are traditional remedies for coughs and congestion. Dried oregano is one of the richest antioxidant sources among kitchen herbs. Adding it generously to everyday meals is an easy way to enjoy its benefits.

Overview

The name comes from the Greek oros (mountain) and ganos (joy). Oregano is rich in carvacrol and thymol, compounds studied in the lab for their antimicrobial activity. It is also a good source of antioxidants, especially when dried.

What it does in your body

  • Immune system: Antimicrobial activity against bacteria and fungi.
  • Respiratory system: Eases coughs and congestion.
  • Whole body: High antioxidant content.

How it works chemically

  • Carvacrol (Phenolic monoterpene): Damages microbial cell membranes so bacteria and fungi leak and die.
  • Thymol (Phenolic monoterpene): Works alongside carvacrol as an antiseptic.
  • Rosmarinic acid (Polyphenol): Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.

Benefits explained

Rich in antioxidants (Some research)

Dried oregano is one of the most antioxidant-rich culinary herbs, thanks mainly to rosmarinic acid, carvacrol and thymol.

May help fight germs (Some research)

Carvacrol and thymol show strong antibacterial and antifungal activity in laboratory studies. Human research is still limited, so oregano oil is not a replacement for medical treatment.

Coughs and congestion (Traditional use)

Oregano tea and steam are traditional Mediterranean remedies to ease coughs and clear a stuffy nose.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 450–500 mg (Powdered oregano leaf), 1–2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Seasoning tomato sauces, pizza, grilled vegetables and beans
  • A traditional warm tea for coughs and colds
  • Steam inhalation for a congested nose

How to prepare

Add dried oregano early in cooking; fresh leaves at the end. For tea, steep 1 teaspoon dried leaf for 5–10 minutes.

Safety & cautions

Oregano oil is very concentrated: never take it undiluted, and avoid it during pregnancy. People allergic to mint-family plants may react.
