Nutmeg

Myristica fragrans · Myristicaceae (nutmeg family) · Culinary, Restful Sleep, Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Nutmeg is a warming spice that brings comfort in very small amounts. A tiny pinch in warm milk is a traditional bedtime remedy for relaxation. It is traditionally used to ease gas, indigestion and loose stools. Its compounds fight mouth bacteria in laboratory studies. It must always be used in small culinary amounts, because large doses can be poisonous.

Overview

Nutmeg is the seed of a tropical evergreen tree; its lacy red covering is the spice mace. Wars were fought over the nutmeg trade in the 1600s. Traditionally, a tiny pinch in warm milk at bedtime was used to encourage sleep.

What it does in your body

  • Nervous system: Very small amounts are traditionally relaxing.
  • Digestive system: Eases gas and loose stools.
  • Mouth: Antibacterial.

How it works chemically

  • Myristicin (Phenylpropanoid): Weakly inhibits MAO and affects the nervous system — calming in pinches, but toxic (hallucinations, racing heart) in large amounts.
  • Elemicin (Phenylpropanoid): Adds to the psychoactive effect at high doses.
  • Sabinene & pinene (Monoterpenes): Aromatic oils that aid digestion.

Benefits explained

Restful sleep (Traditional use)

A tiny pinch in warm milk is a traditional bedtime remedy. Myristicin may have mild sedative effects, but evidence is anecdotal — and larger amounts are harmful.

Settling digestion (Traditional use)

Used in small amounts in traditional medicine to ease diarrhea, gas and indigestion.

Fights mouth bacteria (Some research)

Laboratory studies show nutmeg compounds are active against bacteria that cause tooth decay.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Do not take nutmeg as capsules — concentrated nutmeg can be poisonous.

The herb by itself: 0.1–0.5 g (Freshly grated nutmeg — a pinch (never more than 500 mg a day)), Once a day.

Traditional uses

  • A pinch in warm milk at bedtime
  • Easing gas and mild indigestion in tiny amounts
  • Flavoring custards, eggnog, white sauces and spinach

How to prepare

Grate whole nutmeg fresh with a microplane — just a few scrapes are enough.

Safety & cautions

Keep to culinary pinches. Large amounts (around two teaspoons or more) contain enough myristicin to cause nausea, dizziness, hallucinations and heart palpitations. Avoid larger amounts during pregnancy.
