Parsley

Petroselinum crispum · Apiaceae (carrot family) · Culinary, Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Parsley is far more than a garnish — it is a nutrient-packed green. Just two tablespoons supply more than a day's vitamin K for strong bones and healthy clotting. It is also rich in vitamin C, folate and antioxidants such as apigenin. Parsley freshens the breath and has long been used to aid digestion. It acts as a gentle natural diuretic that may help with water retention.

Overview

Ancient Greeks crowned victorious athletes with parsley. Today it is an everyday kitchen herb that is surprisingly rich in vitamins C, A and K. Flat-leaf (Italian) parsley has the strongest flavor; curly parsley is milder.

What it does in your body

  • Urinary system: Mild diuretic.
  • Blood & bones: Rich in vitamin K for clotting and bone health.
  • Digestive system: Freshens breath and aids digestion.

How it works chemically

  • Apiol & myristicin (Phenylpropanoids): Increase urine flow; in large amounts stimulate the uterus, which is why high doses are unsafe in pregnancy.
  • Apigenin (Flavonoid): Anti-inflammatory and mildly calming.
  • Vitamin K1 (Vitamin): Needed to make blood-clotting proteins and bone proteins.

Benefits explained

Packed with nutrients (Some research)

Just two tablespoons of fresh parsley supply more than a day's vitamin K, which supports healthy blood clotting and bones, plus useful vitamin C and folate.

Fresh breath and digestion (Traditional use)

Parsley's chlorophyll and aromatic oils freshen the breath, and it has long been served with meals to aid digestion.

Gentle diuretic (Traditional use)

Parsley is traditionally used to increase urine flow, and animal studies support a mild diuretic effect.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 450–900 mg (Powdered parsley leaf), 1–2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Freshening the breath after a meal
  • A mild traditional diuretic tea
  • The star of tabbouleh, chimichurri and gremolata

How to prepare

Chop generously into salads, grains, soups and sauces. Use the stems too — they are full of flavor.

Safety & cautions

Food amounts are safe. Avoid large medicinal amounts, parsley seed or oil during pregnancy and with kidney disease. High in vitamin K, relevant for warfarin users.
