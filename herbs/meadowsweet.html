Meadowsweet

Filipendula ulmaria · Rosaceae (rose family) · Digestion, Aches & Joints

Benefits at a glance

Meadowsweet is the almond-scented flower that inspired aspirin. It contains natural salicylates that traditionally ease aches and joint pain. Unlike aspirin, it is used to soothe the stomach and acid indigestion. Its tannins help protect the stomach lining. It should be avoided by anyone allergic to aspirin.

Overview

Meadowsweet contains natural salicylates. In 1838, salicylic acid was first produced from it, and the name 'aspirin' comes from its old name, Spiraea. Unlike aspirin, it's traditionally used to soothe stomach acidity.

What it does in your body

  • Stomach: Soothes acid indigestion.
  • Joints: Eases aches.
  • Whole body: Anti-inflammatory.

How it works chemically

  • Salicylaldehyde & methyl salicylate (Salicylates): Converted in the body to salicylic acid, which inhibits COX enzymes — the same pathway as aspirin.
  • Tannins (Polyphenols): Astringent; protect the stomach lining.
  • Spiraeoside (Flavonoid): Anti-inflammatory.

Benefits explained

Acid indigestion (Traditional use)

Meadowsweet's tannins and mucilage soothe the stomach lining, making it a traditional remedy for heartburn.

Joint pain (Traditional use)

Its natural salicylates — the same family as aspirin — are traditionally used for aches and rheumatic pain.

Anti-inflammatory (Some research)

Laboratory studies confirm anti-inflammatory activity.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the flowers as tea.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried flowers steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Soothing acid indigestion and heartburn
  • Traditional support for achy joints
  • Flavoring mead and cordials

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons dried flowers in hot water for 10 minutes.

Safety & cautions

Avoid if you are allergic or sensitive to aspirin, have asthma triggered by aspirin, take blood thinners, or are pregnant. Not for children under 16 (risk of Reye's syndrome).
