Elderflower

Sambucus nigra · Viburnaceae (elder family) · Breathing & Throat, Immune Support, Skin & Hair

Benefits at a glance

Elderflower is a fragrant blossom traditionally used for colds and fevers. Its hot tea encourages gentle sweating to help the body through a feverish cold. It is used in European remedies to ease sinus congestion. Its flavonoids calm inflammation and support small blood vessels. Elderflower also makes a refreshing cordial and a gentle facial rinse.

Overview

Elder's lacy flowers bloom in early summer and have a sweet, muscat-like perfume. They're used for the famous English elderflower cordial and in European folk medicine as a hot tea to encourage sweating during colds and fevers.

What it does in your body

  • Immune system: Supports the body through feverish colds.
  • Respiratory system: Eases sinus congestion.
  • Skin: Gentle toner.

How it works chemically

  • Flavonoids (rutin, quercetin) (Polyphenols): Anti-inflammatory and support small blood vessels.
  • Phenolic acids (chlorogenic acid) (Polyphenols): Antioxidant and promote sweating.
  • Triterpenes (Terpenoids): Anti-inflammatory.

Benefits explained

Colds and fevers (Traditional use)

Elderflower tea is diaphoretic — it encourages gentle sweating, which has long been used to help the body through feverish colds.

Sinus congestion (Traditional use)

Elderflower is included in European combination remedies used for sinus congestion.

Anti-inflammatory (Some research)

Elderflower's flavonoids show anti-inflammatory activity in laboratory studies.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the flowers as tea.

The herb by itself: 3–4 g (Dried flowers steeped as tea (per cup)), 3 times a day, hot.

Traditional uses

  • A hot tea at the start of a cold
  • Easing sinus congestion and seasonal sniffles
  • A toning facial rinse

How to prepare

Steep 2 teaspoons of dried flowers in hot water for 10 minutes. For cordial, infuse fresh flower heads with lemon and sugar syrup for 24 hours.

Safety & cautions

Use only the flowers; leaves and stems are toxic. Avoid with autoimmune conditions or if allergic to the plant.
