Yarrow

Achillea millefolium · Asteraceae (daisy family) · Skin & Hair, Immune Support, Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Yarrow is a feathery herb with a long history of healing. It was traditionally used on minor cuts to help stop bleeding. Its bitter compounds stimulate appetite and digestion and ease cramping. A hot yarrow tea encourages sweating to help the body through fevers. It is approved in Germany for loss of appetite and indigestion.

Overview

Its Latin name honors Achilles, who in legend used it to treat his soldiers' wounds. Yarrow leaves were traditionally applied to stop minor bleeding, and the tea was taken hot to encourage sweating during fevers.

What it does in your body

  • Skin: Helps minor bleeding stop.
  • Digestive system: Stimulates appetite; eases cramps.
  • Immune system: Promotes sweating in fevers.

How it works chemically

  • Achilleine (Alkaloid): May speed blood clotting.
  • Sesquiterpene lactones & chamazulene (Terpenoids): Bitter and anti-inflammatory.
  • Apigenin & luteolin (Flavonoids): Antispasmodic on the gut.

Benefits explained

Minor cuts (Traditional use)

Yarrow's achilleine may help blood clot, which is why it was used on the battlefield to stop minor bleeding.

Digestion (Some research)

Yarrow is bitter and relaxes the gut. It is approved in Germany for loss of appetite and indigestion.

Fevers (Traditional use)

A hot yarrow tea encourages sweating, traditionally used to help the body through fevers.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the herb as tea.

The herb by itself: 1.5–2 g (Dried herb steeped as tea (per cup)), 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Fresh leaves on minor cuts and scrapes (traditional)
  • A hot tea at the start of a cold
  • A bitter tea to stimulate digestion

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons dried flowering tops for 10 minutes. Quite bitter — blend with peppermint and elderflower.

Safety & cautions

Avoid during pregnancy and with daisy-family allergies. May increase sun sensitivity and affect blood clotting.
