Lemon Verbena

Aloysia citrodora · Verbenaceae (verbena family) · Calm & Mood, Digestion, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Lemon verbena is the most intensely lemon-scented herb in the garden. It is rich in verbascoside, an antioxidant studied for protecting muscles after exercise. Its tea is a traditional evening drink for relaxation and sleep. It also eases indigestion and gas after meals. Its bright flavor makes healthy drinks and desserts feel special.

Overview

Brought from South America to Europe in the 1600s, lemon verbena became a favorite in French herbal teas, where it's known as verveine. Its leaves release a strong, clean lemon fragrance when brushed.

What it does in your body

  • Muscles: Antioxidant protection after exercise.
  • Nervous system: Relaxing evening tea.
  • Digestive system: Eases indigestion.

How it works chemically

  • Verbascoside (acteoside) (Phenylpropanoid glycoside): Strong antioxidant that reduces muscle damage markers after exercise.
  • Citral (Monoterpene aldehydes): Lemon aroma; antispasmodic.
  • Limonene (Monoterpene): Supports digestion.

Benefits explained

Antioxidant protection (Some research)

Lemon verbena is rich in verbascoside, a powerful antioxidant studied for protecting muscles from damage after exercise.

Relaxation and sleep (Traditional use)

A traditional evening tea in France and Spain, enjoyed to unwind and encourage sleep.

Digestive comfort (Traditional use)

Taken after meals to ease indigestion and gas.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 400 mg (Lemon verbena extract), Once a day.

The herb by itself: 1.5–3 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A relaxing after-dinner tea
  • Settling indigestion and gas
  • Flavoring desserts, syrups, sorbets and lemonade

How to prepare

Steep a few fresh or dried leaves in hot water for 5 minutes. Infuse leaves in cream or sugar syrup for desserts.

Safety & cautions

Generally safe in tea amounts. May irritate the kidneys in very large amounts over time.
