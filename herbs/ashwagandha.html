Ashwagandha

Withania somnifera · Solanaceae (nightshade family) · Calm & Mood, Energy & Focus, Restful Sleep, Men's Health

Benefits at a glance

Ashwagandha is one of the most researched adaptogens for stress. Clinical trials found it lowered stress scores and cortisol levels over eight weeks. It helps people fall asleep faster and improves sleep quality. Studies found it increased muscle strength and endurance with exercise. In men, several trials found it raised testosterone levels and improved sperm quality.

Overview

Its Sanskrit name means 'smell of the horse,' referring to its odor and its reputation for giving the strength of a horse. Used in Ayurveda for over 3,000 years, it has become popular as an adaptogen; several clinical studies found it reduced stress scores and improved sleep quality.

What it does in your body

  • Stress response: Lowers cortisol and stress.
  • Nervous system: Improves sleep quality.
  • Muscles: Supports strength and endurance.

How it works chemically

  • Withanolides (withanolide A, withaferin A) (Steroidal lactones): Calm the stress-hormone axis to lower cortisol and act on GABA receptors for relaxation.
  • Sitoindosides (Glycowithanolides): Adaptogenic and antioxidant.
  • Triethylene glycol (leaves) (Glycol): Promotes sleep in animal studies.

Benefits explained

Stress and cortisol (Well researched)

Several randomized trials found ashwagandha root extract reduced stress scores and lowered cortisol — the main stress hormone — by roughly 20–30% over eight weeks.

Better sleep (Some research)

Trials show ashwagandha helped people fall asleep faster and improved sleep quality, including in people with insomnia.

Strength and stamina (Some research)

Studies found that people doing strength training gained more muscle strength when taking ashwagandha, and endurance (VO2 max) improved in athletes.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 300 mg (Ashwagandha root extract), 1–2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–3 g (Root powder in warm milk), 1–2 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Supporting a sense of calm during stressful periods
  • Encouraging restful sleep
  • Traditional support for strength and stamina

How to prepare

Stir 1/4–1/2 teaspoon of root powder into warm milk with honey and cinnamon (moon milk), or use capsules as directed.

Safety & cautions

Avoid during pregnancy. Rare cases of liver injury have been reported. Check with your doctor if you have thyroid or autoimmune conditions, or take sedatives, thyroid, diabetes or blood pressure medicine.
