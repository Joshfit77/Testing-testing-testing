Hawthorn

Crataegus monogyna · Rosaceae (rose family) · Heart & Circulation

Benefits at a glance

Hawthorn is the classic herbal heart tonic of European tradition. Its flavonoids help the heart muscle pump more efficiently and relax blood vessels. Studies found it improved exercise tolerance in people with mild heart failure. It may modestly lower blood pressure. It is rich in antioxidants that protect the heart and blood vessels.

Overview

Hawthorn hedges have bordered European fields for centuries. Its berries, leaves and flowers are the classic Western herbal heart tonic. Research has looked at hawthorn extracts for supporting heart function and circulation in mild heart failure.

What it does in your body

  • Heart: Helps the heart muscle pump more efficiently.
  • Blood vessels: Widens coronary arteries; may lower blood pressure.
  • Whole body: Antioxidant.

How it works chemically

  • Oligomeric procyanidins (OPCs) (Polyphenols): Help blood vessels release nitric oxide so they relax, and strengthen heart muscle contraction.
  • Vitexin & hyperoside (Flavonoids): Increase blood flow in the coronary arteries and act as antioxidants.
  • Triterpene acids (Terpenoids): Mild heart-protective effects.

Benefits explained

Supporting the heart (Some research)

Hawthorn's flavonoids and procyanidins help the heart muscle pump more efficiently and relax blood vessels. A review of trials found improved exercise tolerance and fewer symptoms in mild heart failure — always under a doctor's care.

Blood pressure (Some research)

Small studies found hawthorn modestly lowered blood pressure.

Antioxidant protection (Traditional use)

The berries, leaves and flowers are rich in antioxidant flavonoids that protect blood vessels.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 160–450 mg (Hawthorn leaf & flower extract), 2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–1.5 g (Dried berries or leaf & flower as tea (per cup)), 3–4 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional support for heart health and circulation
  • Rich in antioxidant flavonoids
  • Jams, jellies and fruit leathers made from the berries

How to prepare

Simmer 1–2 teaspoons dried berries for 15 minutes, or steep leaf and flower for 10 minutes.

Safety & cautions

Do not use for a heart condition without your doctor — it may interact with heart medicines such as digoxin, beta blockers and blood pressure medicines. Do not eat the seeds.
