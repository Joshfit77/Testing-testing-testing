Witch Hazel

Hamamelis virginiana · Hamamelidaceae · Skin & Hair

Benefits at a glance

Witch hazel is a gentle astringent for the skin. Its tannins tighten the skin and reduce oiliness, making it a classic toner. It calms redness, sunburn, razor burn and insect bites. It is an approved remedy for the itching and discomfort of hemorrhoids. Alcohol-free versions are best for sensitive skin.

Overview

Native Americans taught European settlers to use witch hazel bark and leaves for swelling and skin irritation. Its tannins tighten and tone the skin. It's an FDA-recognized ingredient for hemorrhoid relief and a staple toner in many bathroom cabinets.

What it does in your body

  • Skin: Tightens and tones, reduces oiliness.
  • Skin: Calms redness and itching.
  • Blood vessels (skin): Shrinks swollen tissue (hemorrhoids).

How it works chemically

  • Hamamelitannin (Tannin): Astringent: binds proteins on the skin surface, tightening tissue and reducing oozing.
  • Proanthocyanidins & gallic acid (Polyphenols): Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Calm small blood vessels.

Benefits explained

Hemorrhoid relief (Well researched)

Witch hazel is an officially approved astringent for temporary relief of hemorrhoid itching and discomfort.

Calming skin redness (Some research)

Its tannins and polyphenols reduce inflammation; studies show it eases sunburn redness and irritated skin.

Toning oily skin (Traditional use)

Witch hazel tightens the skin and reduces shine, making it a classic toner.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not taken by mouth — witch hazel is for use on the skin.

For use on the skin only — do not swallow.

Traditional uses

  • A toner to reduce oiliness and tighten pores
  • Soothing razor burn, bug bites and minor irritation
  • Cooling relief for puffy eyes (on a cotton pad, avoiding the eyes)

How to prepare

Apply distilled witch hazel with a cotton pad. Choose alcohol-free versions for sensitive skin.

Safety & cautions

For external use. Products containing alcohol can dry and sting sensitive skin. Do not drink distilled witch hazel.

For education only — not medical advice.
