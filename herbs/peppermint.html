Peppermint

Mentha × piperita · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Digestion, Breathing & Throat, Energy & Focus, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Peppermint is one of the best-studied herbs for digestive comfort. Its menthol relaxes the muscles of the intestines, and enteric-coated peppermint oil reduces pain and bloating in irritable bowel syndrome. Rubbed on the temples, diluted peppermint oil can ease tension headaches. Its cooling aroma makes a stuffy nose feel clearer and gives a refreshing lift when energy dips. As a tea it is a pleasant, caffeine-free way to settle the stomach after meals.

Overview

Peppermint is a natural cross between watermint and spearmint. Its high menthol content gives the cooling sensation it is famous for. Enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules are among the better-studied herbal products for bloating and discomfort associated with irritable bowel syndrome.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Relaxes intestinal muscle, easing spasms, gas and bloating.
  • Nervous system: Cools and dulls pain signals when applied to the skin.
  • Respiratory system: Creates a feeling of clearer airflow.

How it works chemically

  • Menthol (Monoterpene alcohol): Activates TRPM8 'cold' receptors (the cooling feeling) and blocks calcium channels in gut smooth muscle so it can't contract as hard.
  • Menthone (Monoterpene ketone): Adds to the antispasmodic and antimicrobial effect.
  • Rosmarinic acid (Polyphenol): Antioxidant and mildly anti-allergic.

Benefits explained

Bloating and IBS relief (Well researched)

Menthol relaxes the muscles of the intestines by blocking calcium channels, easing spasms. Many clinical trials and reviews show enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules reduce abdominal pain and bloating in irritable bowel syndrome.

Tension headaches (Some research)

Diluted peppermint oil rubbed on the temples creates a cooling sensation that dampens pain signals. In small studies, it relieved tension headaches about as well as a standard dose of acetaminophen.

Feeling of clearer breathing (Traditional use)

Menthol stimulates cold receptors in the nose, creating the sensation of air flowing more freely. It doesn't physically open the airways, but it can make a stuffy nose feel much more comfortable.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 180–200 mg (Enteric-coated peppermint oil), 3 times a day, before meals.

The herb by itself: 1.5–3 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Easing bloating and indigestion after a heavy meal
  • Steam inhalation to help clear a stuffy nose
  • A caffeine-free pick-me-up when energy dips in the afternoon

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons of dried leaf or a small handful of fresh leaves in hot water for 5–7 minutes. Add fresh leaves to water, fruit salads and iced tea.

Safety & cautions

May worsen heartburn or acid reflux. Never apply peppermint oil near the face of infants or young children, as menthol can affect their breathing.
