Thyme

Thymus vulgaris · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Breathing & Throat, Immune Support, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Thyme is a time-honored remedy for coughs and chest congestion. Its thymol and carvacrol relax the airways and help loosen mucus so coughs clear more easily. These same compounds are powerful germ-fighters, which is why thymol is used in mouthwash. A warm thyme tea with honey soothes sore throats during colds. It is approved in Germany for symptoms of bronchitis.

Overview

Thyme was burned as incense by the ancient Greeks and placed under pillows in the Middle Ages to ward off nightmares. Its oil is rich in thymol, an antiseptic compound used in some mouthwashes. Thyme is a traditional European remedy for coughs and is approved in Germany for symptoms of bronchitis.

What it does in your body

  • Respiratory system: Loosens mucus and calms coughing.
  • Immune system: May help fight germs in the mouth and throat.
  • Digestive system: Relieves gas.

How it works chemically

  • Thymol (Phenolic monoterpene): Breaks open bacterial and fungal cell membranes and relaxes the smooth muscle of the airways.
  • Carvacrol (Phenolic monoterpene): Strong antimicrobial that also helps the tiny hairs (cilia) in the airways clear mucus.
  • Luteolin and other flavonoids (Flavonoids): Anti-inflammatory and antispasmodic.

Benefits explained

Cough relief (Some research)

Thymol and carvacrol relax the airway muscles and help loosen mucus. Clinical trials of thyme combined with ivy leaf found people with acute bronchitis stopped coughing sooner than with placebo.

May help fight germs (Some research)

Thyme oil is strongly antimicrobial in laboratory studies. Thymol is the active ingredient in some well-known antiseptic mouthwashes.

Sore throats (Traditional use)

Warm thyme tea with honey is a traditional European throat remedy, and thyme is officially approved in Germany for symptoms of bronchitis.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1000 mg (Powdered thyme herb), 2–3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried herb steeped as tea (per cup)), 3–4 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A warm tea with honey to soothe a cough and loosen chest congestion
  • A gargle for a scratchy throat
  • Seasoning soups, stews, beans and roasted vegetables

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons of dried thyme in hot water, covered, for 10 minutes. Sweeten with honey.

Safety & cautions

Food amounts are safe. Thyme oil is strong and should never be swallowed or used undiluted. Avoid large amounts if allergic to oregano or other mints.
