Astragalus

Astragalus membranaceus · Fabaceae (pea family) · Immune Support, Energy & Focus

Benefits at a glance

Astragalus is one of the most important tonic roots in Chinese medicine. Its polysaccharides activate immune cells and support the body's defenses. Studies found it reduced fatigue, including in people receiving cancer treatment. Research is exploring its protective effects on the heart and kidneys. It is easy to use by simmering a few slices in soups and broths.

Overview

Astragalus (huang qi) is one of the most important tonic herbs in Chinese medicine, used for over 2,000 years to strengthen the body's defenses and energy. Its flat, sliced roots look like tongue depressors and are often simmered into soups.

What it does in your body

  • Immune system: Activates immune cells.
  • Energy: Reduces fatigue.
  • Heart & kidneys: Studied for protective effects.

How it works chemically

  • Astragalus polysaccharides (APS) (Polysaccharides): Activate macrophages and other immune cells through Toll-like receptor 4.
  • Astragalosides (astragaloside IV) (Triterpene saponins): Anti-inflammatory and protective for heart and kidney cells.
  • Formononetin & calycosin (Isoflavones): Antioxidant and support blood vessels.

Benefits explained

Immune support (Some research)

Astragalus polysaccharides stimulate immune cells in laboratory studies, supporting its traditional use for building resistance.

Energy and fatigue (Some research)

Studies, mostly from China, found astragalus reduced fatigue, including in people receiving cancer treatment.

Heart and kidneys (Some research)

Research in China has explored astragalus for heart and kidney health, with early promising results.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 250–500 mg (Astragalus root extract), 2–3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 3–10 g (Sliced root simmered in soup or tea), Once a day.

Traditional uses

  • Long-term support for immune resilience
  • Adding to bone broths and soups during winter
  • Traditional support for energy and stamina

How to prepare

Add 2–3 slices of dried root to a pot of soup or broth while it simmers, and remove before serving.

Safety & cautions

Avoid with autoimmune conditions and immune-suppressing medicines (including after transplants). Some wild Astragalus species are toxic to livestock — use only A. membranaceus from trusted sources.
