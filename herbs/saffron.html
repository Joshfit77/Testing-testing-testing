Saffron

Crocus sativus · Iridaceae (iris family) · Calm & Mood, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Saffron is the world's most precious spice and a well-studied mood booster. Reviews of trials found 30 mg of saffron daily improved mild to moderate low mood. It also reduced PMS symptoms such as irritability in trials. Its crocin may protect the retina and support vision. It brings golden color and aroma to rice and desserts.

Overview

Each crocus flower gives just three red stigmas, and around 150,000 flowers are needed for a kilogram of saffron. It has been used in Persian, Indian and Mediterranean cooking for millennia, and studies suggest saffron extract may support mood.

What it does in your body

  • Brain: Lifts low mood.
  • Hormonal / mood: Eases PMS symptoms.
  • Eyes: Protects the retina.

How it works chemically

  • Crocin (Carotenoid): Antioxidant that may increase serotonin activity and protect nerve and retina cells.
  • Safranal (Monoterpene aldehyde): The aroma; acts on GABA-A receptors and has anti-anxiety effects.
  • Picrocrocin (Glycoside): Gives the bitter taste; breaks down into safranal.

Benefits explained

Mood support (Well researched)

Reviews of clinical trials found saffron extract (about 30 mg daily) improved symptoms of mild to moderate depression compared with placebo, and similarly to some antidepressants.

PMS (Some research)

Trials found saffron reduced PMS symptoms such as irritability and mood swings.

Eye health (Some research)

Crocin and safranal may protect the retina; a small trial found improved vision in early macular degeneration.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 30 mg (Saffron extract (or 15 mg twice a day)), Once a day.

The herb by itself: 0.03–0.1 g (Saffron threads soaked in warm water or milk), Once a day.

Traditional uses

  • Golden color and aroma in paella, risotto and biryani
  • Traditional support for mood and emotional wellbeing
  • Saffron milk and desserts

How to prepare

Soak a pinch of threads in a few tablespoons of warm water or milk for 15 minutes, then add with the liquid.

Safety & cautions

Culinary amounts are safe. Large amounts (5 grams or more) are toxic, and high doses should be avoided in pregnancy. Beware of cheap fakes made from safflower or dyed threads.
