Holy Basil (Tulsi)

Ocimum tenuiflorum · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Calm & Mood, Immune Support, Energy & Focus

Benefits at a glance

Holy basil, or tulsi, is an adaptogen that helps the body cope with everyday stress. Studies found it reduced stress and anxiety and improved sleep after six to eight weeks. It may help lower blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes. Tulsi supports the body during coughs and colds and fights germs in lab studies. It makes a calming, caffeine-free daily tea with a warm, clove-like flavor.

Overview

Tulsi is grown in courtyards across India and honored in Hindu tradition. In Ayurveda it is considered an adaptogen — an herb thought to help the body cope with everyday stress. Small human studies have examined its effects on stress, mood and blood sugar.

What it does in your body

  • Stress response (adrenal): Helps moderate cortisol and stress.
  • Metabolism: May lower blood sugar.
  • Immune & respiratory: Supports defenses during colds.

How it works chemically

  • Eugenol (Phenylpropanoid): Anti-inflammatory (COX inhibition) and antimicrobial.
  • Ursolic acid (Triterpene): Anti-inflammatory and may improve insulin sensitivity.
  • Rosmarinic acid (Polyphenol): Antioxidant and anti-anxiety effects in studies.

Benefits explained

Stress resilience (Some research)

Clinical trials found tulsi reduced stress, anxiety and low-mood scores and improved sleep after six to eight weeks. It is thought to help regulate cortisol, the body's main stress hormone.

Healthy blood sugar (Some research)

Small studies show tulsi may lower fasting and after-meal blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes when used alongside their usual care.

Breathing and immunity (Traditional use)

Ayurveda uses tulsi for coughs and colds. Laboratory studies show antimicrobial and immune-balancing effects.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 300–500 mg (Holy basil (tulsi) leaf extract), 2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 2–3 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), 1–2 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A daily tea for calm, steady energy during stressful seasons
  • Warm tea with ginger and honey during colds
  • A caffeine-free morning alternative to coffee

How to prepare

Steep 1–2 teaspoons of dried leaf in hot water for 5–10 minutes. Blends beautifully with green tea, mint or rose.

Safety & cautions

Avoid during pregnancy or if trying to conceive. It may lower blood sugar and slow clotting — check with your doctor if you take diabetes medicine or blood thinners.
