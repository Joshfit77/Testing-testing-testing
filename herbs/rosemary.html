Rosemary

Salvia rosmarinus · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Energy & Focus, Skin & Hair, Digestion, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Rosemary is the herb of remembrance, linked with alertness and memory. Its aroma contains 1,8-cineole, which may help the brain keep more of the memory messenger acetylcholine. Rosemary oil massaged into the scalp regrew hair about as well as minoxidil in one study. It aids the digestion of rich, fatty foods. Its carnosic acid is also a strong antioxidant that helps protect the body's cells.

Overview

Ancient Greek students wore rosemary garlands while studying, and Shakespeare's Ophelia says 'there's rosemary, that's for remembrance.' Modern research has explored the effects of its aroma on alertness and memory, and rosemary oil has been studied for supporting hair growth.

What it does in your body

  • Nervous system: Supports alertness and memory.
  • Skin & hair: Improves scalp circulation for hair growth.
  • Digestive system: Stimulates digestion of fats.

How it works chemically

  • 1,8-Cineole (Monoterpene oxide): Enters the blood when inhaled and inhibits acetylcholinesterase, which may leave more of the memory messenger acetylcholine.
  • Carnosic acid (Diterpene): Powerful antioxidant that switches on Nrf2 protection in cells.
  • Rosmarinic acid (Polyphenol): Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Alertness and memory (Some research)

1,8-cineole from rosemary's aroma is absorbed into the bloodstream and may influence brain chemistry. Small studies linked breathing in rosemary aroma with better performance on memory and alertness tasks.

Hair growth (Some research)

In one six-month trial, rosemary oil massaged into the scalp regrew hair about as well as 2% minoxidil in people with pattern hair loss, with less scalp itching. It is thought to improve circulation to hair follicles.

Digesting rich foods (Traditional use)

Rosemary's bitter, aromatic compounds encourage digestive juices, which is why it pairs so naturally with roasts and potatoes.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1000 mg (Powdered rosemary leaf), 2 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Inhaling the scent while working or studying to feel more alert
  • A cooled rosemary-tea hair rinse for shine and scalp health
  • Aiding digestion of rich, fatty foods

How to prepare

Strip fresh needles into roasts, potatoes, breads and soups. For tea, steep 1 teaspoon dried leaf in hot water for 5–10 minutes.

Safety & cautions

Safe in cooking. Avoid medicinal doses during pregnancy, and avoid the essential oil if you have epilepsy.
