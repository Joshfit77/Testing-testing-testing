Calendula

Calendula officinalis · Asteraceae (daisy family) · Skin & Hair

Benefits at a glance

Calendula is a sunny flower that is one of the best herbs for skin healing. Its triterpenoids encourage new tissue growth and help wounds heal faster. Calendula ointment improved diaper rash and reduced skin irritation during radiation therapy in studies. It calms eczema, chapped skin, cuts and insect bites. It is gentle enough for baby balms and everyday skin care.

Overview

Calendula has been used since the 12th century to soothe skin and was used to dress wounds during the American Civil War and World War I. Its resinous petals are a common ingredient in baby balms, nappy creams and natural skin care.

What it does in your body

  • Skin: Speeds wound healing and soothes inflammation.
  • Mouth: Soothes mouth sores (rinse).
  • Digestive system: Traditionally soothing to the gut lining.

How it works chemically

  • Faradiol esters (Triterpenoids): The main anti-inflammatory compounds; reduce swelling in skin.
  • Polysaccharides (Complex sugars): Encourage new blood vessels and tissue growth in wounds.
  • Carotenoids & flavonoids (Pigments / polyphenols): Antioxidant protection for healing skin.

Benefits explained

Wound healing (Some research)

Calendula's triterpenoids encourage new tissue and blood vessel growth. Studies found calendula ointment helped wounds heal and reduced skin irritation during radiation therapy.

Diaper rash (Some research)

In one study of babies, calendula ointment improved diaper rash more than aloe gel.

Calming sensitive skin (Traditional use)

Used in balms for eczema, chapped skin and minor irritation for centuries.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly taken as capsules — mainly used on the skin.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried petals steeped as tea or mouth rinse (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Balms for dry, chapped or irritated skin
  • Soothing minor cuts, scrapes and insect bites
  • Brightening salads and rice with edible petals

How to prepare

Infuse dried petals in olive oil for 4–6 weeks in a warm place, strain, then melt with beeswax to make a salve.

Safety & cautions

Avoid if allergic to the daisy family. Not for internal use during pregnancy. Do not apply to deep or infected wounds.
