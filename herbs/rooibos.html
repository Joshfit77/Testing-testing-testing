Rooibos

Aspalathus linearis · Fabaceae (pea family) · Calm & Mood, Culinary, Heart & Circulation

Benefits at a glance

Rooibos is a naturally caffeine-free red tea from South Africa. It contains unique antioxidants, aspalathin and nothofagin, found almost nowhere else. A small study found six cups a day improved cholesterol and antioxidant levels. It is low in tannins and gentle enough for evenings and children. Its naturally sweet, nutty flavor makes it a delicious everyday tea.

Overview

Rooibos grows only in the Cederberg region of South Africa. The needle-like leaves are fermented to give their red color and sweet taste. It is naturally caffeine-free, low in tannins and rich in unique antioxidants such as aspalathin.

What it does in your body

  • Whole body: Antioxidant protection.
  • Heart: May improve cholesterol.
  • Nervous system: Caffeine-free relaxation.

How it works chemically

  • Aspalathin (Dihydrochalcone): Unique to rooibos; antioxidant and may help muscle cells take up glucose (via AMPK).
  • Nothofagin (Dihydrochalcone): Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.
  • Quercetin & luteolin (Flavonoids): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Antioxidants (Some research)

Rooibos contains aspalathin and nothofagin, antioxidants found almost nowhere else, which protect cells from oxidative stress.

Heart health (Some research)

In a small study, adults who drank six cups of rooibos daily for six weeks had improved cholesterol and antioxidant levels.

Caffeine-free calm (Traditional use)

Naturally caffeine-free and low in tannins, rooibos is a gentle drink for evenings, children and anyone avoiding caffeine.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — enjoy it as tea.

The herb by itself: 2–3 g (Dried leaf steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 6 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A caffeine-free tea any time of day or evening
  • A gentle drink suitable for the whole family
  • Red lattes with milk and honey

How to prepare

Steep 1 teaspoon in boiling water for 5–7 minutes — it won't go bitter, so longer is fine.

Safety & cautions

Very safe for most people. Rare reports suggest very large amounts could affect the liver; enjoy in normal amounts.
