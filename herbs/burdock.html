Burdock Root

Arctium lappa · Asteraceae (daisy family) · Skin & Hair, Digestion, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Burdock root is a traditional herb for clear, healthy skin. It has long been used for acne, eczema and boils as a gentle cleansing herb. It is rich in inulin, a prebiotic fiber that feeds good gut bacteria and supports regularity. Its antioxidants, such as quercetin and luteolin, help protect cells. In Japan it is enjoyed as a nutritious root vegetable called gobo.

Overview

Burdock's clinging burrs inspired the invention of Velcro. In Japan, the root (gobo) is a popular vegetable. In Western herbalism it is a classic 'blood cleanser,' traditionally used for skin conditions such as acne and eczema. It contains inulin, a prebiotic fiber.

What it does in your body

  • Skin: Traditionally clears acne and eczema.
  • Gut: Prebiotic fiber feeds good bacteria.
  • Whole body: Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.

How it works chemically

  • Inulin (Prebiotic fiber): Feeds bifidobacteria, which produce short-chain fatty acids that calm inflammation.
  • Arctigenin & arctiin (Lignans): Anti-inflammatory: block NF-κB and inflammatory messengers.
  • Chlorogenic acid (Polyphenol): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Clearer skin (Traditional use)

Burdock is a traditional 'blood purifier' used for acne, eczema and boils. Evidence is mainly traditional, but its anti-inflammatory compounds may help.

Prebiotic fiber (Some research)

Burdock root is rich in inulin, which feeds beneficial gut bacteria and supports regularity.

Antioxidant (Some research)

Burdock contains quercetin, luteolin and phenolic acids that protect cells from oxidative damage.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1000 mg (Powdered burdock root), 2–3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried root simmered as tea (per cup)), 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditionally used for skin troubled by acne or dryness
  • Prebiotic fiber to feed healthy gut bacteria
  • Stir-fried, pickled or simmered as a root vegetable

How to prepare

Simmer 1 teaspoon of dried root in 2 cups of water for 15 minutes. Scrub fresh root and slice thinly for kinpira gobo.

Safety & cautions

Wild burdock can be confused with toxic belladonna root — buy from trusted sources. Avoid with daisy allergies, during pregnancy, and with diuretics or diabetes medicine.
