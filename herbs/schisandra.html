Schisandra

Schisandra chinensis · Schisandraceae · Energy & Focus, Skin & Hair

Benefits at a glance

Schisandra is the five-flavor berry of Chinese tradition. As an adaptogen, it helps the body resist physical and mental stress and improves endurance. Its lignans protect liver cells and boost the antioxidant glutathione. Studies suggest it supports concentration and mental performance. It has long been taken to nourish radiant, healthy skin.

Overview

In Chinese, schisandra is wu wei zi — the five-flavor fruit — because it is sweet, sour, salty, bitter and pungent at once. It's a classic adaptogen in Chinese and Russian traditions, traditionally used for stamina, focus and radiant skin.

What it does in your body

  • Stress response: Improves endurance and resistance to stress.
  • Liver: Protects liver cells.
  • Brain: Supports concentration.

How it works chemically

  • Schisandrin A & B (Dibenzocyclooctadiene lignans): Protect liver cells, boost glutathione, and adjust stress hormones.
  • Gomisins (Lignans): Liver-protective; also change how the liver processes medicines (CYP enzymes).
  • Organic acids (citric, malic) (Acids): Give the sour taste.

Benefits explained

Stress and endurance (Some research)

Schisandra's lignans may help the body adapt to physical and mental stress. Studies found improved endurance and concentration.

Liver protection (Some research)

Schisandrins protect liver cells in laboratory and animal studies, and a derivative is used in Chinese liver medicines.

Radiant skin (Traditional use)

Traditionally taken to nourish and brighten the complexion.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500–1000 mg (Schisandra berry extract), Once a day.

The herb by itself: 1.5–3 g (Dried berries steeped as tea (per cup)), 1–2 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Supporting endurance and focus
  • A traditional tonic for beautiful skin
  • Tart berry tea or syrup

How to prepare

Soak 1 teaspoon of crushed berries in hot water for 10–15 minutes, or use powders and tinctures.

Safety & cautions

Avoid during pregnancy and with epilepsy or severe acid reflux. It can change how the liver processes many medicines — check with your pharmacist.
