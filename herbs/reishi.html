Reishi Mushroom

Ganoderma lucidum · Ganodermataceae · Immune Support, Calm & Mood, Restful Sleep

Benefits at a glance

Reishi is a glossy mushroom revered for longevity in Asian tradition. Its beta-glucans help balance immune activity and support natural killer cells. Small studies found it reduced fatigue and improved wellbeing. Its triterpenes calm inflammation and may help lower blood pressure. It is traditionally used to calm the spirit and support restful sleep.

Overview

Reishi (lingzhi in Chinese) has appeared in Chinese art and medicine for over 2,000 years as a symbol of longevity. It's a woody mushroom that is too tough to eat, so it's made into tea or extracts. Research has focused on its effects on the immune system.

What it does in your body

  • Immune system: Balances immune activity.
  • Energy: Reduces fatigue.
  • Nervous system: Traditionally calming.

How it works chemically

  • β-Glucans (Polysaccharides): Bind dectin-1 receptors on immune cells, increasing the activity of natural killer cells and macrophages.
  • Ganoderic acids (Triterpenes): Very bitter; anti-inflammatory, may reduce histamine release and lower blood pressure (ACE inhibition).
  • Peptidoglycans (Protein-sugar complexes): Immune-modulating.

Benefits explained

Immune balance (Some research)

Reishi's beta-glucans and triterpenes appear to increase the activity of certain white blood cells in some studies.

Fatigue and wellbeing (Some research)

Small studies found reishi reduced fatigue and improved wellbeing.

Calm and sleep (Traditional use)

In Chinese medicine, reishi is considered calming for the spirit and is used for restless sleep.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 1000–1500 mg (Reishi mushroom extract), Once a day.

The herb by itself: 1.5–3 g (Dried slices simmered 1–2 hours as tea), 1–3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional support for the immune system
  • Encouraging calm and restful sleep
  • Adding to coffee, cocoa and broth blends

How to prepare

Simmer a few slices of dried reishi in water for 1–2 hours to make a deep, bitter tea, or use extract powders.

Safety & cautions

May slow blood clotting and lower blood pressure — avoid before surgery and with blood thinners. Rare reports of liver problems with reishi powder. Avoid during pregnancy.
