Turmeric

Curcuma longa · Zingiberaceae (ginger family) · Aches & Joints, Digestion, Skin & Hair, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Turmeric is a golden root best known for its anti-inflammatory compound curcumin. Curcumin calms the body's inflammation signals, and studies suggest it can ease knee arthritis pain for some people. It is a powerful antioxidant that boosts the body's own protective enzymes. Turmeric also supports digestion and the flow of bile. Taking it with black pepper and a little fat greatly increases how much the body absorbs.

Overview

Turmeric has been central to Ayurvedic tradition and South Asian cooking for millennia, and is also used as a dye. Its main active compound, curcumin, is poorly absorbed on its own; pairing it with black pepper and a little fat improves absorption considerably.

What it does in your body

  • Musculoskeletal system: Eases joint pain and stiffness.
  • Digestive system: Stimulates bile flow and digestion.
  • Whole body: Protects cells as an antioxidant.

How it works chemically

  • Curcumin (Curcuminoid (polyphenol)): Switches off NF-κB, a master control for inflammation, lowers COX-2, and switches on Nrf2, the cell's antioxidant defense.
  • Demethoxycurcumins (Curcuminoids): Similar but milder anti-inflammatory activity.
  • Turmerones (Sesquiterpenes): Aromatic oils that may help curcumin absorb and support brain cells.

Benefits explained

Joint comfort (Some research)

Curcumin turns down inflammatory signals in the body (such as NF-κB). Several clinical trials found curcumin extracts eased knee osteoarthritis pain about as well as ibuprofen for some people, though products and doses varied.

Antioxidant protection (Some research)

Curcumin neutralizes free radicals and boosts the body's own antioxidant enzymes. On its own it is poorly absorbed, so it's best taken with black pepper and a little fat.

Digestive support (Traditional use)

In Ayurveda, turmeric is a warming spice that supports digestion and bile flow. Some studies suggest it may ease indigestion, although it can aggravate reflux in some people.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 500 mg (Curcumin extract with black pepper (piperine)), 1–2 times a day with food.

The herb by itself: 0.5–1 g (Ground turmeric in food or warm milk), 2–3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Comfort for achy joints as part of a balanced diet
  • Supporting healthy digestion
  • Traditional face masks with yogurt and honey for a glowing complexion

How to prepare

Add 1/2–1 teaspoon ground turmeric to curries, rice, eggs and soups with a pinch of black pepper. For golden milk, warm milk with turmeric, cinnamon, ginger and honey.

Safety & cautions

High-dose supplements have rare reports of liver injury. Avoid medicinal amounts with gallbladder problems, before surgery, or if taking blood thinners or diabetes medicine. It will stain skin, clothes and counters.
