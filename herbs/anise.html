Anise

Pimpinella anisum · Apiaceae (carrot family) · Digestion, Breathing & Throat, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Anise is a sweet, licorice-flavored seed that soothes digestion. Its anethole relaxes the digestive tract, and a trial found it eased ongoing indigestion. It is a traditional expectorant that helps loosen mucus in coughs. Small studies suggest it may reduce hot flushes and period cramps. Anise tea after meals is a gentle, pleasant way to settle the stomach.

Overview

Aniseed has flavored Mediterranean foods and drinks — from Greek ouzo to Italian biscotti — for thousands of years. Romans served anise cakes after feasts to aid digestion. Like fennel, it contains anethole.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Relieves indigestion and gas.
  • Respiratory system: Loosens mucus.
  • Hormonal system: Weak estrogen-like effects.

How it works chemically

  • trans-Anethole (Phenylpropanoid): Antispasmodic on gut muscle, expectorant, and weakly estrogenic.
  • Anisaldehyde (Aldehyde): Antimicrobial and aromatic.
  • Estragole (Phenylpropanoid): Minor flavor compound; avoid concentrated amounts.

Benefits explained

Indigestion (Some research)

Anethole relaxes the digestive tract. A clinical trial found anise powder improved symptoms of functional dyspepsia (ongoing indigestion).

Loosening coughs (Traditional use)

Anise is a traditional expectorant, helping to thin and loosen mucus so coughs are more productive.

Menopause and period pain (Some research)

Small studies suggest anise may reduce hot flushes and menstrual cramps, likely due to weak estrogen-like effects.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the seed as tea.

The herb by itself: 1 g (Crushed seed steeped as tea (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • A tea after meals to ease gas and bloating
  • A traditional ingredient in cough syrups
  • Flavoring biscotti, breads and stewed fruit

How to prepare

Crush 1 teaspoon of seeds and steep in hot water for 10 minutes.

Safety & cautions

May cause allergy in those sensitive to fennel, celery or carrot. Has weak estrogen-like effects. Do not use the essential oil internally.
