Lovage

Levisticum officinale · Apiaceae (carrot family) · Culinary, Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Lovage is a tall, celery-flavored herb that supports digestion. Its oils relieve gas and help the digestive system work smoothly. Lovage root is approved in Germany for flushing the urinary tract. Its leaves are one of the richest plant sources of the antioxidant quercetin. A little goes a long way in soups and stews.

Overview

Lovage was a staple of medieval monastery gardens and Roman kitchens. Its leaves, stems, roots and seeds are all edible and taste of strong celery. It was traditionally used as a digestive and diuretic herb.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Relieves gas.
  • Urinary tract: Gently increases urine flow.
  • Whole body: Antioxidant.

How it works chemically

  • Ligustilide & butylidenephthalide (Phthalides): Relax smooth muscle and increase urine output; give the celery aroma.
  • Furanocoumarins (Coumarins): Can make skin sensitive to sunlight.
  • Quercetin (Flavonoid): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Digestion (Traditional use)

Lovage's aromatic oils relieve gas and support digestion.

Urinary flushing (Traditional use)

Lovage root is approved in Germany for flushing the urinary tract.

Antioxidant (Some research)

Lovage leaves are one of the richest plant sources of quercetin, an antioxidant flavonoid.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not commonly sold as capsules — use the root as tea.

The herb by itself: 1.5–3 g (Dried root steeped as tea (per cup)), 2–3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Flavoring soups, stocks, potato salad and stews
  • A traditional digestive and gentle diuretic
  • Hollow stems used as straws for Bloody Marys

How to prepare

Use young leaves sparingly — a little goes a long way. Add to soup stocks in place of celery.

Safety & cautions

Food amounts are safe. Avoid medicinal amounts with kidney problems and during pregnancy. May increase sensitivity to sunlight.
