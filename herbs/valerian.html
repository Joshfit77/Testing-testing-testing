Valerian

Valeriana officinalis · Caprifoliaceae (honeysuckle family) · Restful Sleep, Calm & Mood

Benefits at a glance

Valerian is one of the oldest herbal remedies for sleep. Its valerenic acid works on the brain's calming GABA system, much like some sleep medicines. Some studies show it helps people fall asleep faster and sleep more deeply. It may also ease anxious feelings and nervous tension. It tends to work best after two to four weeks of regular use.

Overview

Valerian has been used as a sleep aid since the time of Hippocrates. Its root smells notoriously like old socks — but cats love it. Some studies suggest it can improve sleep quality, especially when taken regularly for a few weeks.

What it does in your body

  • Nervous system: Helps you fall asleep and improves sleep quality.
  • Nervous system: Eases anxiety.
  • Muscles: Relaxes tension and cramps.

How it works chemically

  • Valerenic acid (Sesquiterpene): Binds GABA-A receptors (β2/β3 subunits) to boost calming signals and slows GABA breakdown.
  • Valepotriates (Iridoids): Mildly sedative; break down quickly in storage.
  • Isovaleric acid (Fatty acid): Responsible for the strong smell; mildly relaxant.

Benefits explained

Falling asleep (Some research)

Valerenic acid acts on GABA receptors in the brain, the same calming system targeted by sleep medicines. Some studies show faster sleep and better sleep quality, though results are mixed.

Easing anxiety (Some research)

Small studies found valerian reduced anxious feelings, particularly in stressful situations.

Muscle tension (Traditional use)

Valerian has traditionally been used to ease muscle cramps and tension.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 300–600 mg (Valerian root extract), Once, 30–120 minutes before bed.

The herb by itself: 2–3 g (Dried root steeped as tea (per cup)), Once, 30–60 minutes before bed.

Traditional uses

  • Helping you fall asleep more easily
  • Easing nervous tension
  • Often combined with hops or lemon balm

How to prepare

Steep 1 teaspoon of dried root in hot water for 10–15 minutes and drink 30–60 minutes before bed. Many prefer capsules due to the smell.

Safety & cautions

Causes drowsiness: don't drive after taking it, and don't combine with alcohol or sleep medicines. Avoid during pregnancy and in young children. Stop before surgery.
