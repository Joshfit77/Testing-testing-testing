Saw Palmetto

Serenoa repens · Arecaceae (palm family) · Men's Health

Benefits at a glance

Saw palmetto is a palm berry traditionally used for men's prostate health. It may block the enzyme that turns testosterone into DHT. Early studies suggested it eased urinary symptoms, though larger trials found smaller benefits. Small studies suggest it may help slow hair thinning. It was eaten as a food and tonic by Indigenous peoples of Florida.

Overview

Saw palmetto is a small palm native to the southeastern United States, where the Seminole people ate its berries. Its extract became a popular supplement for urinary symptoms of an enlarged prostate, though large studies have produced mixed results.

What it does in your body

  • Prostate: May ease urinary symptoms of an enlarged prostate.
  • Hair follicles: May slow hair thinning.
  • Hormonal system: Reduces conversion of testosterone to DHT.

How it works chemically

  • Free fatty acids (lauric, oleic, myristic) (Fatty acids): Inhibit 5-α-reductase, the enzyme that converts testosterone to DHT.
  • β-Sitosterol (Phytosterol): May reduce inflammation in prostate tissue.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Urinary symptoms in men (Some research)

Saw palmetto may block the enzyme that converts testosterone to DHT. Early studies were promising, but large trials found little benefit over placebo for enlarged prostate symptoms.

Thinning hair (Some research)

Small studies suggest saw palmetto, taken by mouth or applied to the scalp, may improve hair density in pattern hair loss.

Traditional tonic (Traditional use)

Indigenous peoples of Florida ate the berries as a food and tonic.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 160–320 mg (Saw palmetto berry extract), 160 mg twice a day, or 320 mg once.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Whole dried berries, ground), Once a day.

Traditional uses

  • Traditional support for men's prostate and urinary health
  • Sometimes used in hair-thinning products
  • Historically used as a food by Indigenous peoples

How to prepare

Use standardized berry extract capsules as directed.

Safety & cautions

See a doctor to rule out other conditions before using for urinary symptoms. May cause stomach upset. Avoid during pregnancy, and check with your doctor if you take hormonal medicines or blood thinners.
