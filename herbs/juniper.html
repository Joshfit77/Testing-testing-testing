Juniper Berry

Juniperus communis · Cupressaceae (cypress family) · Digestion, Culinary

Benefits at a glance

Juniper berries are piney, aromatic cones that support digestion. Their bitter compounds stimulate digestive juices, especially with rich foods. They are a traditional diuretic used for water retention. Juniper berries contain antioxidant flavonoids. They should only be used in small amounts for short periods.

Overview

Juniper 'berries' are actually small, fleshy cones. They give gin its name and flavor (from the Dutch jenever) and are used in sauerkraut and with game meats across Europe. Traditionally, they were used for digestion and as a diuretic.

What it does in your body

  • Digestive system: Stimulates digestion.
  • Kidneys: Increases urine flow.
  • Whole body: Antioxidant.

How it works chemically

  • Terpinen-4-ol (Monoterpene alcohol): Increases filtration in the kidneys (diuretic) — and can irritate them in excess.
  • α-Pinene & myrcene (Monoterpenes): Antimicrobial and give the pine aroma.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Antioxidant.

Benefits explained

Digestion (Traditional use)

Juniper's bitter, aromatic compounds stimulate digestion, which is why it is paired with rich meats and cabbage.

Diuretic (Traditional use)

Terpinen-4-ol in juniper increases urine flow; it was traditionally used for water retention (short-term only).

Antioxidant (Some research)

Juniper berries contain antioxidant flavonoids and essential oils.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Not recommended as capsules — use berries short-term in tea or food.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Crushed dried berries steeped as tea (per cup) — max 4 weeks), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Seasoning sauerkraut, venison, pork and stews
  • A traditional digestive aid in small amounts
  • Freshening rooms with their resinous scent

How to prepare

Lightly crush 3–5 berries before adding to marinades or braises.

Safety & cautions

Use only in culinary amounts or for short periods. Avoid with kidney disease, during pregnancy, and with diabetes medicine or diuretics.
