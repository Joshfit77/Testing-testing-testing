White Horehound

Marrubium vulgare · Lamiaceae (mint family) · Breathing & Throat, Digestion

Benefits at a glance

Horehound is a bitter herb famous in old-fashioned cough candy. Its marrubiin helps thin mucus so coughs become more productive. It is approved in Germany for coughs and loss of appetite. As a strong bitter, it stimulates digestive juices. Horehound lozenges remain a classic remedy for sore throats.

Overview

Horehound candy has been a cough remedy since the 1800s, and the herb itself was one of the bitter herbs of Passover. It contains marrubiin, which is thought to help loosen mucus. It's approved in Germany for loss of appetite and bloating.

What it does in your body

  • Respiratory system: Thins mucus and calms coughing.
  • Digestive system: Stimulates appetite and digestion.
  • Throat: Soothing (lozenges).

How it works chemically

  • Marrubiin (Diterpene lactone): Stimulates secretions in the airways (expectorant) and bitter receptors that start digestion.
  • Premarrubiin (Diterpene): Converts to marrubiin.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Mild anti-inflammatory.

Benefits explained

Coughs (Some research)

Marrubiin helps thin mucus. Horehound is approved in Germany for coughs and loss of appetite.

Appetite and digestion (Traditional use)

As a strong bitter, horehound stimulates digestive juices and appetite.

Soothing throats (Traditional use)

Horehound candies and syrups have been used for sore throats since the 1800s.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Usually taken as lozenges or tea rather than capsules.

The herb by itself: 1–2 g (Dried herb steeped as tea (per cup)), 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Horehound lozenges or syrup for coughs
  • A bitter tea to stimulate appetite
  • Easing bloating and fullness

How to prepare

Steep 1 teaspoon dried herb for 10 minutes and sweeten generously with honey.

Safety & cautions

Avoid during pregnancy. May lower blood sugar and affect heart rhythm in large amounts.
