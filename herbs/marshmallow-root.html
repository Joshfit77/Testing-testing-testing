Marshmallow Root

Althaea officinalis · Malvaceae (mallow family) · Digestion, Breathing & Throat, Skin & Hair

Benefits at a glance

Marshmallow root is one of the most soothing herbs for irritated tissues. Its mucilage forms a protective film that calms dry, scratchy coughs. It also coats and comforts an irritated stomach lining and heartburn. As a poultice, it softens and soothes dry skin. A cold infusion draws out the most of its silky, healing gel.

Overview

The first marshmallow sweets were made in ancient Egypt from this root's sap mixed with honey. The root is rich in mucilage, a gel-like substance that coats and soothes irritated tissues in the throat and gut.

What it does in your body

  • Throat: Coats and soothes a dry cough.
  • Digestive system: Protects an irritated stomach lining.
  • Skin: Softens and soothes.

How it works chemically

  • Mucilage polysaccharides (Rhamnogalacturonans and arabinogalactans): Swell in water and stick to mucous membranes, forming a protective film that calms the cough reflex and irritation.
  • Pectin (Fiber): Adds to the soothing gel.
  • Flavonoids (Polyphenols): Mild anti-inflammatory effects.

Benefits explained

Dry coughs (Some research)

Marshmallow's mucilage forms a protective film over irritated membranes in the throat. A study found marshmallow syrup reduced dry, irritating coughs.

Calming the digestive tract (Traditional use)

Traditionally used to soothe heartburn, gastritis and an irritated gut lining.

Skin soothing (Traditional use)

Applied as a poultice to soften and calm dry, irritated skin.

How much to take (adults 100 lb and over)

Capsule form: 1000–2000 mg (Powdered marshmallow root (with a full glass of water)), 3 times a day.

The herb by itself: 2–5 g (Root soaked in cool water 4–8 hours (per cup)), Up to 3 times a day.

Traditional uses

  • Coating and soothing a dry, scratchy throat or cough
  • Comforting an irritated stomach lining
  • Soothing poultices for dry, irritated skin

How to prepare

Make a cold infusion: soak 1–2 tablespoons of chopped root in a jar of cool water for 4–8 hours, strain and drink. Cold water draws out more mucilage than hot.

Safety & cautions

The mucilage may slow absorption of other medicines; take it at least 1–2 hours apart. May lower blood sugar.
