Herbs & Fruits for Children

Children are not small adults — their bodies process herbs differently, and some products that are safe for grown-ups can be dangerous for them. Fresh fruits and gentle culinary herbs in food are wonderful for kids. Medicinal herbs, essential oils and supplements need a pediatrician's guidance.

Babies under 1 year

  • Never give honey — it can cause infant botulism.
  • Don't give herbal teas or remedies unless your pediatrician advises it.
  • Never use essential oils like eucalyptus or peppermint near a baby's face.
  • Breast milk or formula is all a baby under 6 months needs.

Toddlers and young children (1–5)

  • Cut grapes and cherry tomatoes lengthwise and remove pits and seeds to prevent choking.
  • Offer a rainbow of fresh fruits daily.
  • Weak chamomile tea is traditionally used, but ask your pediatrician first.
  • Keep all oils, supplements and medicines locked away.

School-age children (6–12)

  • Never give adult doses — ask your pediatrician for a child's amount.
  • Honey with warm water is a soothing remedy for coughs over age 1.
  • Encourage fruit as snacks instead of juice.

Teenagers

  • Avoid energy, 'fat-burner' and muscle-building supplements.
  • Talk openly about the risks of mixing supplements with medicines and alcohol.
  • Teens should follow adult guidance only with a doctor's okay.

What could happen & what to do

Children's bodies process herbs differently, and some — like eucalyptus oil, neem oil and star anise — can be dangerous even in small amounts.

Ask your pediatrician before giving any herb. Never give honey under age 1, never put essential oils near a young child's face, and keep all oils and supplements locked away.

Herbs & fruits to avoid

  • Tongkat Ali — Raises testosterone — not for anyone under 18.
  • Shilajit — Not for anyone under 18.
  • Mucuna (Velvet Bean) — Contains L-dopa, a medicine.
  • Tribulus — Hormonal effects; not for under 18.
  • Horny Goat Weed — Not for under 18.
  • Cordyceps — Not studied in children.
  • Eucalyptus — Oil is dangerous for young children — never near the face or swallowed.
  • Tea Tree — Toxic if swallowed.
  • Neem — Neem oil can be fatal to children.
  • Star Anise — Never give to babies.
  • Nutmeg — Toxic in larger amounts.
  • Meadowsweet — Salicylates — risk of Reye's syndrome under 16.
  • St. John's Wort — Not for children.
  • Asian Ginseng — Not for children.
  • Licorice Root — Avoid.
  • Lychee — Never give unripe lychee or lychee on an empty stomach.
  • Ackee — Unripe ackee is poisonous.

Use with caution

  • Maca — Fine as food; avoid supplements.
  • Peppermint — Never apply peppermint oil near a baby's or young child's face.
  • Chamomile — Weak tea is traditionally used — ask your pediatrician.
  • Valerian — Not recommended under 3.
  • Grape — Cut lengthwise for young children to prevent choking.
  • Sweet Cherry — Pit cherries for children.
  • Honeydew — Wash rind well.
  • Elderberry — Syrup with honey is not for babies under 1.

Get help right away if…

  • A child swallowed an essential oil, supplement or medicine — call Poison Control (U.S. 1-800-222-1222) right away
  • Trouble breathing, swelling of the lips or face, or hives
  • Unusual sleepiness, vomiting or seizures
