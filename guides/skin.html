Natural Care for Healthy Skin

Your skin reflects what you eat, how you sleep and how you protect it. Calendula and aloe soothe and heal, tea tree oil helps blemishes, and gotu kola supports collagen. From the inside, fruits rich in vitamin C and carotenoids help protect against sun damage and support repair.

Start with the basics

  • Wear sunscreen every day — it's the best anti-aging step there is.
  • Cleanse gently and moisturize while skin is damp.
  • Drink water and eat colorful fruits and vegetables.
  • Sleep 7–9 hours — skin repairs overnight.
  • Patch-test any new product on a small area first.

Herbs that may help

  • Calendula — Wound healing: Calendula's triterpenoids encourage new tissue and blood vessel growth. Studies found calendula ointment helped wounds heal and reduced skin irritation during radiation therapy.
  • Aloe Vera — Burns and sunburn: Studies found aloe gel helped first- and second-degree burns heal faster than some conventional dressings.
  • Tea Tree — Acne: In clinical trials, 5% tea tree oil gel reduced acne lesions compared with placebo. It works more slowly than benzoyl peroxide but with less dryness.
  • Gotu Kola — Skin repair: Asiaticoside and madecassoside stimulate collagen production. Gotu kola is used in creams for scars, stretch marks and sensitive skin.
  • Rose — Easing anxiety: Small studies found that rose oil aroma reduced anxiety and promoted relaxation.
  • Witch Hazel — Hemorrhoid relief: Witch hazel is an officially approved astringent for temporary relief of hemorrhoid itching and discomfort.

Fruits that help

  • Tomato
  • Mango
  • Avocado
  • Sea Buckthorn

Safety first

Tea tree oil must always be diluted and never swallowed. Calendula and other daisy-family herbs can cause allergy. Stop any product that causes redness or itching.

When to see a doctor

  • A mole that changes size, shape or color, or bleeds
  • A sore that doesn't heal in 3 weeks
  • Severe acne, eczema or a rash with fever
