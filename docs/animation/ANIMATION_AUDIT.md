# 2D exercise animation audit (2026-11-13)

All 218 catalog exercises, audited from their frames, with the corrections made in this pass. Machine-readable copy:
[`animation-audit.json`](animation-audit.json) (one record per exercise, with the measured numbers before and after).

**What this audit is and is not.** Each rating is a reviewer's judgement from the rendered frames plus measurements of
the motion (joint angles, foot and hand contact, bar position, frame-to-frame joint speed). It is not a coaching or
clinical sign-off. Nothing here is "anatomically correct" because it renders or because a test passes; the exercises
marked *needs professional review* should be checked by a qualified strength coach or physiotherapist before anyone
calls them correct. The athlete's design (proportions, colours, look) was not changed; only motion data, equipment
placement and framing were.

## Method

1. **Baseline.** `scripts/exercise-review/fingerprint.mjs --out` hashed every exercise's animation (72 samples of every
   landmark, joint angle and prop position). Contact sheets (`contact-sheets.mjs`, six phases per exercise) were rendered
   for all 218 and read one by one; every priority lift and every suspect was re-rendered close up at 4-8 phases.
2. **Measurements** on the frames the player draws (60 fps over one loop): joint ranges, the largest joint change between
   two frames, foot slide and drift while on the floor, how far the floor clamp had to lift the body (a body or limb
   going through the floor), hand-to-equipment distance, and the figure's size and any cropping on a 359 × 375 px
   phone stage.
3. **Ratings.** *Exercise-specific*: no = the motion shown is a different movement; partial = the joint motion is
   identical to another exercise's (same fingerprint) or the defining feature cannot be seen from the camera used.
   *Anatomical accuracy*: good / questionable (plausible but debatable or hard to read) / wrong. *Needs correction*:
   a concrete defect in the motion data, equipment or framing. *Needs professional review*: technique a coach or
   physiotherapist should confirm (high-skill lifts, loaded spinal flexion or end-range positions, depth and lean
   choices, anything drawn in a projection that hides part of the movement).
4. **Fixes** were made in the shared patterns, so every exercise built on a pattern got the same correction (one set
   of squat mechanics, one strict-press solver, one back-bar hold); `fingerprint.mjs --compare` confirms which
   exercises changed and that none disappeared.

## Remaining concerns (not fixed here)

- **Grip and forearm rotation cannot be seen from the side**: chin-up vs pull-up, wide vs normal grip, reverse-grip
  rows and curls, EZ-bar curl, Zottman curl, close-grip push-up, dumbbell squeeze press. Their joint motion is
  identical to the base exercise; a front view per family would be needed. Rated *partial*, not *wrong*.
- **Band external rotation** and **Pallof press** are drawn from the front, where the forearm or the press moves toward
  the camera and is mostly foreshortened.
- **Goblet and box squat** lean further forward at the bottom (about 40° and 50°) than many coaches cue for these
  variants; the lean comes from balancing the load over mid-foot with the shins the pattern allows.
- **Single-leg RDL** balance, **bench dip** shoulder extension at the bottom, **dips** depth, the **Zercher squat**'s
  lean, and the strongman lifts (log, axle) need a coach's eye.
- The **Above** view of the flyes draws a lying body with the standing front-view figure: the bench pad is mostly
  hidden under the torso, and the legs (knees bent, feet on the floor beside the bench) read less clearly than the arms.
