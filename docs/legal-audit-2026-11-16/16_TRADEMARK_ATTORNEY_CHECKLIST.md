## 6. Third-party names inside the app (descriptive references)

Program descriptions say "in the style of" named coaches and methods: Jim Wendler's 5/3/1, Starting Strength,
StrongLifts, PHUL, PHAT, GZCLP. One program **title** contains "80/20". Exercise aliases include "assault bike",
"stairmaster", "ski erg" and "Galloway run-walk". File 02, section 5, lists each with its file and line. Question:
is descriptive reference acceptable as worded, and should any be reworded or carry an attribution?

## 7. What to ask the attorney to do

1. A full search: federal (word and design), state registers, common-law use, business names, domain names, app
   stores and social handles, for every mark in section 1.
2. An opinion on likelihood of confusion with the section 2 users, considering the overlap in services, the shared
   faith-and-fitness positioning, and that an app is offered nationwide.
3. Whether the short form NARROW GATE should be used or protected at all, or only NARROW GATE FITNESS.
4. The classes to file in (questions, not advice: software and apps; fitness instruction and training; nutrition
   information; religious or educational content; clothing only if merchandise is planned).
5. Whether to file on an intent-to-use basis now, and in whose name. An individual can file; if a company will own
   the brand, discuss timing of formation and assignment.
6. A view on the tagline (section 3), on "Nutrition Score" and the Nutri-Score display (section 4), and on "Food IQ"
   (section 5), including whether renaming either feature is advisable. Renaming features is far cheaper before launch.
7. Wording for the descriptive references in section 6.
8. Whether the logo needs a design search and its own application.
9. A watch service after filing.

## 8. What to bring to the attorney

- This checklist and `02_TRADEMARK_CONFLICT_REGISTER.md`.
- Screenshots of each mark in use (the investor deck has them).
- The date the name was first chosen and first shown to anyone outside the project.
- Who designed the logo, and how (see file 11).
- The domains and social handles already held, with registrant names.
- The intended launch date and where the app will be offered.

That any of these marks is available, registrable or safe to use. The evidence so far is secondary and incomplete.
