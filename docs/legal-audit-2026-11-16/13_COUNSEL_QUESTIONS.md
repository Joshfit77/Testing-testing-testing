# 12. Questions for legal counsel

Date: 2026-11-16. Grouped by specialism. Each refers to the file holding the facts.

## Corporate and IP ownership (file 11, file 04)

1. What entity should own and operate the product, and what assignments are needed from the founder for work done
   before it existed?
2. Every visible commit is authored by an AI coding assistant, and one design reference image carries an AI image
   generator's provenance record. What is protectable, what representations can be made to investors about ownership
   of the code, text and artwork, and what record of human direction should be kept from now on?
3. The badge artwork and one supplied exercise clip have no recorded creator or licence. Can they be used, and what
   documentation would suffice?
4. Which accounts (Apple, Google, GitHub, domains, hosting, payment, AI provider) must be in the entity's name before
   launch and before investment?

## Trademark (file 02)

5. Please run a full clearance search. Given the "Narrow Gate" fitness users identified, is NARROW GATE FITNESS
   registrable and usable for software, fitness instruction and nutrition information, and in which classes?
6. Is an intent-to-use application appropriate now, and in whose name?
7. The tagline "Rooted in Faith. Built for Strength." against ROOTED IN STRENGTH: is there a concern?
8. "Narrow Gate Nutrition Score" shown with A–E grades on coloured tiles, against NUTRI-SCORE: is the name or the
   presentation a concern, and do the Nutri-Score usage rules apply to displaying its grades at all?
9. "Food IQ": is the name usable given the reported registration?
10. Program copy names living coaches and their methods (5/3/1, Starting Strength, PHUL, PHAT, GZCLP), and equipment
    aliases include brand names. Is descriptive reference acceptable, and how should it be worded?

## Patent (file 03)

11. Please obtain the independent claims for the patents listed and advise on the features in order: adaptive training
    review and coach proposals; Body Scan capture; sleep and nap classification; food matching; meal-photo estimates;
    GPS processing.
12. Is a freedom-to-operate opinion warranted for any of them before public launch?
13. Does reported enforcement against software-only apps in this field change the risk picture?

## Licensing and data (file 05)

14. Open Food Facts product data is saved into the app's tables alongside USDA, curated and member-entered rows. Is the
    resulting database a derivative database, a collective database or neither under the ODbL, and what follows for
    attribution, share-alike and keeping the data open?
15. Is the current attribution to Open Food Facts sufficient, and where must it appear?
16. Does the generated third-party notices file, linked from the app, satisfy the notice requirements of the MIT, BSD,
    Apache-2.0, MPL-2.0 and OFL licences for the web app and for the native apps?
17. Server-side image processing uses LGPL libraries (libvips) that are not distributed to users. Is anything required?
18. Are the Scripture text's terms and the provenance of the devotional and commentary text adequately documented?

## Privacy and consumer protection (files 06, 07, 08)

19. Which privacy laws apply at launch, given no geographic restriction: state comprehensive laws, Washington's My
    Health My Data Act and Nevada's equivalent, CCPA/CPRA sensitive-information rules, the FTC Health Breach
    Notification Rule, COPPA, and non-US law if the app is available abroad?
20. Is religious-practice data (prayer journal, Bible activity) to be treated as sensitive, and what consent is needed?
21. Health-platform data (steps, sleep) reaches the AI provider after a general AI consent. Is that permitted under
    Apple's and Google's rules and the health-data laws, or is separate consent, or removal, required?
22. Consent is stored as a single timestamp that is erased on withdrawal, and acceptance records are deleted with the
    account. What consent and acceptance records must be retained, and for how long?
23. What retention periods should apply to each data type, including reports about members and AI conversations?
24. Is the age gate (a checkbox and a self-declared age) sufficient, and what must happen if a minor is identified?
25. Members currently accept placeholder Terms and Privacy pages. Is that acceptable for a closed test?
26. Friends: what must the Terms and Privacy Policy say about sharing between members, usernames, blocking,
    reporting and moderation? Should sharing default to off? What moderation commitments are required?
27. Which data-processing agreements are needed (hosting, AI provider, payments, subscriptions, email)?

## Subscriptions and stores (file 09)

28. Do the paywall and renewal disclosures meet federal and state auto-renewal requirements, including cancellation?
29. "Free forever" and "always free": should these promises be made?
30. Is the store listing plan (religious content, health features, AI, user-generated content when Friends is on)
    consistent with the age rating and the stores' policies?

## Health, nutrition and AI claims (file 10)

31. Which features, if any, risk being treated as a medical device or as regulated nutrition advice (Body Scan,
    sleep and recovery scores, calorie targets, the AI coach's suggestions)?
32. What disclaimers are needed, and where? Are the existing ones adequate?
33. What professional review is needed before the AI coach's safety rules, the programs, the supplement notes and the
    exercise demonstrations are offered to the public, and what may be said about that review?
34. Do the supplement evidence labels need per-item sources and the standard disclaimer?

## Investor materials

35. The investor presentation states that the product is pre-launch with no customers, and labels all figures as
    hypothetical. Is any further disclaimer or securities-law language needed before it is shown to investors?
